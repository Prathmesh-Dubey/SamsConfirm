Who we are

A company with one product

SAMS Mobile is registered in the United Kingdom and is building the EX-04Z1, an intrinsically safe smartphone designed for Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust areas. That is the whole of the business. There is no adjacent accessory range, no consumer line and no second model in the catalogue to distract from it.

We have chosen to say only what we can evidence. This site does not carry customer logos, case studies, revenue figures, headcount, factory photographs or a founding legend, because the device is pre-launch and inventing that material would be the easiest thing in the world and the least useful. What we can tell you is what the device is designed to do, which certifications are being pursued and by which route, when we expect to launch, and how to reach a person who will answer a technical question.

The judgement behind the company is a simple one. Hazardous area handsets exist, but the good ones are expensive and ageing, and several of the devices a buyer will be offered run Android releases that a modern corporate security policy will not accept for the years such a device stays in service. Meanwhile the work has moved onto software, and the people doing it are inside classified areas where an ordinary phone is not allowed. That gap is worth one product done well.

So the discipline is narrow scope and honest language. One device. One protection concept. Two zone types, gas and dust, so a plant does not need two part numbers. And a certification programme published as a status rather than a claim.

Engineering position

Why a Snapdragon platform inside an intrinsically safe device is hard

Intrinsic safety is a design philosophy before it is a certificate. Instead of containing an ignition inside a heavy flameproof enclosure, the ia concept limits the electrical and thermal energy available anywhere in the circuit so that no fault, and no combination of two faults, can release enough energy to ignite the surrounding atmosphere. Voltage, current, stored capacitance and inductance are all bounded, and the maximum surface temperature is bounded too.

That constraint fights everything a modern smartphone platform wants to do. A high performance application processor draws sharp current transients under load. A large battery is, by definition, a concentration of energy, and the analysis has to hold even when a cell is short circuited or a connector fails. Radio power amplifiers, camera flash circuits, fast charging paths, connectors, even the electrostatic behaviour of a plastic housing, all have to be brought inside the envelope. This is why the hazardous area handset market is small, why its products stay on sale for many years, and why so many of them run modest chipsets: the easy way to satisfy intrinsic safety is to give up performance.

The EX-04Z1 is built on the opposite decision. A Qualcomm Snapdragon octa-core platform running Android 16, with a 4,000 mAh battery, a 50 MP camera with phase detection autofocus, 4G LTE, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC and four-constellation GNSS, inside a design whose ATEX marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4. IIC is the most demanding gas group, covering hydrogen and acetylene, and T4 caps maximum surface temperature at 135 degrees Celsius. Meeting those two together with that platform is the engineering problem the product exists to solve.

Gas group IIC and Zone 21 dust in the same design is the second deliberate choice. It costs more engineering effort than gas-only, and it is why an integrated site handling both vapour and powder can specify one handset. The value of that decision only shows up when a plant tries to standardise, which is precisely when a gas-only device becomes an expensive mistake.

A last point on why performance is a safety argument and not a luxury. A slow, dim, awkward explosion proof phone gets left in the locker and replaced by an unauthorised consumer device carried in a pocket. Every safety manager has seen it happen. Making the compliant device the one people actually prefer to carry is the only version of this product that improves anything.

How we work

Four commitments

These are the rules we have set ourselves for how this product is described and sold before it launches. They are enforceable against us: if you find us breaking one, tell us.

01

Nothing is certified until it is certified

We will not describe the EX-04Z1 as certified, approved, listed or compliant before a numbered certificate exists. Until then the language is designed for, target marking and certification in progress, and the certification schedule is available in writing on request.

02

No published prices, stock or lead times

While certification is running, any lead time would be a guess and any stock claim would be false. Pre-launch pricing is discussed directly, under confidentiality, with buyers and prospective distributors.

03

Specifications we can stand behind

The published specification is deliberately short. Figures that are not yet frozen for certification, including dimensions, mass, display, memory, certified ambient range and accessories, are described as to be confirmed at launch rather than estimated.

04

A straight answer about fit

If your area is Zone 0, or your process needs T5 or T6, or your market has a national scheme we have not yet reached, we will tell you the device does not suit you. That is a better outcome for both of us than a return from a site.

Contact

How to reach us

The fastest route is info@sams-mobile.com or the enquiry form on this site. Include the country, the site, the area classification you are equipping and roughly how many people carry a device, and the reply will be specific rather than a brochure.

Buyers and end users get the datasheet, the written certification schedule with current status, and a place in the evaluation programme where units are available. Distributors and resellers should say which territory they cover and which operators they hold vendor registrations with; there is a separate page setting out how appointments work. Journalists, analysts and specifiers are welcome to ask about the certification programme directly, and will get the same status we give a customer.

One thing we cannot do is confirm a claim made by somebody else on our behalf. If you have been shown a listing, a quotation or a specification sheet for the EX-04Z1 that states a certificate, a price, a stock position or a delivery date, send it to us before you act on it.

Questions

About SAMS Mobile

Where is SAMS Mobile based?

SAMS Mobile is registered in the United Kingdom. Enquiries from any market go to info@sams-mobile.com and are answered from there.

Is SAMS Mobile a manufacturer or a reseller?

SAMS Mobile is the company behind the EX-04Z1 and is responsible for the product and its certification programme. Supply arrangements in individual countries usually run through appointed distributors, and evaluation units can be supplied directly.

How many products do you make?

One. The EX-04Z1. There is no other model, and we would rather build one intrinsically safe smartphone properly than a range of them approximately.

When does the EX-04Z1 launch?

Q4 2026. IECEx, ATEX and NEC 500 Class I Division 1 certification are in progress and no certificate has been issued yet.

Do you have customers we can speak to?

Not yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. The device is pre-launch and there are no deployments to reference. When there are, they will be named with the customer’s agreement and not before.

Can I visit or audit the manufacturing arrangement?

Serious buyers and prospective distributors can ask about the manufacturing and quality arrangement as part of the technical discussion. An ATEX certificate for Category 2 equipment includes a production quality assurance notification, so this is a fair question and it will be answered directly rather than on a public page.

How do I start a conversation?

Write to info@sams-mobile.com or use the enquiry form, with your country, site and area classification. You will get the datasheet, the certification schedule and an honest answer about timing.
