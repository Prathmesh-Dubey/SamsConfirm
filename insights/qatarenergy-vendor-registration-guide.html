QatarEnergy vendor registration: Mushtaryat, Tawteen and the hazardous-area dossier

September 9, 202610 min readVendor Guides

A working guide to QatarEnergy vendor registration for equipment suppliers: the Mushtaryat portal and the SAP vendor code, how the Tawteen ICV score is built and used, Ras Laffan and Mesaieed site access, and what a Zone 1 device dossier has to contain.

Qatar buys industrial equipment in an unusual shape. A small number of very large assets, most of them inside two planned industrial cities, are procured by one dominant state company and its operating subsidiaries. Ras Laffan and Mesaieed between them hold liquefaction trains, gas to liquids plants, refining, olefins, fertiliser, steel and aluminium. Dukhan runs onshore, and the offshore estate is served from Halul Island. For a supplier that means the addressable market is concentrated, the buyer is identifiable, and the barrier to entry is administrative rather than commercial.

That barrier has two halves. QatarEnergy vendor registration gets you a vendor code and the right to be invited. The Tawteen in-country value programme decides how well you compete once you are. Neither is difficult in isolation. What catches suppliers out is treating them as sequential when they run on different clocks, and treating a hazardous-area device as an ordinary catalogue item when it carries a certification file that a plant engineer will read line by line long after procurement has closed the tender.

What QatarEnergy registration actually gets you

QatarEnergy asks prospective suppliers and contractors to register in order to obtain a QatarEnergy SAP vendor code. That code is not a formality. It is the identifier attached to participation in tenders, to receiving requests for quotation and invitations to tender, to the award of purchase orders, to entering contracts and agreements, and to being paid against an invoice. Without it there is no commercial mechanism through which a supplier can transact, whatever the technical merits of the product.

The registration process is built around a supplier questionnaire, generally described as the know your supplier process, which is a mandatory precondition for QatarEnergy issuing a request for quotation or a tender document to you. Alongside it, suppliers are required to accept QatarEnergy’s supplier principles of conduct as a condition of current and future contractual relations. Read that document rather than clicking through it; it carries obligations on ethics, labour and subcontracting that a small manufacturer may need to reflect in its own policies before it can honestly sign.

Working through the Mushtaryat portal

Registration, and updates to an existing registration, are handled electronically through QatarEnergy’s supply chain portal, Mushtaryat, at mushtaryat.qatarenergy.qa. The portal also carries tender publication and the mechanism for buying and downloading tender documents, so it is worth spending an hour inside it before you begin registering, simply to see how QatarEnergy words its own categories and what it is currently buying.

Three practical points. First, the category selection you make at registration governs which requests for quotation reach you, so a manufacturer of hazardous-area electronics should map itself carefully rather than accepting the nearest broad heading. Second, registration data is expected to be maintained rather than submitted once, and a profile carrying an expired trade licence, lapsed insurance or superseded financial statements is a profile that quietly stops being invited. Third, the portal is the system of record. Relationship building with an operating company is valuable, but a technical champion inside a plant cannot buy from an unregistered supplier, and asking them to try is a poor use of their goodwill.

Tawteen and how in-country value is scored

Tawteen is QatarEnergy’s supply chain localisation programme for the Qatari energy sector, introduced in 2020 and now the reference point for local content across the industry. Its in-country value mechanism does two distinct things, and suppliers regularly confuse them.

The first is the ICV scorecard: a retrospective measure of what your company already contributes to the Qatari economy, expressed as a single percentage. The methodology aggregates the eligible in-country elements of your cost base and divides them by revenue generated in country. The components are broadly what you would expect from a local content formula: spend directed at Qatari suppliers for goods, materials and services; the cost of Qatari nationals and residents on your payroll, including training and development; and investment in fixed assets, facilities and equipment located in Qatar, taken through depreciation. A supplier with no Qatari cost base scores low, because the formula is measuring exactly that.

The second is the ICV plan: a forward commitment about what you will localise if you win, which is assessed differently and which is where a foreign manufacturer with a credible local partner, a local spares holding or a local service capability can make a real difference to a bid.

Both are validated by third-party certifiers approved by Tawteen, working to a defined methodology, and the certification is refreshed annually. Registration and the certification workflow run through the in-country value digital portal. Suppliers engage a certifier through it, obtain the scorecard or plan certification, and appear in the certified supplier database that buyers can search.

What the ICV score does inside a tender is give a bidder a competitive advantage during evaluation in proportion to local contribution. QatarEnergy does not publish a single universal weighting that applies to every tender, and any specific percentage you see quoted should be checked against the actual tender documents rather than assumed. The structural point does not depend on the number: two technically compliant bids at a similar price are separated by ICV, and a supplier with no scorecard at all is starting a race with a handicap that no amount of product superiority reliably overcomes.

QatarEnergy LNG and the rest of the operating landscape

A supplier who has registered with QatarEnergy has not automatically registered with everything in Qatar, and the naming has changed enough recently to cause confusion.

Qatargas rebranded to QatarEnergy LNG in 2023. It operates the liquefaction trains at Ras Laffan and is the operator of the North Field expansion projects that will take Qatari LNG capacity substantially above its current level, with a partner list including Shell, TotalEnergies, ExxonMobil, ConocoPhillips, Eni, Sinopec and CNPC. If your reference documents still say Qatargas, update them.

At Mesaieed the industrial estate carries refining, olefins and polymer production, fertiliser, methanol, steel and aluminium production, operated by a mix of QatarEnergy affiliates and joint ventures. Several of these run their own procurement alongside the group route. When you plan market entry, decide which specific plants you are trying to reach and check how each of them actually buys, rather than assuming the group registration reaches every gate.

Ras Laffan and Mesaieed: getting people, not just product, on site

Equipment suppliers underestimate this step, and it is the one that turns a won order into a delayed delivery. Ras Laffan Industrial City and Mesaieed Industrial City are controlled zones. Any worker deployed to a Ras Laffan site is expected to hold a valid RLIC gate pass, current QatarEnergy QHSE basic safety training, H2S awareness and breathing apparatus certification, plus whatever trade qualifications the scope requires.

For a device manufacturer this matters in three concrete ways. Commissioning, training and fleet rollout all require your people, or your partner’s people, inside the fence. Any classified-area evaluation of a handset happens on the far side of a gate that will not open on a purchase order alone. And the lead time on gate passes and safety training is real, sequential and outside your control, so a project plan that assumes an engineer can attend site next week is a project plan that will slip. Build the access requirement into the schedule at bid stage, and if you intend to work through a Qatari partner, make their existing gate pass and safety training position part of how you select them.

What hazardous-area equipment suppliers need on top

Vendor registration and ICV are commercial gates. There are three technical ones, and they run in parallel rather than in sequence.

  • Ex certification. The document a Qatari hazardous-area engineer reads is an IECEx Certificate of Conformity, generally alongside an ATEX certificate where the buyer works to the European scheme. What is assessed is the complete equipment marking string: the equipment protection level, the gas or dust group, the temperature class and the certified ambient range. For a handheld device the practical target is Zone 1 and Zone 21, gas group IIC. A buyer searching for an explosion proof mobile phone or a flameproof mobile phone is, in certification terms, almost always looking for an intrinsically safe device with an Ex ia protection level, and being precise about that in your submission saves an evaluation cycle.
  • Product conformity for import. Conformity for regulated goods entering Qatar is administered through the Ministry of Commerce and Industry and the national standards framework, which expects regulated imports to arrive with a conformity certificate issued by an accepted body. The scope and the accepted-body list change, so confirm the current requirement for your product category before you ship anything, including evaluation samples.
  • Radio type approval. A phone is radio and telecommunications terminal equipment, so it needs type approval from the Communications Regulatory Authority. Applications run through the CRA e-Spectrum services portal at e-spectrum.cra.gov.qa. CRA operates a streamlined route for equipment already type approved or tested in a recognised country and a standard route for everything else, which is worth knowing because it materially changes the effort involved for a device already approved in Europe.

Two Qatar-specific technical points belong in the dossier rather than in a later clarification. Ambient temperature is the first: summer conditions sit well above the reference ambient many certificates assume, so the certified ambient range and temperature class will be checked. Dust is the second: ammonia, urea and polymer production at Mesaieed put Zone 21 alongside Zone 1, and bagging lines and conveyor galleries are exactly where a gas-only specification runs out.

Ownership, agents and which entity holds the contract

Qatar liberalised foreign ownership under Law No. 1 of 2019, which permits up to 100 per cent foreign ownership across most sectors, with certain activities excluded. Commercial agencies remain among the categories subject to their own regime, so a registered commercial agency is not the same proposition as a foreign-owned company established under the investment law.

For an equipment manufacturer the useful question is not which structure is theoretically available but which one your buyer can actually transact with, and who will hold the spares. A Qatari entity with an ICV scorecard, a Ras Laffan gate pass position and a warehouse is worth more inside a bid than a marginally better legal structure with none of those things. Decide the structure before you register, because changing the contracting entity after registration means doing the registration again.

What stalls Qatari registrations

  1. Incomplete know your supplier submissions, particularly ownership, beneficial ownership and sanctions declarations left half-finished because the form is long.
  2. Category selection that does not match the actual scope of supply, so the right buyer never sees the profile.
  3. No ICV scorecard, or a scorecard allowed to lapse between annual validations, which turns into a scoring penalty at the worst possible moment.
  4. Documents that have expired since submission: trade licences, insurance, financial statements.
  5. A technical dossier sent as a brochure rather than as the certificate, the marking string and the declarations of conformity, which produces a document request and a lost fortnight.

On fees and timelines, treat every figure you find online with suspicion, this article included. The variables are your document readiness, your category, the certifier queue for ICV and whether your legal entity already exists in Qatar. Confirm current requirements and processing times with the Mushtaryat portal and the in-country value digital portal directly.

Specifying the device itself

Registration is procedure; specification is where a device either fits Qatar or does not. The demand pattern is consistent across the two industrial cities: field operators walking long distances inside a single perimeter at Ras Laffan; mechanical and instrument technicians on turnaround scopes at Mesaieed; marine and jetty teams working loading arms and berths; and offshore crews rotating through Halul Island who inherit whatever the previous rotation managed to write down.

Buyers arrive at that requirement through plain search terms rather than certification codes. Enquiries come in as ATEX phone, ATEX mobile phone, ex phone, zone 1 mobile phone, explosion proof mobile phone and, most commonly of all, as some form of explosion proof mobile phone price in Qatar. The vocabulary is loose; the requirement underneath it is not. It is a handset that can legitimately be carried into a Zone 1 area with a certificate behind it, enrolled in the same enterprise mobility console as the rest of the estate, and supported by somebody who can get a replacement through the gate.
