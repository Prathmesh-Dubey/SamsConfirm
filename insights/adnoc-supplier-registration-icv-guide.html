ADNOC supplier registration and the ICV certificate: a guide for equipment suppliers

September 9, 202610 min readVendor Guides

How ADNOC supplier registration and prequalification actually work on the Supplier Hub, what the ICV certificate is and how its audit cycle runs, how ICV weighting changes a bid, and what MoIAT ECAS and TDRA require of hazardous-area equipment.

The United Arab Emirates is the Gulf market where a technically excellent product loses to a commercially better organised one more often than anywhere else. That is not a criticism of how the country buys. It is a direct consequence of the in-country value framework, which converts local economic contribution into a number that sits inside bid evaluation. Two compliant offers at a similar price get separated by it. A supplier who has never obtained the certificate is not really competing.

This guide covers ADNOC supplier registration from an equipment manufacturer’s point of view: how the Supplier Hub actually works, what prequalification asks for, what the ICV certificate is and what its audit cycle does to your planning, and the separate conformity route that the equipment itself has to clear before it can be imported and used. It is written for hazardous-area suppliers, whose dossiers are read by a plant engineer long after procurement has finished with them.

Registration and prequalification on the ADNOC Supplier Hub

ADNOC runs supplier onboarding through the ADNOC Supplier Hub at supplierhub.adnoc.ae, built on SAP Ariba. The important structural point is that this is two stages, not one, and suppliers who complete the first and stop are surprised when nothing arrives.

Registration creates the supplier record. You open an Ariba account, complete a profile questionnaire covering company information, and complete integrity due diligence, which ADNOC reviews against its compliance and ethical standards. The document set is the familiar UAE pack: a valid trade licence with activities that actually match what you intend to supply, VAT registration, shareholder and ultimate beneficial ownership information, organisation chart, employee list, quality and management system certificates and insurance.

The trade licence point deserves emphasis because it causes more rejections than anything else. The activities listed on the licence have to align with the products and services you are offering. A licence that says general trading when you are proposing certified hazardous-area electronics, or a licence whose activity list was written for a different business five years ago, will be queried. Fix the licence before you register rather than after.

Prequalification is the second stage and the one that actually determines whether you are invited. You submit a supplier prequalification questionnaire tailored to the product or service category you are claiming, and you select the product and service groups relevant to what you make. Expect it to be technical: manufacturing capability, quality control, test and inspection evidence, original equipment manufacturer credentials, product test reports and references.

Category selection is the most consequential decision in the whole process and the one suppliers take least seriously. Buyers search the qualified supplier base by group. Hazardous-area electronics can plausibly sit under communications equipment, under electrical equipment for classified areas, under safety equipment and under IT hardware, and the person raising the requisition may search any of them. Claim every group that genuinely applies, including accessories, chargers and any device management or support service you provide. A manufacturer filed under one broad group is invisible in the other three searches, and that is a database problem no follow-up call solves.

Once approved you become eligible to receive invitations to tender, to respond to requests for quotation and to receive electronic purchase orders. As with every large operator, approval is a maintained state rather than a permanent one: expired documents on the profile take you off invitation lists quietly.

The ICV certificate and its audit cycle

In-country value began as an ADNOC programme and has since been adopted nationally. The National ICV Programme is run by the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology, and the certificate issued under it is used by a growing list of federal and Abu Dhabi government entities and major national companies alongside ADNOC. A company that previously held a unified ICV certificate can use it for the national programme.

Mechanically, the certificate is an audit output. You appoint one of the certifying bodies approved for the purpose, and they assess your company against the ICV methodology using your audited financial statements as the primary evidence, alongside supporting data such as your procurement of goods and services and your payroll records. The score reflects the things the methodology values: local spend with UAE suppliers, investment in assets located in the UAE, Emiratisation and the workforce, and related contributions. The output is a single score attached to a certificate.

The audit cycle is where planning goes wrong. The certificate is valid for a defined period measured from the date of issuance of the audited financial statements it was based on, commonly stated as fourteen months. That means two things in practice. First, the clock starts at your financial statements, not at the date the certifier signs, so a company that audits its accounts late has already burned part of its certificate’s life before it applies. Second, the renewal is annual in rhythm rather than in name, and a lapse is not a paperwork inconvenience. It arrives as a scoring penalty at the exact moment a tender is being evaluated. Confirm the current validity rule with your certifying body, because the methodology and the certifying body list are both revised periodically.

How ICV weighting changes a bid

ADNOC applies the ICV score inside bid evaluation, and the effect is easiest to understand in three cases.

A bidder with a strong ICV score and a competitive price wins comfortably. A bidder with a weaker score can still win, but has to be enough better on price or technical merit to overcome the differential. And a bidder with no valid certificate at all is treated as scoring nothing on that component, which for a tender of any size is usually decisive.

ADNOC does not publish one universal weighting that applies to every tender, and figures circulating online vary enough that none of them should be relied on. The weighting that matters is the one stated in the tender documents in front of you. What is stable is the direction of travel: ICV has been extended, not narrowed, and ADNOC has continued to add mechanisms that reward deeper local sourcing.

For a foreign equipment manufacturer, the useful response is not to complain about the differential but to identify which of your costs can legitimately move onshore. Device fleets are better suited to this than most capital equipment, because the value is not all in the shipment. Spares holding, configuration and enrolment, staging, training, warranty handling and repair logistics all happen after the first delivery and all can be performed by a UAE entity. Structuring that properly, with a partner who already holds a certificate and a UAE trade licence, is usually faster and cheaper than trying to build your own score from nothing.

ADNOC Onshore, Offshore, Gas and Refining: what each one is actually like

ADNOC is a group, and the operating companies buy for very different environments. A specification that ignores this reads as generic to the person evaluating it.

  • ADNOC Onshore runs the mainland fields, Bab, Bu Hasa, Asab and Sahil among them. The realities are distance, heat and dust, and crews covering long routes between wellheads, manifolds and processing facilities.
  • ADNOC Offshore runs the offshore fields including Upper Zakum, Umm Shaif, Umm Lulu and SARB, worked from platforms and artificial islands. The defining constraint is that a replacement is a boat or a helicopter, not a walk to stores, so ruggedness, sealing and the size of the spares pool decide the specification more than headline features do.
  • ADNOC Gas covers onshore processing at Habshan and the liquefaction operation on Das Island. Continuous process operation and marine interface at the same site.
  • ADNOC Refining and the wider Ruwais complex, including Borouge polyolefins and the chemicals zone being developed there, is the densest concentration of classified-area work in the country and the place where a single standardised device across a contractor population has the most value.

Outside the ADNOC group, ENOC operates at Jebel Ali, SNOC at Sajaa, and the storage and bunkering cluster at Fujairah sits outside the Strait of Hormuz. Each of these buys separately. An ADNOC registration does not reach them.

MoIAT ECAS: conformity for the equipment itself

Supplier registration governs who may sell. Conformity governs what may be imported and placed on the market, and it is administered by the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology through the Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme, ECAS. ECAS is the mandatory scheme for regulated products and results in an ECAS certificate of conformity, generally issued for a defined period, commonly stated as one year. Alongside it MoIAT operates the Emirates Quality Mark, EQM, which is voluntary, involves assessment of the manufacturer’s quality management system as well as the product, and is licensed for a longer period.

For a radio product there is a further, separate approval: the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority, TDRA, registers and approves radio equipment placed on the UAE market. ECAS does not cover it and neither does ADNOC registration.

Confirm the current technical regulation applying to your category, the scheme scope and the certificate validity with MoIAT or an approved conformity body before you plan a shipment, including evaluation samples. Scopes are revised, and a conformity certificate that does not match the product as it is actually shipped, accessories and chargers included, is discovered at the border rather than at the desk.

What the Ex dossier needs

UAE operators assess hazardous-area equipment from certificates, not brochures. Assemble one indexed pack containing the IECEx Certificate of Conformity, the ATEX certificate, the complete equipment marking string and the relevant declarations of conformity, and send it as a single document. Suppliers who do this clear technical review in one pass. Suppliers who send a datasheet receive a document request and lose a fortnight.

Three UAE-specific points shape the technical answer. Ambient temperature and humidity together are harsher than dry-heat testing suggests, so the certified ambient range on the certificate matters and so does evidence of humidity and salt fog exposure. Offshore duty is unforgiving on sealing and on battery endurance, because there is no same-day replacement on Das, Zirku or a Zakum island. And dust is not only a Saudi problem: polymer and bulk handling at Ruwais puts Zone 21 alongside Zone 1, so a gas-only device covers part of an integrated complex rather than all of it.

One terminology note worth carrying into your submission. Buyers search for an explosion proof mobile phone or a flameproof mobile phone, but for a handheld device the protection concept that actually applies is intrinsic safety, an Ex ia equipment protection level. Using the search term in marketing is reasonable. Using it in a technical submission where the certificate says something different is not, and evaluators notice.

What stalls ADNOC registrations

  1. A trade licence whose listed activities do not match the goods being offered.
  2. Integrity due diligence submitted with ownership or beneficial ownership sections incomplete.
  3. Registration completed but prequalification never submitted, so the supplier record exists and nothing is ever invited.
  4. Too few product and service groups claimed, leaving the supplier invisible to most buyer searches.
  5. No ICV certificate, or one that lapsed because the audited financial statements it was based on aged out.
  6. Technical evidence supplied as marketing material rather than as certificates, marking strings and declarations of conformity.

Timelines depend almost entirely on document readiness and on whether a UAE legal entity already exists, and published figures vary widely. Treat anything you read, including here, as indicative and confirm current requirements, forms and processing times with the ADNOC Supplier Hub and your ICV certifying body directly.

Specifying the device itself

The UAE demand pattern is distinctive because so much classified-area work is performed by contractor crews rather than operator staff, which changes how devices are issued, tracked and recovered. Offshore supervisors care about survival between rotations. Inspection engineers at Ruwais and Jebel Ali care about photographic evidence good enough to attach to a work order. IT teams care about enrolment into the enterprise mobility platform they already run and the ability to wipe a lost unit. Procurement cares about which registered UAE entity holds the supply agreement, and asks that question earlier than most buyers elsewhere.

Those buyers find suppliers through plain search terms rather than certification codes: ATEX phone, ATEX mobile phone, explosion proof mobile phone, ex phone, zone 1 mobile phone, and most often some form of explosion proof mobile phone price in UAE. The vocabulary is loose; the requirement underneath it is exact. Our United Arab Emirates market page sets out that operating context in detail, and the Saudi Arabia page covers the equivalent buying pattern next door, where the localisation programme is iktva rather than ICV.
