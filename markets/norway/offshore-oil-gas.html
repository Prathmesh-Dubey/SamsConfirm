Explosion proof mobile phone for Offshore Oil and Gas in Norway

Offshore is where Norway's explosion proof mobile phone question is hardest: wet, cold, remote and unforgiving of a device that fails. This page covers fixed installations, floating production vessels and normally unmanned platforms across the Norwegian continental shelf, operated by companies such as Equinor, Aker BP, Vår Energi and ConocoPhillips. The EX-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21; its ATEX and IECEx certification is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

The shelf · Offshore oil and gas

Installations on the Norwegian continental shelf

The shelf has several installation types, and each changes the handset question. Fixed steel and concrete platforms such as the Troll A gas platform and the Ekofisk centre carry process modules, wellheads and accommodation. Floating production vessels such as those at Skarv, Alvheim and Goliat add cargo tanks and a turret. Normally unmanned platforms are visited for a day by a small crew. Equinor and Aker BP operate most of the fields; Vår Energi operates Balder and Goliat; ConocoPhillips operates the Greater Ekofisk area; and Shell operates Ormen Lange through subsea facilities tied to Nyhamna. See the Equinor and Aker BP pages for field detail.

Offshore weather decides a handset's life and offshore rotations decide who holds it.

Spray, ice and wind

A deck in the northern North Sea or the Barents Sea sees salt spray and sub-zero temperatures for months. IP68 sealing and rugged build are aimed at that, and the certified temperature range will be on the datasheet.

Rotation and handover

A device passes between crews every few weeks. The console wipes and re-enrols it from onshore, so each incoming crew finds a clean, correctly configured phone.

Norwegian on the device

The framework regulations expect Norwegian to be used as far as possible offshore. Android 16 carries a Norwegian interface, and documents are produced in Norwegian and English.

Work patterns · Offshore

How work is organised and what procurement will ask

The common rotation is two weeks on, four off, with helicopter flights from bases such as Sola and Flesland on the North Sea and Kristiansund for the Norwegian Sea. Havtil supervises safety and working environment on the shelf, and the regulations are functional: they state goals, point to standards such as NORSOK and IEC 60079, and the operator documents its choice. Equipment must be selected, installed and inspected under that regime.

On the supplier side, operators share a qualification register, Magnet JQS, formerly EPIM JQS, and add their own technical requirements and equipment registers. SAMS has not been assessed by any operator. Ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams what a new Ex device must provide.

Questions · Offshore oil and gas

Frequently asked questions

Is Havtil involved in approving the handset?

Havtil supervises the operator's compliance; it does not approve individual products. The operator selects equipment and documents that it meets the regulations and standards. A certificate and a documented selection are what the operator needs.

Can the device be used offshore today?

No. Certification is in progress and no certificate has been issued. Offshore trials are part of the evaluation plan after certification, and each needs the operator's permission.

Does the sector also need dust protection?

Mostly gas. Dust arises where bulk powders are handled, and the design target covers Zone 21 as well as Zone 1, but certification for it is still in progress.
