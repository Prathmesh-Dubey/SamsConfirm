Explosion proof mobile phone for power generation in Chile

An explosion proof mobile phone for power generation in Chile has to suit a grid that was built to serve northern mining, combining thermal generation with a fast-growing share of solar and wind capacity. Classified areas are concentrated at fuel-handling, gas-fired and storage equipment, not the solar and wind assets themselves.

Why it fits power generation

Built for a mining-driven, increasingly renewable grid

Power generation serving Chile's northern mining belt combines conventional thermal plant hazards with a grid that is shifting rapidly toward solar and wind, a mix that narrows the classified-area footprint compared with an oil and gas-heavy power sector elsewhere.

Zone 1/2 gas at thermal generation fuel systems

Gas and diesel-fired generation plants serving the mining grid have fuel-handling and gas-supply areas classified Zone 1 or Zone 2, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups relevant to that fuel supply.

A grid built to serve remote, high-altitude mining loads

Much of the generation capacity in this sector exists specifically to power copper and lithium operations at altitude in the Atacama, meaning maintenance and inspection crews for power infrastructure often work in the same remote, high-desert conditions as the mines themselves.

Renewable capacity reduces but doesn't eliminate classified-area work

As solar and wind capacity grows on this grid, the remaining thermal generation and its fuel-handling equipment stay classified areas, even as the overall proportion of a mixed generation portfolio's footprint requiring Ex-rated equipment shrinks.

Desert environmental exposure shared with the mining sector it serves

Power infrastructure in this region faces the same high-altitude, low-humidity, high-UV exposure as the mining operations it feeds, environmental factors reflected in the device's testing regime.

Fleet management across a dispersed generation and transmission network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a generation or transmission operator managing devices across multiple plant sites feeding the northern mining grid.

Thermal and renewable generation built primarily to serve Chile's northern mining belt, including Escondida, Collahuasi and Atacama lithium operations.

Fuel-handling and gas-supply systems at thermal generation plants.
Trend

Sites and hazards

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is carried in Chilean power plants

The largest cluster of thermal plant sits on the Mejillones and Tocopilla coast, with more at Quintero in the centre. Operators include Engie Energía Chile, AES Andes, Colbún and Enel Generación Chile. Solar plants dominate the Atacama interior, and the Cerro Dominador solar thermal plant there uses molten salt storage.

Classified situations

Gas supply to turbines
Pressure-reduction and metering stations, normally Zone 2 outdoors.
Coal handling
Stockyards, conveyors and transfer towers where coal dust forms Zone 21 or 22 areas.
Hydrogen-cooled generators
Hydrogen in the generator seal-oil and purge areas, group IIC.
Fuel oil and diesel tanks
Storage for startup and backup fuel.
Transformer yards
Mineral oil, generally a fire risk more than a classified area.
Battery rooms
Ventilation and gas detection requirements set by the battery supplier.
How the work is organised

Mining-scale loads, remote plants and outage seasons

Much of the capacity exists to supply mines under long contracts, so outages are planned around the customer's needs. The coastal plants have operators on a continuous shift pattern.

Thermal01

Boiler and turbine rounds

A plant operator walks turbine halls, coal conveyors and gas stations in one shift. The classified area changes along the route, and a Zone 1 and Zone 21 design avoids a second device for coal handling.

Outages02

Planned maintenance windows

Outages bring turbine and boiler specialists from abroad as well as local contractors. Handsets enrolled in the plant's console carry permits and drawings without personal phones entering classified areas.

Renewables03

Substations and storage sites

Solar fields themselves are mostly not classified, but substations, transformers and storage rooms in the same desert are inspected by crews who also visit thermal plants. One handset can serve both kinds of site. See the explosion proof phone for Chilean mining for the customers many of these plants supply.

Questions · Power

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for power generation use in Chile yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Do solar and wind sites need an explosion proof device?

Generally no, renewable generation assets themselves are not classified hazardous areas; the relevant classified areas are at thermal plants' fuel-handling and gas-supply systems.

Does the device suit the high-altitude desert conditions of this grid?

Environmental testing addresses the altitude, aridity and UV exposure shared with the mining operations this grid was built to serve.

Is this a distinct customer base from the mining sector itself?

Often closely linked, since much of this generation capacity exists specifically to supply major mining operators, sometimes under direct power-purchase arrangements with the mines themselves.

Are coal plants in Chile still a factor?

Many coal units are scheduled for retirement or conversion under national decarbonisation plans, but those that remain keep their coal-dust classified areas. Check the status of each plant with its operator.

Do hydrogen-cooled generators need a special handset?

The area around the hydrogen system is a gas group IIC area, which the EX-04Z1 marking target covers. Whether a plant uses hydrogen cooling, and where the zone ends, is set in its own classification study.

Who inside a generator decides on a new handset?

Usually the operations manager, instrumentation engineers and HSE together. Ask for their equipment approval and contractor access procedure.
