Chile · Mining

Explosion proof mobile phone for mining in Chile

An explosion proof mobile phone for mining in Chile has to cope with two very different operations at extreme altitude in the Atacama Desert: hard-rock copper extraction at sites such as Chuquicamata, Escondida and Collahuasi, and brine-based lithium evaporation on the salt flats. Dust is the shared hazard, but altitude, aridity and process differences shape how a device gets specified.

Why it fits mining

Built for high-altitude desert mining, both hard-rock and brine

Chile's copper belt and lithium salt flats sit at some of the highest working altitudes in the global mining industry, in one of the driest environments on Earth.

Zone 21/22 dust rating for ore processing

Copper concentrator and crushing circuits generate combustible dust, and the EX-04Z1's Zone 21 / Zone 22 target addresses these processing stages across the major Chilean copper operations.

High-altitude operating conditions

Escondida and Collahuasi sit above 3,000 metres, and Chuquicamata above 2,800, altitudes that affect battery performance and component behaviour differently from a sea-level or low-altitude industrial site, a factor built into the device's environmental testing.

Extreme aridity and UV exposure

The Atacama is among the driest deserts on Earth with intense solar UV exposure, conditions that accelerate wear on seals and displays differently from a humid or coastal industrial environment.

A distinct brine-evaporation process for lithium

Lithium extraction on the salt flats uses solar evaporation ponds rather than hard-rock crushing, introducing a different, hypersaline environmental exposure ahead of any dust hazard at the processing plant.

Fleet management across a dispersed high-desert footprint

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices across geographically separated, high-altitude mine sites from one console.

How the work is organised

Remote shifts, long distances and contractor-heavy campaigns

Mine sites in Chile are remote from the coast, with camps, rotating shifts and long transfers. The handset has to survive that way of working as much as the classified area.

Concentrators01

Rounds in dust and noise

A concentrator operator moves between crushing, grinding and flotation areas. IP68 sealing is designed for fine dust that gets into every seam, while enrolment in an existing console keeps the fleet under control.

Maintenance02

Planned stoppages

Mills and crushers are relined during stoppages that bring large contractor crews. Photographs of wear and a logged position support the maintenance planner at the same time.

Brine03

Pond fields and drying plants

Brine operators drive long routes between ponds and plants. Four-constellation GNSS puts a coordinate on a pump or pond inspection where there are few landmarks. See the explosion proof phone for SQM for the lithium side.

Questions · Mining

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for mining use in Chile yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and the IECEx dust-rating scope is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does high altitude affect the device's rating?

Altitude affects environmental and battery performance testing rather than the Ex classification itself; the certificate will state its exact scope and test conditions once issued.

Is lithium extraction the same hazard class as copper mining?

Both fall under combustible-dust classification at their respective processing plants, though the brine-evaporation stage of lithium extraction is a distinct process from hard-rock copper crushing.

Does one device work across Chile's major mining regions?

Yes, the Zone 21/22 dust rating is intended to cover the processing plants of both the northern copper belt and the Atacama lithium operations.

Which Chilean mining sites sit highest?

Escondida and Collahuasi are above 3,000 metres and Chuquicamata above 2,800. Altitude affects battery and environmental testing rather than the Ex classification itself.

Do we need different handsets for copper and lithium?

No. The Zone 1 and Zone 21 design target covers both, though the pond fields themselves are largely not classified and the plants are where the rating matters.

Where do we start with a specific operator?

Read the operator page first, such as the explosion proof phone for Codelco, then ask that operator's procurement and HSE teams for their equipment approval route.
