Explosion proof mobile phone for oil, gas and refining in Chile

An explosion proof mobile phone for oil, gas and refining in Chile has to suit a downstream sector that runs at opposite ends of the country's climate range: refining at Concón and Biobío in the temperate centre, and gas production in Magallanes, near the sub-Antarctic tip of South America. One national operator, two very different classified-area environments, plus a handful of terminals and plants in between.

Why it fits oil, gas and refining

Built for a national operator spanning temperate refineries and sub-Antarctic gas fields

ENAP's refining and upstream gas operations sit thousands of kilometres apart under one operator, with climate conditions that range from mild coastal weather to some of the harshest cold in the hemisphere.

Zone 1 gas at refinery process units

Crude distillation, catalytic units and tank farms at Concón and Biobío are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of refining service.

Extreme cold at Magallanes gas fields

Gas production in the Magallanes region operates in a sub-Antarctic climate with severe wind chill and sustained low temperatures, conditions that test battery performance and material flexibility well beyond what a temperate-zone refinery experiences.

One operator, two climate extremes

ENAP runs both the temperate-zone refineries and the far-southern gas fields, meaning a single device standard adopted company-wide needs to perform reliably across that full climate range rather than being optimised for one end of it.

Remote southern logistics

Magallanes gas operations are far from Chile's main population and supply centres, closer in character to a remote wellfield than an urban-adjacent refinery, which raises the value of a rugged, long-battery-life device.

Centrally managed across a geographically split operation

EMM and MDM enrolment supports ENAP managing devices consistently across refining and upstream gas operations separated by thousands of kilometres.

How the work is organised

Import terminals, shutdowns and long southern distances

The sector is small in headcount compared with mining but dense in classified area. Work follows the ships, the planned shutdowns and, in Magallanes, the weather.

Terminals01

Ship arrival and cargo transfer

Quintero Bay and Mejillones handle gas, crude and product carriers. The operator on the jetty controls transfer, checks hoses and logs readings, and needs a handset that works in spray and does not have to be left at the gate.

Refining02

Planned unit turnarounds

A refinery's units are stopped on a planned cycle and contractors outnumber operators for weeks. Handset enrolment through a console the operator controls allows quick onboarding and quick removal.

Southern gas03

Methanol, gas and wind-fuel sites

Operations in Magallanes run in cold, wind and short winter daylight, far from central Chile. The sites are different in character, from field wells to a methanol plant, but each needs positions and photographs sent without leaving the classified area.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use in Chile's oil and gas sector yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device suit Magallanes' extreme cold?

Environmental durability testing is specified with sustained cold and wind-chill exposure in mind, though the certificate will state its exact test conditions once issued.

Does one device cover both refining and upstream gas operations?

Yes, the same Zone 1, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is intended to serve both ENAP's refinery process units and its Magallanes gas-field classified areas.

Is ENAP the only refiner in Chile?

ENAP operates the country's main refineries at Concón and Biobío; it is the dominant state-linked operator across both refining and upstream gas.

Is ENAP the only operator that needs an explosion proof phone in Chile?

No. Terminal operators, the methanol plant at Cabo Negro, LPG and fuel distributors and the new hydrogen projects all handle flammable gas or liquid. Each has its own procurement and HSE process, and none is described here as a customer.

Are hydrogen projects a different hazard class?

Hydrogen is a gas group IIC substance, the most demanding of the common groups, and the EX-04Z1 target marking is Ex ib IIC T4. A project's own area classification and equipment list decide what is accepted.

How should we approach a terminal operator?

Ask its HSE and procurement teams for the equipment approval route, and the marine terminal's own rules on portable electronic devices, which often differ from the shore plant.
