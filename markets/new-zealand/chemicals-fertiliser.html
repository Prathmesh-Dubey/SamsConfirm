Why it fits chemicals and fertiliser

Built for ammonia synthesis, not just hydrocarbon processing

Urea and ammonia production shares some classified-area logic with oil and gas processing, but ammonia's toxicity risk sits alongside the flammability hazard in a way that shapes site safety procedures beyond the Ex rating alone.

Zone 1/2 gas at the synthesis and reforming units

Ammonia synthesis and the natural-gas reforming process feeding it involve Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas-hazard areas, and Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety is specified for the hydrocarbon and process gas groups involved.

Ammonia toxicity as a second hazard layer

Beyond the flammable-gas classification, ammonia's toxicity means site safety procedures around a communications device carried into process areas need to account for gas-detection and emergency-response protocols specific to ammonia exposure, not only explosion risk.

Feedstock supply tied to Taranaki's declining gas basin

As a gas-intensive process, fertiliser production at Kapuni is directly exposed to the same upstream gas-supply variability affecting Taranaki's methanol producers, a dynamic that puts a premium on reliable field communications during supply-driven operational changes.

A single dedicated plant, not a distributed network

Unlike the dairy sector's many processing sites, Kapuni represents New Zealand's primary domestic fertiliser production point, meaning device standardisation here is a single-site decision rather than a network-wide rollout.

Fleet management for a single large process site

EMM and MDM enrolment suits managing devices across Kapuni's reforming, synthesis and product-handling areas from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at fertiliser plants in New Zealand yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does ammonia toxicity require a different certification from a standard gas-hazard device?

Ex certification addresses the flammable-gas classification; ammonia toxicity is managed through separate site gas-detection and emergency-response protocols rather than a different device certification.

Is Kapuni the only fertiliser plant in New Zealand?

It is the country's primary domestic urea and ammonia production site; some fertiliser products are also imported.

Does gas-supply variability affect fertiliser production the same way it affects methanol?

Yes, both are gas-intensive processes drawing on the same Taranaki basin and are similarly exposed to upstream supply variability.
