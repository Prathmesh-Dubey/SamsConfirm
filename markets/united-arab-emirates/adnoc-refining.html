The site · Ruwais refinery

Ruwais refinery: East, West and the crude chain behind them

Ruwais is two refineries side by side. Press reports put the complex at around 840,000 barrels a day of crude distillation, split between Ruwais West, commissioned in 2015 as part of Takreer's expansion, and the older Ruwais East, whose roots go back to the industrial city opened in the early 1980s. Crude reaches Ruwais from the Jebel Dhanna terminal, which receives Upper Zakum and other grades. In 2018 ADNOC announced a Crude Flexibility Project to let Ruwais West process heavier and sourer crude, which meant new fractionators and residue desulphurisation reactors lifted into a working site. Refinery work is cyclical: long runs, then planned shutdowns when both refineries were taken down for maintenance in early 2020, and a continuing programme of tie-ins in between.

Hydrogen sets the gas group

Hydrotreating and residue desulphurisation run on high-pressure hydrogen, which is gas group IIC. A group IIC design target is therefore not decoration at Ruwais: it is the reason the EX-04Z1 is specified to Ex ib IIC T4, and the certificate will state the exact scope when issued.

Turnaround surges

During a shutdown the contractor population and permit volume climb sharply, and so does the number of devices issued at the gate. A handset enrolled in the operator's console, carrying the permit and the site directory, shortens that issue and lets you wipe a unit at demobilisation.

Rounds over a large footprint

A refinery round runs from distillation through pump rows to tank farms. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified to cover a shift and four-constellation GNSS puts a position on a photograph of a leaking flange.

Heat and salt together

Ruwais is coastal and hot, so sealing and temperature class both matter. IP68 and rugged build are the design answers; the temperature class on the certificate is what is checked against the area classification.

The approval path

Two gates for ADNOC Refining

National conformity comes through MoIAT's ECAS-Ex scheme, which builds on IECEx certification; with TDRA approval for the radio, these are in progress for the EX-04Z1 and none is issued. The second gate is ADNOC's own supplier registration, pre-qualification and In-Country Value scoring, which is separate. We cannot describe the internal criteria ADNOC Refining applies to handhelds, so ask the Ruwais procurement and HSE teams, and ask also whether contractors may bring their own devices during a turnaround or must use operator-issued ones.

Questions · ADNOC Refining

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 approved for ADNOC Refining sites?

No. National certification is a prerequisite for ADNOC's own vendor review, and neither is complete. SAMS has no approval, order or relationship with ADNOC Refining; this page describes design intent.

Can we get evaluation units for a Ruwais turnaround trial?

Evaluation units are for safe-area handling and management trials ahead of launch, not for classified-area use before the certificate exists. Contact SAMS with your operator affiliation to discuss timing.

Why does hydrogen matter for a phone?

Hydrogen is in gas group IIC, the most demanding group, because its ignition energy is very low. A handset marked only for a lower group cannot be taken into hydrogen service, so the group on the certificate matters for hydrotreating units.
