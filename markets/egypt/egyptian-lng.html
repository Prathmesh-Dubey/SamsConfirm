Why it fits

Built for a dedicated liquefaction and export site

Egyptian LNG's Idku terminal runs a narrower set of processes than an integrated refinery, but the liquefaction trains and marine loading arms it does run bring their own specific handling requirements.

Feed-gas treatment through to liquefaction in one classified chain

From feed-gas reception through treatment and liquefaction to LNG storage, the terminal's classified areas form one continuous Zone 1 / Zone 2 chain that a single device specification is designed to cover end to end.

Marine loading arms and jetty operations

LNG carrier loading at the terminal jetty combines a classified marine environment with cryogenic-adjacent equipment, and the device's IP68 sealing and environmental testing are specified with that jetty duty cycle in mind.

A tighter, more specialised operator profile than a multi-process refinery

As a single-purpose liquefaction terminal, Idku's engineering and safety review is likely more centralised than at a diversified refining and petrochemical site, which can mean a more direct approval path once the certificate basis is accepted.

Coastal exposure on the Mediterranean

Idku's Mediterranean coastal location adds salt-air and humidity exposure to the environmental testing profile alongside the gas-hazard classification.

Fleet management for a single large terminal

EMM and MDM enrolment suits managing devices across one terminal's process, storage and marine-loading areas from a single console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Idku yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and IECEx certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device need cryogenic-specific certification for the loading berth?

Ex certification addresses the gas-hazard classification of the area; separate environmental testing addresses cold-exposure durability, and the certificate will state its exact scope once issued.

Is Idku's approval process the same as Damietta's?

No, the two terminals are operated by different companies (Egyptian LNG and Union Fenosa Gas respectively), each with its own site engineering review.

Does the device cover the full site or just the loading berth?

The Zone 1 / Zone 2 specification is intended to cover the full process chain from feed-gas treatment through liquefaction to marine loading, not the berth alone.
