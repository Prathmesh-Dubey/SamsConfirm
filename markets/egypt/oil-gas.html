Egypt · Oil & Gas

Explosion proof mobile phone for oil and gas in Egypt

Egypt's upstream sector spans three very different environments at once: the mature offshore platforms of the Gulf of Suez, the deepwater Zohr field in the Mediterranean, and onshore Western Desert production. The EX-04Z1 is designed for the Zone 1 classified areas common to all three, for the operators and contractors who move between them.

Why it fits oil and gas

Built for offshore, deepwater and desert operations alike

Gulf of Suez platforms, the Zohr deepwater field and Western Desert wellsites put very different operating conditions under the same Zone 1 gas-hazard umbrella.

Zone 1 gas as the common baseline

Wellhead platforms, production separators and gathering stations across all three basins are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres. Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety is specified to cover the hydrocarbon gas groups found across Egypt's upstream mix.

Offshore duty cycle in the Gulf of Suez

Decades-old Gulf of Suez platforms combine an older asset base with a marine environment, a combination that puts a premium on a handset that tolerates salt-air exposure and vibration without special handling.

Deepwater support work for Zohr

Zohr's subsea infrastructure is supported from onshore and nearshore facilities where crews carry a device between control rooms, jetties and support vessels, environments with different classification boundaries in close proximity.

Western Desert's remote wellsite pattern

Onshore Western Desert fields are spread over long distances from the nearest base, closer in character to a remote wellfield than a compact platform, where IP68 sealing and a long-life battery matter for crews who may not return to base the same day.

Centrally managed across a mixed operator landscape

EMM and MDM enrolment lets an operator or joint-venture partner manage devices consistently across offshore, deepwater and onshore assets from one console.

Who runs it

Oil and gas operators in Egypt

Upstream activity is coordinated through the state holding companies, with international partners operating the largest individual fields.

Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation (EGPC)
State operator overseeing exploration, production-sharing agreements, refining and distribution across the Egyptian oil sector.
Eni / Petrobel & Petroshorouk
Operator of the Zohr field, the largest natural gas discovery in the Mediterranean, under production-sharing agreement with EGAS.
Typical classified areas
Wellhead platforms and gathering stations, production separators, onshore processing and export facilities.
Certification target
IECEx Certificate of Conformity as the technical basis; ATEX in parallel given the strong European IOC presence in Egyptian upstream.
Questions · Oil & Gas

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil and gas use in Egypt yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does it work for both offshore and onshore fields?

The Zone 1, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is designed to cover both, so an operator active in the Gulf of Suez and the Western Desert does not need separate devices for each environment.

Who approves equipment for use on EGPC-licensed fields?

Approval typically follows the operating partner's own engineering review, built on the underlying international certificate, alongside EGPC's regulatory role as concession holder.

Does the device suit joint-venture and IOC-operated fields?

Yes — the IECEx-based documentation is intended to be recognisable to the international oil companies operating fields like Zohr under production-sharing agreements, not only to state entities.
