Why it fits LNG and gas processing

Built for the liquefaction-to-transmission chain

LNG export and domestic gas processing put different equipment and different failure modes in the same classified-area conversation, from cryogenic loading arms to compressor stations hundreds of kilometres inland.

Zone 1 gas atmospheres at the liquefaction train

Feed-gas treatment, liquefaction and LNG storage and loading areas at Damietta and Idku are classified Zone 1 or Zone 2 depending on process step, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups present.

Low-temperature exposure at the loading berth

Cryogenic LNG loading arms and jetties introduce an extreme cold exposure alongside the gas hazard, and the device's environmental testing is specified with that combination in mind rather than a purely hot-climate assumption.

A nationwide transmission network, not one site

GASCO's processing and transmission network spans compressor stations and gas plants across Egypt, a distributed footprint where a single device standard simplifies procurement compared with site-by-site specification.

Marine terminal environment at both export sites

Both Damietta and Idku sit on the Mediterranean coast, adding salt-air and marine-jetty exposure to the classified-area equipment list.

Fleet management across a distributed asset base

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a gas transmission operator managing devices across many compressor stations and two coastal terminals from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for LNG terminal use yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device need special certification for cryogenic exposure?

The Ex certification covers the gas-hazard classification of the area; separate environmental testing addresses cold-exposure durability, and the certificate scope will state exactly what conditions are covered once issued.

Does one device cover both LNG terminals and inland gas plants?

That is the design intent — a single Zone 1 / Zone 2 specification meant to serve both the coastal liquefaction terminals and the inland processing and compressor stations in the GASCO network.

Do Damietta and Idku use the same vendor-approval process?

No, they are operated by different companies (Union Fenosa Gas and Egyptian LNG respectively) and each runs its own site engineering review.
