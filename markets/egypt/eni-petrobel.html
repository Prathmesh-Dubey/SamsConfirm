Why it fits

Built for an Italian-operated deepwater development

Eni's own procurement standards, shaped by its European base and its ATEX-region operating experience, sit alongside the Egyptian regulatory framework, and the two don't always default to the same certificate first.

ATEX alongside IECEx as the documentation pair

As an Italian operator with an EU regulatory home base, Eni's engineering teams are likely to expect ATEX documentation as a familiar reference point in addition to IECEx, and the EX-04Z1's certification programme is being run to produce both.

Deepwater subsea development supported from onshore

Zohr's gas is produced from deepwater subsea wells tied back to onshore processing, meaning most handset use happens at onshore support facilities, control rooms and the associated gas-processing plant rather than on an offshore platform deck.

A joint-venture operating structure

Petrobel and Petroshorouk are joint ventures between Eni, EGPC and EGAS, so equipment decisions can involve alignment across more than one shareholder even with Eni as lead operator.

The largest single gas project in Egypt's upstream

Zohr's scale means a large associated contractor and engineering workforce cycling through the project, which is the kind of volume EMM and MDM fleet management is built to simplify.

Comparable IOC experience elsewhere in the Mediterranean

Eni operates similar deepwater gas projects across the Mediterranean, and documentation prepared to Eni's typical evidence standard for Zohr is intended to be reusable for Eni-operated sites elsewhere in the region.

About the operator

Eni / Petrobel & Petroshorouk

Eni operates the Zohr field, the largest natural gas discovery in the Mediterranean, through its Petrobel and Petroshorouk joint ventures with Egyptian state partners.

Field
Zohr, a deepwater gas field discovered in 2015, producing since 2017.
Operating structure
Petrobel and Petroshorouk joint ventures between Eni, EGPC and EGAS, under production-sharing agreement.
Field type
Deepwater subsea wells tied back to onshore gas processing.

Eni's engineering review, informed by both ATEX (its European regulatory home) and IECEx, alongside EGAS's regulatory oversight.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Zohr yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and certification, including both IECEx and ATEX, is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does Eni require ATEX certification specifically, or is IECEx enough?

Eni's engineering review is more likely to reference ATEX given its European base, so the certification programme is targeting both rather than assuming IECEx alone is sufficient.

Is most of the classified-area work offshore or onshore for Zohr?

The wells are offshore and subsea, but a large share of day-to-day handset use happens at onshore processing and support facilities, which is where most classified-area device traffic occurs.

Does approval at Zohr carry to other Eni-operated fields?

Not automatically, but documentation prepared to Eni's typical evidence standard is intended to be a useful starting point for other Eni-operated sites in the region.
