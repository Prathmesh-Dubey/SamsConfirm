Why it fits

Designed for a company blending Japanese and Shell-heritage engineering practice

The 2019 merger with Showa Shell means Idemitsu Kosan's engineering review can draw on both its own long-standing Japanese practices and standards inherited from Shell's international operations.

European Shell-heritage documentation alongside Japanese practice

Showa Shell's history as part of the Shell group means some of its former sites carry documentation and engineering conventions closer to Shell's international (ATEX-influenced) standards, which the EX-04Z1's certification programme is designed to be recognisable against, alongside IECEx and JIS.

A historically independent, family-founded corporate culture

Idemitsu Kosan's roots as an independently run, family-founded company gave it a distinct corporate culture from the more consolidation-driven ENEOS, an independence that has shaped its own vendor relationships and procurement style even after the Showa Shell merger.

Zone 1 gas across refining and petrochemical operations

Refinery process units and petrochemical production at Idemitsu Kosan sites are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of both.

A company still integrating two distinct heritages post-merger

As with any major merger, harmonising former Idemitsu and former Showa Shell sites' engineering and vendor-approval conventions is an ongoing process, relevant context for how a new vendor's documentation gets received at a given site.

Fleet management across a merged refining and petrochemical network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports Idemitsu Kosan managing a consistent device profile across sites with different corporate heritages.

The sites

Explosion proof mobile phone use across Idemitsu refineries in Japan

After the 2019 integration Idemitsu's refining footprint includes Hokkaido at Tomakomai, Chiba at Ichihara, Aichi at Chita and Yokkaichi in Mie, together with the Tokuyama petrochemical complex in Yamaguchi Prefecture. The Chiba site is a refinery and petrochemical complex in one, so a shift can pass from a petrochemical unit to crude distillation within the same fence. Typical hazards are hydrocarbon vapour from crude and product streams, hydrogen from hydroprocessing, aromatics such as benzene, and polymer powder in downstream plants. Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas areas dominate, with dust areas where powder is handled. Check the classification drawing for the specific unit before choosing a device.

Use cases

How work is organised across two heritages

Integration01

Two documentation conventions

Former Showa Shell sites may be more used to documentation in the style of an international oil major, while legacy Idemitsu sites follow Japanese practice. A supplier that prepares one clear document set suited to both avoids being asked for it twice.

Turnaround02

Shutdown campaigns

Idemitsu refineries run planned shutdowns when units are stopped, inspected and restarted. Contractors arrive in numbers, and a handset enrolled in a managed fleet can be issued with the permit and photo-reporting apps already installed.

Cold weather03

Hokkaido winter rounds

Outdoor rounds in a northern winter test battery capacity and touchscreen use with gloves. Check the ambient temperature range on the certificate against the lowest temperature the site expects.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Idemitsu Kosan sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the Shell heritage mean ATEX certification is expected?

Some former Showa Shell sites may be more familiar with ATEX-style documentation given Shell's international background, so the certification programme is designed to be recognisable across both ATEX-influenced and standard Japanese conventions.

Has the 2019 merger fully unified vendor-approval practices?

Harmonisation is an ongoing process; sites with different corporate heritage may still reflect some of their own historical conventions.

Is Idemitsu Kosan the same as ENEOS or Cosmo Oil?

No, all three are separate, competing Japanese refiners with their own distinct procurement processes.

Which Idemitsu sites would use an explosion proof phone?

Refinery process units, tank farms, jetties and petrochemical plants at sites such as Chiba, Aichi and Yokkaichi are the usual candidates. The classification drawing and permit rules at each site decide, and should be checked with the site engineer.

What does a supplier ask Idemitsu's teams about registration?

Ask the relevant Idemitsu procurement and HSE teams for the supplier registration process and hazardous-area equipment requirements. SAMS has not verified a specific programme and does not claim one.

Do the two heritages need two device specifications?

Not on the device side. The design target is one Zone 1 / Zone 21 handset, with a documentation set a reviewer used to either convention can follow. Each site still reviews it independently.

Is polymer powder a dust hazard on these sites?

Combustible powder handling creates dust areas, often Zone 21 or 22, at downstream plants. The EX-04Z1 design target includes Zone 21, subject to the certificate once issued.
