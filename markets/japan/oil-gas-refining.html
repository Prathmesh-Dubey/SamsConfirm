Japan imports nearly all of its crude oil, so its downstream sector runs on a small number of large, tightly managed refiners operating nationwide, several of them the product of major industry consolidation in the past two decades. Equipment reaching these sites needs to satisfy both international certification and Japan's own JIS-referenced explosion-protection standards.

Why it fits oil, gas and refining

Built for a consolidated, import-dependent refining sector

Japan's refining industry has consolidated significantly through mergers over the past fifteen years, producing large national refiners that each operate multiple sites with different engineering histories.

Zone 1 gas at refinery process units

Crude distillation, catalytic units and tank farms across Japan's refineries are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and the device's Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety targets the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of refining service.

JIS-referenced explosion protection alongside IECEx

Japan's domestic standards for explosion-protected equipment are referenced through the Japanese Industrial Standards (JIS) system, generally aligned with IEC standards but with its own certification and marking conventions that Japanese operators expect to see addressed directly.

Merger-driven mixed engineering standards

Major refiners have formed through consolidation of previously independent companies, meaning a single refiner's sites can carry different legacy engineering conventions from their predecessor companies even under one current corporate name.

Coastal refinery and marine-terminal exposure

Japan's refineries are predominantly coastal, with tanker unloading and marine terminal facilities adding salt-air exposure and jetty classified areas to the equipment list.

Fleet management across a nationwide, multi-site refiner

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a refiner managing devices consistently across multiple sites with different operating histories from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil and gas use in Japan yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx and JIS-referenced marking, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is IECEx certification sufficient for use in Japan?

IECEx is generally recognised as supporting technical evidence, but Japanese operators typically also expect JIS-referenced marking specific to the domestic system.

Do merged refiners like ENEOS have one unified vendor list?

Not necessarily; sites that were part of different predecessor companies before a merger can retain some of their own legacy equipment conventions.

Does the device suit Japan's coastal refinery locations?

Yes, environmental testing and IP68 sealing are specified with marine and coastal terminal exposure in mind.
