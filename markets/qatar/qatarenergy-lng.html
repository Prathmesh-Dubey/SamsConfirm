The operator · QatarEnergy LNG

Explosion proof mobile phone for QatarEnergy LNG in Qatar

QatarEnergy LNG operates the liquefaction trains at Ras Laffan, which public sources put at 14 trains and about 77 million tonnes a year of capacity. The North Field East expansion is adding four mega trains of 8 million tonnes a year each, and a North Field South phase follows, so the operator is running a mature plant and commissioning new capacity on the same site. Each train takes North Field gas, removes acid gas and water, liquefies it at about minus 160 degrees Celsius and sends it to tank. Cargoes leave from the LNG berths in the port. This page deals with the operator's own trains and berths. The wider city, with GTL and the condensate refinery, is covered on the Ras Laffan page.

Operator-run areas

Train feed gas reception
Slug catchers, inlet separation and metering where gas arrives from the sea lines.
Acid gas removal
Solvent-based units that take out hydrogen sulphide and carbon dioxide.

What multi-train LNG operation asks of a device

The trains are alike, but the work around them differs by train, berth and project. Four requirements recur.

IP ratingIP68
Battery4,000 mAh
Camera50 MP
OSAndroid 16

One specification across old and new trains

Existing trains and the expansion trains may be classified differently in detail. A target of Zone 1, gas group IIC and T4 is set at the demanding end so one issued device follows a technician across both.

Cold service and heavy gloves

Cryogenic areas mean insulated gloves. A handset that cannot be worked while gloved is left in a pocket. Evaluation crews should test glove handling at the cold box, and we would rather hear the result before a fleet decision.

Managing hundreds of units from one console

A multi-train operator has a large workforce on a shared network. Android Enterprise with the existing MDM console lets telecoms enrol units, push applications and set camera policy centrally.

Berth work with a vessel alongside

Cargo transfer is timed. IP68 sealing copes with spray and washdown, and the camera and call functions cover the check-and-report tasks that currently use a radio and a separate permitted camera.

How work is organised

Trains, turnarounds and approval at QatarEnergy LNG

LNG plants are run train by train. Planned turnarounds are staged, so most trains are producing while one is down.

Train-based operation
Each train has its own operators and control room team, and a field round covers one train or its auxiliaries.
Turnarounds
A train is shut for planned inspection and maintenance, and a large contractor population arrives for the scope. Handsets issued for that period need fast enrolment and recovery.
Commissioning of new trains
New trains are handed from construction to operations in stages, with both groups on site under different rules.
Vendor approval
Ask QatarEnergy LNG procurement and HSE what a supplier must supply before equipment is used in a train. Registration and a local-content score may apply across QatarEnergy contracts.
National conformity
MOCI conformity and CRA radio approval are in the launch programme and have not been granted.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 approved for Ras Laffan LNG trains?

No. It is pre-launch, with a Q4 2026 launch date and certification in progress. The operator's engineering and HSE review follows the certificates, and nothing has been issued.

Is a handset suitable for cryogenic areas?

A certificate sets the permitted area by zone, gas group and temperature class. Cold service is a separate question of materials and handling. Test glove use and cold exposure on your own plant before committing.

Which parts of Ras Laffan does this page not cover?

GTL, the condensate refinery, helium and the port as a whole are covered on the Ras Laffan page. This page concerns the liquefaction operator's own trains and berths.

Can one console manage units across several trains?

Yes. The device enrols in existing MDM consoles, so groups can be set by train, shift or contractor, with camera and application policy applied to each.
