Finland · Mining, metals and battery chemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for mining, metals and battery chemicals in Finland

An explosion proof mobile phone for Finland's battery metals chain has to work in three very different places: the open pit and bioleaching heaps at Terrafame's Sotkamo site, the chemical and metals park at Kokkola, and the processing plants around Harjavalta. Each has its own operator, its own area classification documents and its own view of what a handset may do. This page is for the safety, instrumentation and telecoms people who make that call.

The sites · Sotkamo, Kokkola, Harjavalta

An explosion proof mobile phone for Finnish mining and metals sites

Terrafame's site at Sotkamo in Kainuu runs from its own open-pit mine, through heap bioleaching, to a battery chemicals plant, all on one industrial site. Air is blown into the heaps and an acidic solution is irrigated through them, so much of the site is open ground covering a large area, with the classified zones concentrated in the chemical plant rather than the mine. Kokkola is a different kind of place: an industrial park where Boliden's zinc plant, Umicore's cobalt and precursor operations, CABB, Yara and others share services and neighbours. Harjavalta in Satakunta adds copper, nickel and battery material processing further south.

Open-air ground, largely outside the classified boundary, with long distances between inspection points.
Built for local conditions · Kainuu to Kokkola

What a battery metals site does to a handset

Gas is the hazard people expect. In this sector the dust, the acid and the cold decide how a trial goes.

Metal and precursor powders are not grain dust

Fine metal and compound powders can be conductive, which is why Zone 21 dust protection, and the group IIIC target for it, matter more here than at a feed mill.

Kainuu is a cold place to carry a phone

Sotkamo sits far enough north that February rounds happen in deep frost. rugged build covers low temperature and thermal shock, and the lowest permitted ambient temperature will be fixed by the certificate and published with the datasheet. Ask for that figure before anything else.

Wet, acidic and splashed

IP68 sealing is designed to cope with water and washdown. Resistance of the housing to a particular acid or reagent is a separate question, and the right place to settle it is the datasheet and your own chemical compatibility list, not a general assurance.

A chain of separate companies

Sotkamo, Kokkola and Harjavalta are run by different operators, and contractors move between parks. Android Enterprise enrolment lets each operator keep devices in its own existing MDM console and apply its own policy.

Use cases · Mining and metals

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work in Finland.

Mining01

Heap and plant rounds at Sotkamo

An operator checking irrigation lines and collection points covers distance in freezing weather, then walks into the chemical plant where the classification changes. One handset that covers both the open ground and the classified process areas avoids a swap at the plant door.

Metals02

Maintenance windows at Kokkola

A park with several process plants has contractors from outside moving between employers' areas during planned stops. A photograph of an isolation point, tied to a position and a time, sits in the work record instead of on a personal phone.

Processing03

Sampling and handover

A sample taken from a powder line or a solution tank needs a location, a time and a photograph of the label before it goes to the laboratory. Capturing those at the sample point, inside the zone, replaces notes written later at a desk.

Shifts, contractors and access

How work is organised, and what site teams will ask for

Mine and process sites in Finland run continuous shifts with planned maintenance stops, during which the contractor headcount rises sharply. In an industrial park such as Kokkola, a contractor may hold access to more than one operator's area in a single week, and each operator applies its own rules about what may be carried.

For the equipment itself, the operator's explosion protection document is what governs. The employer records the area classification and the equipment chosen for each area, so the safety engineer will want the certificate reference, the full marking and the temperature class before a handset is added to that document. We have not verified a specific supplier registration scheme at any of these operators, so ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams what they require, and which of the sites on your list apply it.

Questions · Mining and metals

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at a mine or smelter in Finland yet?

No. It is pre-launch, ATEX and IECEx certification is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch, and no certificate has been issued. A handset should not enter a classified area at Sotkamo, Kokkola or anywhere else before the certificate covering that use exists.

Why does the dust group matter for metal processing?

Dust protection is divided into IIIA, IIIB and IIIC, and IIIC covers conductive dust. Metal powders can fall into that class, so the design target of Zone 21 protection to IIIC is relevant here. The final selection rests on the operator's own testing of the powder involved.

Can one device be used at both Sotkamo and Kokkola?

The design target is the same, Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust. But each operator classifies its own plant and keeps its own equipment list, so a handset accepted at one site still has to be accepted at the next.
