Why it fits mining, metals and battery chemicals

Built for a metals chain with dust risk at every processing stage

Battery-grade nickel, zinc and cobalt processing carries combustible metal dust risk from mining through refining, a hazard profile distinct from the gas atmospheres more common in oil and gas.

Zone 21/22 dust atmospheres in metal processing

Ore handling, concentrate drying and metal powder processing at Terrafame's Sotkamo site and Boliden's Kokkola zinc smelter generate combustible dust atmospheres, and Ex tb IIIC T135°C dust protection applies where a purely gas-rated device would not.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Finland's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives across the mining and processing chain.

A processing chain spread across separate companies and regions

Sotkamo in Kainuu, Kokkola on the west coast and Harjavalta further south are run by different operators, meaning a battery materials company sourcing from more than one stage needs equipment consistent across sites it does not itself own.

Fleet management across a national metals processing network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator or contractor managing devices consistently across geographically separated mining and processing sites.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for mining use in Finland yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is dust the main hazard in this sector rather than gas?

Metal ore and concentrate handling is primarily a dust hazard, though sites can carry gas-classified areas too; the EX-04Z1's certification programme is designed to address both under one rating.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for Finnish operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Does approval at one processing site extend to another company further down the chain?

No, each company in the mining-to-processing chain runs its own procurement process independently.
