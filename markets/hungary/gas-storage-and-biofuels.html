Why it fits gas, storage and biofuels

Built for a landlocked gas storage system and a separate biofuel sector

Without marine gas import options, Hungary relies on underground storage and cross-border pipeline transmission for security of supply, while bioethanol and starch processing form an entirely separate agricultural-industrial hazard profile.

Zone 1 gas at storage sites and compressor stations

Underground gas storage at Magyar Földgáztároló's Zsana facility and compressor stations on the FGSZ transmission network are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the natural gas atmospheres typical of this equipment.

Zone 1/21 atmospheres in bioethanol and starch processing

Fermentation, distillation and starch drying at Pannonia Bio's Dunaföldvár plant and Hungrana's Szabadegyháza site generate both gas and dust classified areas, a different hazard profile from conventional gas infrastructure.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Hungary's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives across both the gas and biofuel sectors.

A landlocked storage-dependent gas security model

Being without coastal LNG import options, Hungary's gas security rests more heavily on underground storage capacity than in a country with its own import terminals, a dependency that shapes where classified-area equipment investment concentrates.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use in Hungary yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does being landlocked change the classified-area rating itself?

It affects supply security strategy rather than the Ex classification; the same Zone 1 gas rating applies to landlocked storage and transmission equipment as to a coastal country's.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for Hungarian operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Do bioethanol plants carry the same classified-area profile as gas storage sites?

No, bioethanol and starch processing typically combine gas and dust atmospheres from fermentation and drying, a different profile from the purely gas-rated areas at a storage or transmission site.
