Explosion proof mobile phone for LNG & Gas Processing in Malaysia

The sector · LNG and gas processing

Explosion proof mobile phone for gas processing and LNG in Malaysia

Gas processing in Terengganu is carried out at six plants in two complexes, Gas Processing Kertih and Gas Processing Santong, taking feed gas from offshore fields. Processed gas reaches Peninsular customers through the Peninsular Gas Utilisation pipeline system. In the east, the Sabah Oil and Gas Terminal at Kimanis, operational since December 2013, takes production from Sabah and links to the Sabah-Sarawak gas pipeline that runs towards Bintulu. At Bintulu, nine liquefaction trains make LNG (see the explosion proof phone for Malaysia LNG page), and PETRONAS has also built floating LNG units for offshore fields. Regasification is on the other side of the chain, with a terminal at Sungai Udang in Melaka and another within the Pengerang complex in Johor, covered on the Pengerang page. Each stage presents a different workday. A gas plant operator patrols open process areas, while a regas terminal technician works around a small number of cryogenic tanks and vaporisers. The hazard is predominantly methane, with condensate and refrigerant hydrocarbons at the plants that handle them.

Inlet and slug catching
Pressure letdown and separation, with free hydrocarbons present.

Fractionation
Columns, reboilers and pumps handling light hydrocarbons.
Compression
Gas compressor halls, often with forced ventilation.
Cryogenic tanks and vaporisers

One handset where there were three

A gas plant operator normally carries a radio, a torch and a camera, each separately certified. The EX-04Z1 brings voice, a 50 MP camera and a screen into one intrinsically safe device, which cuts what must be checked at a gate and what can be dropped over a handrail.

Offshore feed, onshore plants

Kerteh and Santong depend on platforms that send gas ashore, so a plant operator often talks to offshore control rooms. Calls, messages and photographs travel on the same device, and Android Enterprise lets IT push site apps and contacts to every unit.

Temperature class and the gas actually present

Methane has a high auto-ignition temperature, but condensate and heavier hydrocarbons in the same plant do not. The temperature class on the certificate, with a marking target of T4, is the figure an instrument engineer checks against the zone drawing.

Isolated, unmanned wellhead and terminal sites

Remote metering sites are visited by roving teams rather than resident staff. Four-constellation GNSS records where a reading was taken and a photograph of the gauge goes with it, which suits audits and incident reviews.

Short, wet gaps between safe areas

Operators move often between control room, workshop and process area in monsoon rain. IP68 sealing and glove operation keep the phone working through those transitions.

Questions · gas processing

Frequently asked questions

Is methane really the same hazard at every gas plant?

Mostly, but not entirely. Methane is gas group IIA and is the main component of pipeline gas. Plants that fractionate condensate, run refrigerant loops or handle hydrogen sulphide introduce other substances, and the area classification drawing names the group and temperature class that apply. The handset's marking must cover what is actually on that drawing.

What is different about a floating LNG unit?

A floating unit packs a full liquefaction process onto a vessel, so classified areas sit close to accommodation and marine equipment. Crew changes arrive by boat or helicopter, and marine safety rules apply alongside plant permits. Equipment must meet both.

Do regasification terminals need a different phone from liquefaction plants?

Not in principle. Both handle LNG and methane, and both depend on classification drawings and permit-to-work rules. The difference is in the workday: regas terminals are smaller, with fewer shifts, so fewer handsets are involved, but the area rules are just as strict.
