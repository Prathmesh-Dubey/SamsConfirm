The sector · Singapore refining and storage

An explosion proof mobile phone for refining and storage in Singapore

Singapore has no crude of its own, so refining and storage are an import business: crude arrives by tanker, products leave by ship and the tanks between hold working stock for the Asian market. Refineries are operated at Pulau Bukom by Aster Chemicals and Energy, at Jurong by ExxonMobil and on Jurong Island by Singapore Refining Company. Storage is run by terminal operators, including Vopak at Sebarok, Banyan and Penjuru, and by Jurong Rock Caverns, which keeps liquid hydrocarbons in rock below the seabed near Banyan. The hazards are mostly hydrocarbon vapour in group IIA and IIB, with hydrogen at the upgrading units pushing the refinery side to IIC. Dust is rare in this sector and mostly confined to catalyst handling. Work is organised around unit rounds, tank gauging, jetty operations and periodic turnarounds, and the cargo and the weather together set the pace.

What tanks and jetties do to a handset

In this sector the person is often the only sensor that moves. The handset has to record what they see.

Island trips set the working day

Bukom, Sebarok and Busing are reached by boat. A device that carries the call, camera, position and permit lookup avoids a second trip. A 4,000 mAh battery is sized for a full shift.

Hydrogen on the refinery side, IIA on the tank side

Refinery upgrading units can contain hydrogen, while tank storage is mostly IIA and IIB vapour. Marking the device II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 means one fleet covers both, with the temperature class on the certificate checked against each product.

Rain, spray and humidity

Jetties and tank tops are wet more often than dry. IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H construction are the design answer, and the certificate states the ambient range.

Gauging that has to be provable

A level or sample taken in the field has commercial value. A reading captured with position and time is easier to reconcile than a handwritten figure.

Each operator approves in its own way

Refiners and terminals run their own contractor equipment and permit systems. Ask each operator’s procurement and HSE teams what they require; we do not claim any listing.

Questions · Singapore refining and storage

Frequently asked questions

Do refineries and storage terminals need different handsets?

Not necessarily. The EX-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust with a gas group IIC target, so one device can cover refinery units, tank farms and jetties. Which areas it is accepted in is each site’s decision.

Is the underground storage a special case?

The caverns are below ground, but the surface plant and shafts are classified like other storage. Ask the operator for the area classification of the surface facilities.

Can SAMS say which refinery will use it?

No. SAMS has no named customers in Singapore and this page does not imply any. The device is pre-launch.

What does the sector regulator expect?

The Workplace Safety and Health Act puts duties on the occupier and the employer, and on Major Hazard Installations the safety case governs. A handset has to be consistent with the risk assessment, which has to name it.
