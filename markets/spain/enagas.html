The sites · Enagás

Where Enagás works in classified areas in Spain

Enagás is a different kind of operator from a refiner. Its hazardous areas are mostly methane, which is gas group IIA and relatively hard to ignite, spread across a national network instead of a single site. Three of its terminals are wholly owned, at Barcelona, Cartagena and Huelva, and it also holds the El Musel plant at Gijón and interests in the BBG terminal in Bilbao and the Saggas terminal at Sagunto. Between the terminals sit compression stations, pressure regulating and metering stations, and three underground storage facilities: Serrablo in Huesca, Yela in Guadalajara and Gaviota, an offshore field off the Bizkaia coast. Enagás also acts as technical system manager for the Spanish gas system. At a terminal the busy classified areas are the ship berth, the cryogenic tanks, the vaporisers and the send-out lines. Many open-air gas areas are Zone 2, with Zone 1 where a release is expected in normal operation, such as vents and sampling points.

What an Enagás site does to a handset

A gas transmission operator spends its time between sites, so the handset has to be useful at a terminal, on a pipeline route and in a car. The conditions below shape that.

Methane first, other gases in places

Methane is IIA, but odorant systems, nitrogen and sampling lines each have their own considerations. The EX-04Z1 is targeted at II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, which covers the gas groups an operator is likely to meet. The temperature class still has to be read against each classification drawing.

Lone workers at remote stations

Metering and regulating stations are often visited by one person, a long way from help. Four-constellation GNSS gives a position that can be shared with the control centre, and the 4G and Wi-Fi radios keep the call link up where coverage allows.

Ship unloading to a timetable

A carrier arrives to a schedule, and the people on the jetty, in the tank area and in the control building are all in contact for the duration. One device that works across those boundaries removes a swap each time.

Salt air and wash-down

Barcelona, Cartagena, Huelva, Gijón and the Bizkaia coast are all marine environments. IP68 sealing is meant for that rather than for an office.

Questions · Enagás

Explosion proof mobile phone questions for Spain

Is a Zone 1 handset overkill at a terminal that is mostly Zone 2?

Equipment suitable for Zone 1 may be used in Zone 2, so one handset covers both. Operators often prefer to issue a single device for the whole site rather than map each pocket of Zone 1 to a different phone.

Does Enagás publish equipment requirements for suppliers?

Operators of this size normally run supplier qualification and technical review, and some publish tender conditions. We have not verified a specific Enagás route for handsets, so ask Enagás procurement and the plant's HSE team what they require.

What about the offshore Gaviota storage field?

It is an offshore installation, so access, transport and communications are planned for a specific set of conditions. The EX-04Z1 has not been tested for it and nothing on this page claims that it has. Any offshore trial would need its own approvals.

Does the device replace a gas detector?

No. It is a communications and data device. Personal gas detection is a separate piece of equipment and a handset does not substitute for it.
