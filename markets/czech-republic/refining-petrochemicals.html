Czech Republic · Refining and petrochemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for refining and petrochemicals in the Czech Republic

An explosion proof mobile phone for the refining and petrochemical sector in the Czech Republic has to work in a small number of very large sites, all of them in Bohemia. The country has no coast, so its refineries at Litvínov and Kralupy nad Vltavou run on pipeline crude and supply the Záluží petrochemical complex and a handful of neighbours. This page is for the operators and contractors who decide what may be carried into those plants.

The sector · Refining and petrochemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for Czech refineries and petrochemical plants

Two refineries define the sector: Litvínov, which shares the Záluží complex with the steam cracker and polymer units, and Kralupy nad Vltavou. Both belong to Orlen Unipetrol and are described on the Orlen Unipetrol page. Beside the Kralupy refinery Synthos operates a synthetic rubber site; the company announced that its emulsion styrene-butadiene rubber line there stopped in the second quarter of 2023, and says that butadiene rubber production at Kralupy continues. At Pardubice, Paramo produces asphalt products, process oils and lubricants, and is owned by the Unipetrol group. What matters for a handset is the mix of hazards. Hydrocarbon vapours and hydrogen in the refineries; ethylene in the cracker; butadiene, a flammable gas, at a rubber plant; hot bitumen and process oil at Pardubice, where the hazard is more about flash point and temperature class than about gas group. The point to take from that is that a single device standard has to be cleared against each site's own drawings.

What a landlocked refining sector asks of a handset

Without a port, supply is constrained by pipeline capacity, and that makes planned shutdowns carefully timed. The practical requirements follow from that.

Planned shutdowns, short and busy

When a unit comes down the site fills with contractors for a few weeks. A handset has to be enrolled, restricted and wiped quickly, and Android Enterprise is the mechanism for that.

Gas group IIC in one place, IIA in another

The target of II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 covers hydrogen areas and the lower groups. The certificate states the exact scope, and each operator's drawings decide where it applies.

Dust beside gas

Polymer powder, rubber crumb and similar materials are classified by the operator as dust zones. Zone 21 is part of the same design target.

Czech documents

Instructions and the declaration of conformity are being prepared in Czech alongside English, which an operator's HSE file will want.

A common device across neighbours

Where sites sit next to each other, a common standard simplifies fleet management, although each operator reviews it separately.

Questions · Refining and petrochemicals

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for Czech refineries yet?

No. It is pre-launch and ATEX certification is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is a refinery handset also fit for a rubber plant?

The hazards differ. Butadiene and the polymer finishing areas have their own classification, so the zone and gas group should be confirmed from that plant's drawings.

Does being landlocked change the hazardous areas?

It changes where crude arrives from, not how the plants are classified. The process units are classified on the same basis as any other refinery.

Can one approval cover Litvínov, Kralupy and Pardubice?

No. Each site is reviewed separately even where the owner is the same group.
