Why it fits

Designed for chlorine chemistry, not a generic gas hazard

Chlorine's toxicity, alongside caprolactam's own solvent and process chemistry, shapes Spolana's site safety procedures in ways a general hydrocarbon Zone 1 gas rating doesn't fully capture.

Chlorine toxicity as a distinct site consideration

Beyond any flammability hazard, chlorine's toxicity means Spolana runs its own gas-detection and emergency-response protocols, and equipment carried into affected process areas needs to work alongside those procedures rather than only meeting a general Ex rating.

Zone 1/2 gas at PVC and caprolactam synthesis

PVC production and caprolactam synthesis involve process gas and solvent handling classified as Zone 1 or Zone 2 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the relevant gas groups depending on the specific chemistry.

A historically significant, long-operating industrial site

Spolana's Neratovice operations have a long industrial history, meaning classified-area documentation spans multiple eras of plant construction and modification.

Caprolactam production connects to nylon fibre manufacturing

Caprolactam is a precursor for nylon fibre production, meaning Spolana's output feeds into a downstream textile and materials supply chain distinct from the fuels and plastics produced at Czech refining sites.

Fleet management for a specialised chemical site

EMM and MDM enrolment suits managing devices across Spolana's chlorine, PVC and caprolactam production areas from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Spolana yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and ATEX certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does chlorine toxicity change the Ex certification requirement?

Ex certification addresses the flammable-gas classification; chlorine toxicity is managed through Spolana's own separate gas-detection and emergency-response protocols.

Is caprolactam production the same hazard as PVC production?

Both fall broadly under Zone 1/2 gas-hazard classification, but the specific solvents and process chemistries differ; the certificate will state its exact scope once issued.

Is Spolana's process comparable to Deza's coal-tar processing?

No, both are specialised chemical processes but with entirely different chemistries; each runs its own separate procurement process.
