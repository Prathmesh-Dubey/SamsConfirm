The sites · Romgaz

Where gas production is classified

Romania’s gas history starts in Transylvania. The first deposit was found at Sărmășel in Mureș county in 1909, and the basin around Mediaș and Târgu Mureș is still the centre of Romgaz production. A producing gas field of this kind is a long chain of small classified places: wellheads, separators, drying and dehydration stations, compressor stations and metering points, joined by flowlines and visited on a route. The gas is predominantly methane, so the gas group is normally IIA with a T1 temperature class, which is the least demanding case for ignition energy but not for the volume that can be released from a pressurised system. Alongside production, gas is stored in depleted fields at Bilciurești, Urziceni, Bălăceanca, Ghercești and Sărmășel by the storage operator Depogaz, and Romgaz also generates power at Iernut. With the 2027 first-gas target for Neptun Deep, offshore and landfall work joins the picture.

Classified situations

Wellheads
Zone 1 or Zone 2 around valves, sampling points and pressure relief outlets.
Dehydration units
Glycol and gas drying stations; vent and flare areas classified separately.
Compressor stations
Gas-engine or turbine halls with ventilation and gas detection as the primary control.
Metering and pressure reduction
Cabinets and runs where regulators vent small volumes of gas.
Condensate handling
Light liquids at tanks and loading points, gas group IIA or IIB.
Storage injection wells
High pressure wellhead areas with periodic maintenance campaigns.

What an explosion proof mobile phone has to do for a Romanian gas producer

Few staff at a gas field work in groups. These are the design points that follow from that.

One person, many sites

A producing area can be covered by a single operator who drives between sites. Four-constellation GNSS and a 4G LTE link let a supervisor see where that person is, and the same fix is stamped on each inspection photograph taken with the 50 MP camera.

A shift without a charger

There is rarely a safe-area power point at a wellsite or a remote metering station. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified to carry a full round with the work-order application running, rather than most of one.

Mud, rain and snow

Transylvanian winters and spring thaw leave wellsites wet and muddy. IP68 sealing and rugged construction are meant to survive wet ground and a hose-down at the end of the round.

Methane, hydrogen and the marking

For methane the IIC target is more than is strictly needed, which means one handset can be issued across the estate. The certificate’s gas group and T4 class are what the explosion protection document will quote.

Managed like any other phone

Android Enterprise enrolment lets a gas producer apply the same policy, application list and remote wipe the estate already uses, and Romanian is available in the interface.

Questions · Romgaz

Frequently asked questions

Is methane an easier case for an explosion proof phone?

In terms of ignition energy, yes: methane is gas group IIA. But the area is still classified, and the equipment must still be certified for it. A IIC-rated handset covers IIA and IIB areas too, which is why it can be issued across a mixed estate.

Who would decide whether the handset is acceptable at a Romgaz site?

The operator’s own HSE, instrumentation and procurement teams, working from the certificate and the explosion protection document for that area. We do not claim any approval, programme or relationship with Romgaz.

Does it work at a remote wellsite with weak coverage?

It supports 4G LTE and Wi-Fi, but coverage is a network question that no handset can fix. The practical answer is to survey the sites with the operator’s own network before a trial.

Is the device certified now?

No. Certification to ATEX 2014/34/EU, IECEx and NEC 500 is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch, and nothing will be supplied into a classified area before the certificate exists.
