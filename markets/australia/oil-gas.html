Why it fits oil and gas

Built for LNG-export-scale offshore and onshore gas operations

Few countries run as many major LNG projects simultaneously as Australia, each operated by a different international company bringing its own engineering culture to a broadly similar Zone 1 gas-hazard environment.

Zone 1 gas across onshore trains and offshore platforms

LNG liquefaction trains, offshore platforms and remote gas-processing hubs are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety covers the hydrocarbon gas groups common across Australia's major projects.

A genuinely international mix of operators

Woodside, Chevron, INPEX and Shell each bring a different home-market engineering culture to their Australian operations, meaning device documentation needs to be recognisable across American, Japanese, Anglo-Dutch and Australian conventions rather than one national standard.

Remote coastal and island operating locations

Barrow Island, the Karratha and Darwin coastal facilities, and offshore platforms in the Browse and Bonaparte basins sit far from major population centres, favouring rugged, long-battery-life equipment for crews without same-day resupply access.

Coal seam gas alongside conventional and LNG operations

Queensland's coal seam gas fields feeding Curtis Island's LNG plants add an onshore, distributed gas-gathering pattern distinct from the concentrated offshore and coastal LNG operations in Western Australia and the Northern Territory.

Fleet management across a geographically vast sector

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices across onshore, offshore and remote coastal assets from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil and gas use in Australia yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device suit remote coastal and island locations like Barrow Island?

Ruggedness and battery life are specified with that isolation in mind, though site-specific access and quarantine protocols, such as those on Barrow Island, should always be confirmed independently.

Does one device cover both offshore platforms and onshore LNG trains?

Yes, the Zone 1, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is designed to cover both, so an operator with mixed offshore and onshore assets does not need separate devices.

Do all the major LNG operators in Australia use the same vendor-approval process?

No, each international operator runs its own engineering review reflecting its home-market standards, even where all reference the same underlying IECEx certification.
