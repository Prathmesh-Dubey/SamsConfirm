Why it fits marine and offshore

Built for the logistics layer connecting Australia's resource projects to the world

Supply bases, export wharves and support vessels sit at the interface between onshore processing and offshore production, carrying their own classified-area and marine-environment considerations.

Zone 1/2 gas at fuel bunkering and loading operations

LNG and fuel bunkering, tanker loading and marine fuel-transfer areas at export wharves and supply bases are classified Zone 1 or Zone 2 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups involved.

Offshore support vessel operations

Vessels supplying and supporting offshore platforms operate in a marine environment with their own onboard classified areas around fuel and cargo systems, in addition to the exposure of open-sea operation.

A logistics bridge between mining and LNG operations

Iron ore and coal export wharves and LNG marine terminals both fall under this sector's broad remit, meaning device standardisation here can span bulk mineral loading dust hazards as well as LNG and fuel gas hazards depending on the specific facility.

Remote coastal and offshore basing

Supply bases supporting Australia's offshore projects are often located in remote coastal towns far from major cities, extending the same resupply and ruggedness considerations relevant to the offshore platforms they serve.

Fleet management across bases, wharves and vessels

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator or logistics provider managing devices across supply bases, export wharves and support vessels from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for marine and offshore use in Australia yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device suit offshore support vessel operations?

Environmental testing and IP68 sealing address marine exposure generally; vessel-specific classified-area requirements should always be confirmed with the vessel operator.

Does this sector overlap with the mining and oil and gas pages on this site?

Yes, marine and offshore facilities serve both sectors, so equipment used here may need to satisfy requirements referenced on either the mining or oil and gas pages depending on the specific cargo or fuel type handled.

Does the device suit remote coastal supply base locations?

Ruggedness and battery life are specified with that kind of isolation in mind, consistent with the broader remoteness of Australia's resource sector.
