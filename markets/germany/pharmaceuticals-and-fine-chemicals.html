Why it fits pharmaceuticals and fine chemicals

Built for combined solvent and powder handling risk

Active pharmaceutical ingredient production typically moves between solvent-charged reaction vessels and powder drying and milling stages, meaning a single production line can carry both gas and dust classified-area requirements.

Zone 1 gas in solvent handling areas

Solvent charging, reaction and recovery areas in active pharmaceutical ingredient production at Merck's Darmstadt site are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the solvent vapour groups typical of that production.

Zone 21/22 dust atmospheres in powder processing

Milling, drying and powder handling stages downstream of solvent reactions generate combustible dust atmospheres, and Ex tb IIIC T135°C dust protection applies where a purely gas-rated device would not.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Germany's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives across the pharmaceutical and fine chemical sector.

Silicon and specialty chemistry alongside pharmaceutical production

Wacker Chemie's Burghausen site adds silicon and silicone production to the sector's classified-area profile, a different process chemistry from solvent-based pharmaceutical manufacturing but one that shares the same dual gas-and-dust equipment need.

Questions · Pharmaceuticals and fine chemicals

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for pharmaceutical use in Germany yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does one device need to cover both solvent and powder areas in the same plant?

Yes, active ingredient production commonly moves material between solvent-based and powder-based stages, which is why a dual gas-and-dust rating matters for this sector specifically.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for German pharmaceutical operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Does silicon chemistry at Wacker carry the same classified-area profile as pharmaceutical solvent handling?

The underlying chemistry differs, but both generate a mix of gas and dust classified areas that the same dual-rated device is designed to address.
