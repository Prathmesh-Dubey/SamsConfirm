Explosion proof mobile phone for fermentation, enzymes and biogas in Denmark

An explosion proof mobile phone for fermentation, enzymes and biogas in Denmark is a different proposition from an oil and gas handset. The hazard is biological: powders and solvents in the pharmaceutical and enzyme plants at Kalundborg, and methane at biogas plants that turn farm and food by-products into biomethane.

The sites · Kalundborg and the biogas network

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is needed in Danish fermentation and biogas

Kalundborg is the centre of gravity. Novo Nordisk has made insulin there since 1969 and describes the site as supplying around half of the world’s insulin. Novonesis, formed from Novozymes and Chr. Hansen in 2024, runs one of the largest enzyme operations anywhere on the same fjord. Both are partners in the Kalundborg Symbiosis, a network of public and private companies in which one company’s residue becomes another’s input. That is also where the biogas starts. The Bigadan plant at Kalundborg, inaugurated in 2018 as a joint project with Ørsted, takes about 300,000 tonnes a year of process by-products from Novo Nordisk and Novozymes and upgrades the gas to biomethane. Elsewhere in Denmark, smaller upgrading and grid-injection plants, including those on Lolland, run on farm and food waste, and pharmaceutical sites such as Hillerød and Bagsværd add solvent and powder handling on Zealand.

Where classification applies

Powder dispensing and drying
Combustible dust areas, typically Zone 21 or 22, in rooms that look like ordinary production space.
Solvent handling
Gas atmospheres where ethanol or other solvents are used in purification, dependent on the process.
Digesters and gas handling
Methane and associated gases around digester tops, gas treatment and upgrading skids.
Grid injection stations
Small, often unmanned compounds visited by one technician.

What fermentation and biogas sites do to a handset

Neither sector looks like a hazardous area from outside, which is exactly why equipment discipline matters. These are the points a Danish evaluation usually tests.

One device for dust and gas

A Kalundborg contractor can work in a powder room in the morning and beside an upgrading skid in the afternoon. The design target covers Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust under one programme, so the same handset does not have to be swapped at the airlock; the dust marking and surface temperature are stated on the certificate once it is issued.

Wash-down, gowning and cleaning regimes

Production areas are cleaned often and some sit under strict hygiene rules. IP68 sealing is aimed at wet cleaning and damp halls; check separately what your own cleaning chemistry does to a casing and a screen.

A photograph that stays with the batch

The 50 MP camera allows an operator to attach a clear image to a deviation or a maintenance task, and MDM lets a quality or HSE team decide where those images may go.

Lone working at remote plants

Four-constellation GNSS gives a position fix, which helps when a single technician is working a route of small plants and a colleague is a drive away.

Danish on the device and in the file

Android 16 carries a Danish interface, and the manual and safe-use instructions are planned in Danish alongside English, which matters where contractors rotate through quickly.

Use cases · Fermentation and biogas

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work.

Pharma01

Powder and solvent rooms in one building

Adjacent rooms can hold solvent handling, drying and powder dispensing. One handset valid for both gas and dust removes the habit of leaving a phone at one airlock and picking up another at the next.

Enzymes02

Dust-area rounds and shutdown work

Enzyme granulate and powder handling puts combustible dust in drying, milling and packing areas. A maintenance technician photographs the fault, logs the reading and closes the job in the system without returning to a desk.

Biogas03

A single technician across several plants

Upgrading and grid-injection plants run with very small crews. The handset has to hold a data link outdoors, show a position and let one person document a fault properly the first time.

How the work is organised

Campaign shutdowns, contractors and scattered plants

Work is organised around production campaigns and cleaning cycles at the large sites, and around routes at the small ones.

At the Kalundborg factories, maintenance and construction are carried out by contractor crews working to the site’s permit system, often in short shutdown windows between production campaigns. Those crews are the people most likely to need a managed handset for a few weeks. At the biogas plants the pattern is the reverse: little shift work, a technician visiting on a route, and an operator who may have several plants of different ages and owners.

Danish equipment rules are those of the EU: ATEX for the product, and the employer’s duty to assess explosion risk and hold an explosion protection document under Arbejdstilsynet. We have no verified detail of any individual company’s supplier qualification here, so ask the operator’s procurement and HSE teams what they require, including whether they expect an Achilles JQS profile, and whether approval at one plant carries to the next.

Questions · Fermentation, enzymes and biogas

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for Danish fermentation or biogas plants yet?

No. It is pre-launch, and ATEX and IECEx certification are in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. Evaluation in a classified area waits for the certificate covering it.

Do fermentation and biogas sites need different ratings?

Often yes, because powder rooms are dust zones and digester areas are gas zones, so the selection reads two parts of the classification. The EX-04Z1 programme targets both Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust, but the drawing for each room decides which applies.

Can it be used as a methane or hydrogen sulphide alarm at a biogas plant?

No. It is a communications device. Personal gas detection stays separate, and hydrogen sulphide in raw biogas is a toxic-gas question, not an ignition one.

Does approval at one plant extend to the others in a group?

Not by default. Each plant normally runs its own site procurement, even under common ownership, so confirm the buying and approval route for each location.
