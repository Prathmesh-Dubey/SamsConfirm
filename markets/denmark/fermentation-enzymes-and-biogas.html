Why it fits fermentation, enzymes and biogas

Built for dust and biogas atmospheres spread across many smaller sites

Unlike the concentrated hydrocarbon risk of a refinery, Danish fermentation and biogas hazards are distributed across a large pharmaceutical campus and a country-wide scatter of smaller biomethane plants, a pattern that favours a device that travels well between sites.

Zone 21/22 dust atmospheres in fermentation and drying areas

Powder handling in insulin and enzyme production at Kalundborg creates combustible dust atmospheres, and Ex tb IIIC T135°C dust protection applies where gas-rated equipment alone would not be sufficient.

Zone 1/2 biogas atmospheres at upgrading plants

Biomethane upgrading at Bigadan and similar plants around the country generates Zone 1 and Zone 2 methane atmospheres from digester and gas-handling equipment, a gas group the same Ex ib IIC T4 rating covers.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Denmark's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives across both the pharmaceutical and biogas scopes.

A concentrated pharmaceutical cluster at Kalundborg

Novo Nordisk and Novonesis both run large-scale production at Kalundborg, meaning a single geographic cluster carries a disproportionate share of the country's fermentation classified-area equipment need.

Fleet management across a distributed biogas plant network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices consistently across a nationwide scatter of biogas plants rather than a single large site.

Questions · Fermentation, enzymes and biogas

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use in Danish fermentation or biogas plants yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Do fermentation and biogas sites need the same certification?

Fermentation drying areas are primarily dust-rated Zone 21/22 atmospheres, while biogas upgrading is a gas-rated Zone 1/2 atmosphere, so the two scopes rely on different parts of the same dual gas-and-dust rating.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for these operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Does one biogas plant's approval extend to others in the same network?

No, each plant in a distributed network typically runs its own site-level procurement even under common ownership.
