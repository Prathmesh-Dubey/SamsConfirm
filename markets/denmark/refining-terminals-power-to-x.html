Why it fits refining, terminals and Power-to-X

Built for a refining site turning into an energy transition hub

Fredericia is unusual in combining an operating refinery, harbour tank farms and a large-scale electrolysis build on adjoining land, which means classified-area equipment needs to work across a conventional hydrocarbon site and an emerging hydrogen one at the same time.

Zone 1 and Zone 2 across refinery and tank farm areas

Process units, loading racks and tank farm dike areas at Fredericia carry a mix of Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas classifications, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups present across both.

Hydrogen atmospheres arriving alongside conventional fuel handling

The Power-to-X electrolysis project under construction beside the refinery introduces hydrogen-specific classified areas, a gas group consideration that a purely conventional refining site does not carry.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Denmark's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives, and that remains the primary certification reference even as the site's hydrogen scope grows.

A single supply-critical refinery with no domestic backup

Crossbridge Energy's Fredericia refinery is the country's dominant liquid fuel source, so operational continuity considerations at this one site carry more national weight than at a refinery competing with several domestic peers.

Fleet management across refinery, terminal and electrolysis contractors

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator or its EPC contractors managing devices consistently as the site's Power-to-X buildout proceeds alongside ongoing refinery operations.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Fredericia yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the hydrogen project need separate certification from the refinery?

Hydrogen classified areas involve a different gas group consideration than conventional hydrocarbons, so equipment intended for both scopes needs a certification basis that covers each.

Is ATEX the only certification needed at this site?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Does approval with the refinery operator extend to the electrolysis project next door?

No, Crossbridge Energy and Everfuel are separate companies and each runs its own procurement process even though the sites are adjacent.
