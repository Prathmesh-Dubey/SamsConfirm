Why it fits mining

Built for high-altitude Andean copper mining under diverse ownership

Peru's major copper mines sit among the highest-altitude large-scale mining operations in the world, run by international operators who each bring their own engineering standards on top of Peruvian regulation.

Zone 21/22 dust rating at concentrator plants

Copper and copper-zinc ore crushing and concentration generate combustible dust, and the EX-04Z1's Zone 21 / Zone 22 target addresses these processing stages across Peru's major mines.

Extreme altitude across multiple major sites

Antamina and several southern copper operations run above 4,000 metres, altitudes that affect battery performance and human physiology alike, a factor built into the device's environmental testing and long-life battery specification.

A mix of international ownership structures

Peru's copper sector includes a Mexican-owned major, a four-party international joint venture, a US-headquartered operator and a Chinese-owned operator, meaning device documentation needs to be recognisable across several distinct corporate engineering cultures rather than one national standard.

Remote highland logistics and social-license considerations

Several sites, particularly in Apurímac, are far from coastal infrastructure and depend on long overland concentrate-transport corridors that have periodically faced community access disputes, underlining how much a mine's device supply chain depends on logistics resilience, not just the mine gate.

Fleet management across geographically dispersed high-altitude sites

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices across multiple Andean sites from one console.

Questions · Mining

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for mining use in Peru yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and the IECEx dust-rating scope is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does high altitude affect the device's certification?

Altitude affects environmental and battery-performance testing rather than the Ex classification itself; the certificate will state its exact scope and test conditions once issued.

Do all Peru's major copper mines use the same vendor-approval process?

No, each operator runs its own procurement process shaped by its own parent company's standards, so approval at one site does not carry automatically to another.

Does the device suit remote highland sites with limited resupply access?

Ruggedness and battery life are specified with that isolation in mind, but resupply logistics should always be planned for independently of device durability.
