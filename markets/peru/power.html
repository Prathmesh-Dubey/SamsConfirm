Why it fits power generation

Built for a mining-driven grid with a large hydro share

Power infrastructure serving Peru's mining sector combines conventional gas-fired hazards with a substantial hydroelectric base, narrowing the classified-area footprint relative to an all-thermal grid.

Zone 1/2 gas at thermal plant fuel systems

Gas-fired generation plants fed by Camisea gas have fuel-supply and gas-handling areas classified Zone 1 or Zone 2, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups relevant to that fuel.

A grid built to serve remote, high-altitude mining loads

Much of Peru's generation and transmission capacity exists specifically to power mines in the high Andes, meaning maintenance crews for power infrastructure often work in the same remote, high-altitude conditions as the mining sites themselves.

Hydroelectric capacity reduces but doesn't eliminate classified-area work

Peru's large hydro share keeps a significant portion of generation outside classified-area scope entirely, while the remaining Camisea-fed gas plants keep their fuel-handling equipment squarely within it.

Transmission corridors reaching remote mine sites

Power lines and substations serving highland mines can run through similarly remote and difficult-access terrain as the mines themselves, a logistics reality that favours a rugged, self-sufficient device for maintenance crews.

Fleet management across a dispersed generation and transmission network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a generation or transmission operator managing devices across multiple plant sites feeding the mining sector.

Questions · Power

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for power generation use in Peru yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Do hydroelectric sites need an explosion proof device?

Generally no, hydro generation assets themselves are not classified hazardous areas; the relevant classified areas are at gas-fired plants' fuel-handling systems.

Does the device suit high-altitude transmission and generation infrastructure?

Environmental testing addresses the altitude conditions shared with the mining operations this grid was built to serve.

Is this sector distinct from the mining sector itself?

Often closely linked, since much of this generation and transmission capacity exists specifically to supply major mining operators.
