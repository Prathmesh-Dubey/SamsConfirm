Why it fits

Designed for a remote, logistics-constrained highland operation

Las Bambas' remoteness and its history of transport-corridor disruptions make equipment reliability and communications continuity a bigger operational factor here than at a coastal or urban-adjacent mine.

Zone 21/22 dust rating at the concentrator

Copper ore crushing and concentration at Las Bambas generate combustible dust, and the EX-04Z1's Zone 21 / Zone 22 target addresses this stage of the operation.

An unusually long and exposed concentrate-transport corridor

Las Bambas ships concentrate by road over a long corridor that has periodically faced community-related access disruptions, a logistics reality that raises the value of reliable field communications for coordinating around delays, well beyond the mine gate itself.

Remote highland isolation beyond most Peruvian mine sites

Apurímac is among the more isolated regions hosting large-scale mining in Peru, far from major coastal supply centres, where IP68 sealing, MIL-STD-810H construction and long battery life matter for crews who may not have same-day access to replacement equipment.

A relatively recent, large-scale greenfield development

Las Bambas began production more recently than several other major Peruvian mines, meaning its classified-area infrastructure is more uniformly modern rather than a mix of decades-old and newly built sections.

Chinese ownership under MMG

MMG is headquartered in Melbourne but majority-owned by China Minmetals, an ownership structure that brings its own engineering and reporting relationships into how vendor decisions get made.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Las Bambas yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and the IECEx dust-rating scope is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the transport-corridor risk affect the device's specification?

It underlines the value of reliable, rugged communications equipment for coordinating operations during logistics disruptions, though it does not change the underlying Ex certification requirements.

Does the device suit Las Bambas' remote highland location?

Ruggedness and battery life are specified with that isolation in mind, but resupply logistics should always be planned for independently of device durability.

Is Las Bambas' ownership relevant to vendor approval?

MMG's own engineering review process governs site-level equipment decisions; its ownership by China Minmetals is a corporate structure fact rather than a technical certification requirement.
