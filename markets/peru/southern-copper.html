Why it fits

Designed for a Mexican parent company's standards applied in Peru

As a Grupo México subsidiary, Southern Copper's procurement decisions can draw on standards and vendor relationships established at the parent company's Mexican operations, not solely on local Peruvian mining-sector practice.

A parent company's cross-border engineering standard

Grupo México operates mining assets in both Mexico and Peru, and equipment already recognised through the parent company's engineering review may carry more weight with Southern Copper's Peru-based teams than a device with no relationship to the wider group.

High-altitude southern Andes operations

Cuajone and Toquepala sit in the high southern Peruvian Andes, altitudes that affect battery performance and require environmental testing beyond what a lower-elevation industrial site needs.

Long-operating assets with established local relationships

Both mines have been in continuous operation for decades, meaning Southern Copper's existing local supplier and community relationships in the Moquegua and Tacna regions are already well established, a context new vendors need to work within rather than around.

A publicly listed, US exchange-traded operator

As a NYSE-listed company, Southern Copper's disclosure and governance standards add a layer of documentation rigour to vendor evaluation beyond what a privately held operator might require.

Fleet management across two major mine sites

EMM and MDM enrolment supports Southern Copper managing a consistent device profile across the Cuajone and Toquepala operations.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Southern Copper sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and the IECEx dust-rating scope is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does Grupo México's Mexican approval carry over to its Peru operations?

Not automatically, but documentation and relationships built with the parent group can smooth the path for Peru-based evaluation.

Does the device suit Cuajone and Toquepala's altitude?

Environmental testing addresses high-altitude conditions typical of the southern Peruvian Andes, though site-specific confirmation is always advisable.

Is Southern Copper the same company as Antamina or Cerro Verde?

No, all three are separate operators in Peru's copper sector with distinct ownership and their own procurement processes.
