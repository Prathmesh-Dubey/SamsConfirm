The site · Constancia

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is needed at Constancia

Hudbay owns 100 per cent of Constancia, which covers the Constancia pit and the Pampacancha satellite deposit. Construction was completed in 2014 and commercial operation began in April 2015. Ore is crushed, ground and floated at the site into a copper concentrate with molybdenum recovered in a separate circuit, and concentrate is then trucked via Yauri to the port of Matarani, about 460 km by road. For a handset, the useful way to look at Constancia is as a compact single-site operation. Hazardous areas are in the mill, the moly circuit, the reagent stores and the fuel facilities; the long haul road is a communications problem rather than a classification one. Staff live in camp, shifts run in blocks and contractors rotate through, so the people who need a device are a mix of permanent operators and visiting crews.

What Constancia does to a handset

Altitude is the first thing a visitor feels and the first thing that goes wrong with a poorly chosen device. At more than 4,000 metres, cold nights, strong ultraviolet and thin air all show up in a handset sooner than at lower sites. The EX-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 duty and is specified for harsh mining conditions; certification is in progress and these points are design intentions, not certified claims.

A single site, one device standard

With one operating asset in Peru, Hudbay can choose one handset and issue it to a whole workforce. That makes support, spares and training simpler than at a multi-site operator where several models accumulate.

Cold nights and thin air on shift

Battery capacity falls in the cold, and night rounds at this altitude are cold. The 4,000 mAh cell is specified to cover a shift, and operators will want to test it against their own winter nights before roll-out.

Dust on a dry highland mine

The concentrator and conveyors produce persistent fine dust. IP68 sealing keeps it out, and the Zone 21 design target is aimed at the areas where a dust layer or cloud is a credible ignition scenario.

Documentation for a North American reporting regime

As a company listed in Toronto and New York, Hudbay produces detailed safety reporting. Complete certificates, schedules of limits and a clear record of device models make it easier to show what was approved and when.

Rotating crews and camp life

Shifts change in blocks and visiting contractors come and go. Android Enterprise enrolment lets the IT team issue, restrict and wipe devices at the start and end of each rotation, rather than relying on handover by hand.

Frequently asked questions

Hudbay is a mid-tier miner: does that make approval quicker?

It can, because a smaller company has fewer layers between the user, the HSE team and the buyer. It does not shorten the technical requirements, and no supplier should assume it will be waved through. Ask the operator what evidence it wants.

Does the haul road to Matarani need a different handset?

No. The road is a coordination and breakdown risk rather than a classified area. A rugged handset with GNSS and robust coverage handling is useful there, but the Zone 1 and Zone 21 design target is for the plant.

How does the device handle 4,000 metres?

It is designed for harsh environments with IP68 sealing and a battery sized for a shift. Altitude, cold and UV are the conditions to test in an on-site trial, and site-specific confirmation is always advisable.

Is the EX-04Z1 available for Constancia today?

No. It is pre-launch and no certificate has been issued. Certification is in progress for a Q4 2026 launch, and any evaluation before then is based on documents and data, not use in classified areas.
