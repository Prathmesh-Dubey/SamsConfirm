The site · Kitimat

What is classified at the Kitimat terminal

LNG Canada is a joint venture of Shell (40 per cent), PETRONAS (25), PetroChina (15), Mitsubishi Corporation (15) and KOGAS (5). The first phase has two liquefaction trains with a combined capacity of 14 million tonnes a year, taking gas by pipeline from north-eastern British Columbia. The terminal is at Kitimat, on the Douglas Channel, and the site was built in Haisla territory.

The hazardous areas follow the process: inlet and gas treating where acid gas and water are removed, then liquefaction trains with refrigerant systems, LNG storage tanks, a loading jetty and flare systems. Methane is gas group IIA, but refrigerant and condensate streams can bring heavier hydrocarbons, so a handset with an IIC design target covers the range. Zone 2 is common around rotating equipment, with Zone 1 where releases are more likely, so the area drawings, not a general rule, decide.

What a coastal LNG plant asks of a handset

Wet, windy coastal weather

Kitimat has heavy rain and snow, and a jetty is open to the channel. IP68 sealing is designed for water and dust, and the certificate’s ambient range, once issued, is what an engineer checks against winter lows.

Cryogenic areas and the certificate scope

Cold-service equipment is not the same as a cold-weather device. The Ex certificate states the gas and temperature range, and any use close to cryogenic surfaces should be agreed with LNG Canada’s engineers.

Rotation and shift handover

An operations team works shifts, and a managed device hands over cleanly through the MDM, with the round list and photos staying with the asset.

Pipeline-to-ship chain

Coordination between the plant, the jetty and the carrier is by voice and text. Android 16 and the 50 MP camera let an operator report a transfer-line fault with a photograph.

Questions · LNG Canada

Frequently asked questions

Is Kitimat Canada’s first LNG export terminal?

LNG Canada describes itself as Canada’s first large-scale LNG export facility, with its first cargo loaded in 2025. Small-scale LNG plants existed before it.

Which gas group applies?

Methane is IIA, but other process fluids can be in IIB or IIC. A handset designed to IIC covers the range, though the area drawings set the requirement.

Does the Haisla territory setting affect equipment approval?

Not the Ex certification. It matters for the project’s community and procurement context, which is separate.

What about the Montney gas supply chain?

Upstream compressor stations and plants are a different set of sites. See the guide for LNG, gas and offshore in Canada.
