Why it fits oil sands and heavy oil

Built for two extraction methods and an extreme winter climate

Mining and thermal in situ extraction present different equipment environments, and both operate through Alberta winters that regularly reach temperatures few Ex-rated device specifications are tested against.

Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust depending on extraction method

Upgrading units at mining operations carry Zone 1 gas-hazard classification, while bitumen processing and coke handling can introduce combustible dust considerations, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups relevant to both extraction and upgrading processes.

CSA certification as the Canadian domestic reference

Canada's CSA (Canadian Standards Association) certification system is the domestic hazardous-area equipment standard operators generally expect, and the EX-04Z1's certification programme is targeting CSA recognition alongside IECEx.

Steam-generation infrastructure unique to thermal in situ

SAGD (steam-assisted gravity drainage) operations run extensive steam-generation facilities and well pads spread across boreal forest terrain, a distributed footprint distinct from a concentrated open-pit mine site.

Extreme cold-weather operation

Northern Alberta winters regularly bring sustained sub-zero temperatures well beyond what most global Ex-rated equipment testing anticipates, making environmental durability as significant a specification driver as the hazard classification itself.

Fleet management across mining and dispersed in situ sites

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices across both concentrated mining operations and dispersed thermal in situ well pads from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil sands use in Canada yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including CSA recognition, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is IECEx certification enough for use in Canada?

IECEx is generally recognised as supporting technical evidence, but Canadian operators typically also expect CSA-certified equipment.

Does the device suit extreme Alberta winter conditions?

Environmental testing is specified with sustained sub-zero cold in mind, and the certificate will state its exact test conditions once issued.

Does one device cover both mining and thermal in situ operations?

Yes, the design intent is a single device standard usable across both extraction methods, though each site's specific classification should be confirmed.
