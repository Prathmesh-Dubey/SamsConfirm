Austria · Oil, gas and refining

Explosion proof mobile phone for oil, gas and refining in Austria

Austria's oil and gas sector is small in volume and long in history: a refinery at Schwechat, mature fields in the Vienna basin, gas storage in Upper Austria and Salzburg, and a high-pressure transit grid centred on Baumgarten. This page explains where an explosion proof mobile phone is used across those assets in Austria and what the EX-04Z1, an intrinsically safe Zone 1 phone designed to ATEX and IECEx and still in certification, has to prove to each operator.

The sector · Austria

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is used in Austrian oil, gas and refining

Four distinct kinds of site share one sector label. Schwechat is a refinery. The Vienna basin around Matzen is an old onshore field with wells that have produced for decades. Underground storage at places such as Puchkirchen, Haidach and Aigelsbrunn takes summer gas and gives it back in winter; RAG describes more than six billion cubic metres of storage capacity across its facilities. The transit grid around Baumgarten an der March, which feeds Germany, Italy and the domestic market, runs through compressor and metering stations that are usually visited rather than permanently staffed. A handset suitable for all four has to cope with a refinery crew, a lone field technician, a storage site with wellhead and compressor areas, and a hydrogen pilot.

Classified units and areas

Refinery process units
Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas around distillation, treating and loading.
Wellheads and flowlines
Onshore wells in the Vienna basin, mostly outdoors.
Storage wellhead clusters
Injection and withdrawal wells with piping and dehydration at storage sites.
Compressor stations
Gas turbine or motor driven compressors in buildings and enclosures, ventilated and classified.
Metering and pressure regulation
Smaller stations with valves and instrumentation, often unmanned.
Hydrogen pilot facilities
Gas group IIC, which is why the device target marking includes IIC.

Alone at a valve station

Many grid and field locations are visited by one technician at a time. Four-constellation GNSS and 4G LTE give a position and a link, and a lone-worker application can be installed through the console.

Compressor noise and enclosure

Compressor halls are loud and classified. The question for the datasheet is the loudspeaker output and the microphone noise handling, which we will publish rather than promise beforehand.

Hydrogen as a new gas

Pilot work with hydrogen storage adds gas group IIC to areas that were previously natural gas only. A target marking of II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 is designed to cover that.

An old basin with mixed documents

Fields developed over decades have classification drawings of varying age. The safety officer decides each location; the handset should not depend on a particular vintage of plant.

German-language records

Documentation in German and English suits both operators and the Arbeitsinspektion, which inspects explosion protection documents.

Use cases · Oil, gas and refining

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work in the sector.

Transmission01

Compressor and valve station checks

A technician walking a station records valve positions and noise or vibration observations, photographs a leak indication and reports it from the yard. The position stamp on the record shows exactly which skid was inspected.

Storage02

Wellhead rounds at storage sites

Injection and withdrawal seasons change the workload. In the injection season, wells are checked for pressure and integrity; a handset that carries the checklist, photographs and call-out numbers removes paperwork from a round that already covers a large site.

Field03

One engineer and many Vienna basin wells

An engineer driving between small locations needs a camera, a position and a reliable call. Records are synchronised when the vehicle reaches coverage, and the work order is closed with a photograph.

What operators in the sector would ask of a handset supplier

We have not found published handset supplier programmes at OMV, Gas Connect Austria or RAG Austria and claim none. Austrian operators in this sector work to the same employer law, so the evidence requested is similar even where the routes differ. Ask each operator's procurement and HSE teams which supplier file, audit or induction applies, and whether a transmission operator and a storage operator accept the same documents.

Questions · Austrian oil and gas

Frequently asked questions

Is one handset enough for refinery, field and gas grid?

The specification is designed to cover Zone 1 gas, including IIC, so the marking is not the limit. The practical limit is that each area has its own classification and the safety officer must confirm suitability.

Does hydrogen change the answer?

It raises the gas group to IIC, and the target marking of Ex ib IIC T4 is designed to include it. The temperature class and the exact certificate scope are what to check for a given hydrogen service.

Is all of the Gas Connect Austria pipeline classified?

No. Most of the pipeline is buried and unclassified. The classified areas are stations: compressor buildings, valve and metering stations and tie-ins, which is where a handset is carried.

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for these operators?

No. Certification is in progress for a Q4 2026 launch, and we have no customer or pilot relationship with any operator named on this page.
