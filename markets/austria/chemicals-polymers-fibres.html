Why it fits chemicals, polymers and fibres

Built for a genuinely diverse chemicals and materials sector

Unlike a sector organised around one dominant process, Austria's chemicals industry spans petrochemical cracking, cellulose-based fibre production and pharmaceutical fermentation, each with distinct classified-area drivers.

Zone 1 gas at cracker and polyolefin plants

Ethylene cracking and polyolefin production at Schwechat and Linz are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that service.

Solvent-specific hazards in fibre and pharmaceutical production

Viscose fibre manufacturing and pharmaceutical fermentation and extraction processes each involve specific flammable solvents with their own vapour characteristics, requiring the device's Ex rating to be evaluated against each process's actual chemical exposure rather than treated as a generic gas hazard.

Combustible dust in fibre, sugar and starch processing

Cellulose fibre handling and AGRANA's sugar, starch and bioethanol production generate combustible organic dust, adding a Zone 21/22 dimension alongside the sector's gas and solvent hazards.

Pharmaceutical GMP requirements layered on top of Ex classification

Fermentation and finishing facilities like Sandoz's Kundl site run pharmaceutical good manufacturing practice protocols that add material and hygiene requirements beyond the underlying Ex certification.

Fleet management across a diverse chemicals and materials sector

EMM and MDM enrolment supports operators managing devices across genuinely different process types within Austria's broad chemicals sector.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for chemicals use in Austria yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does one certification cover such different processes as cracking, fibre production and pharma fermentation?

The underlying Ex classification (gas or dust atmosphere) is addressed by the certificate, but each process's specific chemical exposure should be evaluated against the certificate's exact scope once issued.

Does the device need separate approval for pharmaceutical GMP environments?

GMP and hygiene requirements are a separate consideration from Ex classification; site-specific pharmaceutical manufacturing rules should be confirmed with the operator.

Is viscose fibre production's solvent hazard covered by a standard gas rating?

The specific solvent used in viscose production has its own vapour characteristics; site HSE teams should confirm the certificate's applicability to that specific chemical exposure.
