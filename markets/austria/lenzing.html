Why it fits

Designed for a carbon disulfide process hazard, not a generic gas rating

Carbon disulfide's specific flammability and toxicity characteristics mean equipment evaluation for Lenzing's process needs to look beyond a general Zone 1 hydrocarbon gas assumption.

Carbon disulfide as the defining process hazard

The viscose xanthation process uses carbon disulfide, a solvent with a notably low ignition energy and its own toxicity profile, and equipment reaching these areas needs documentation addressing that specific chemical exposure rather than only a generic Zone 1 gas rating.

Combustible cellulose dust alongside the solvent hazard

Pulp and fibre handling generates combustible cellulose dust, adding a Zone 21/22 dimension to Lenzing's classified-area profile alongside the carbon disulfide gas hazard.

A specialised, less common industrial process

Viscose fibre production using this specific chemistry is practised at fewer sites globally than oil and gas or general chemicals processing, meaning the local ecosystem of vendors experienced with this exact hazard combination is smaller, an opening the EX-04Z1's certification programme is positioned to address directly.

Upper Austria's established industrial base

Lenzing's long-operating site in Upper Austria has well-established local supplier and community relationships that a new vendor needs to work within.

Fleet management for a specialised single-process site

EMM and MDM enrolment suits managing devices across Lenzing's fibre and pulp production areas from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Lenzing yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and ATEX certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is carbon disulfide covered by a standard Ex gas rating?

Carbon disulfide has a notably low ignition energy and specific hazard characteristics; the certificate will state its exact scope once issued, and site HSE teams should confirm applicability to this specific chemical.

Does the device need separate certification for the dust and solvent hazards?

Both fall under the broader ATEX framework, but the certificate's exact scope for each specific hazard type should be confirmed once issued.

Is Lenzing's process common elsewhere in Austria's chemicals sector?

No, viscose fibre production using this specific chemistry is a specialised process distinct from the petrochemical, pharmaceutical and steel-industry hazards covered on other pages of this site.
