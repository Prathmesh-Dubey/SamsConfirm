Why it fits

Designed for OMV's home-market engineering standard

Unlike OMV's international operations, where its Austrian ATEX heritage travels to a foreign regulatory environment, Schwechat and the Vienna basin are where that engineering culture originates and applies most directly.

ATEX as the native, not adapted, standard

OMV's Austrian operations sit squarely within the EU's ATEX framework as their primary regulatory home, meaning the EX-04Z1's ATEX-based documentation is being prepared as the direct, not cross-referenced, evidence base for evaluation here.

Nearly a century of Vienna basin field development

OMV's upstream operations in the Vienna basin date back to the 1930s, giving the company deep, long-standing local supplier and community relationships that a new vendor needs to work within.

Zone 1 gas across refining and upstream production

Schwechat's refinery process units and the Vienna basin's wellhead and gathering facilities are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of both.

A group headquarters engineering function with international reach

As OMV's corporate headquarters market, decisions and standards set here can inform the group's approach at its international operations elsewhere, giving equipment accepted at the Austrian level potential relevance beyond this market alone.

Fleet management across upstream and refining operations

EMM and MDM enrolment supports OMV managing devices consistently across the Vienna basin's fields and the Schwechat refinery.

Does approval at OMV's Austrian sites carry to its international operations?

Not automatically, but documentation accepted at OMV's headquarters level can be a useful reference point given the company's centralised engineering function.

Does the device suit the Vienna basin's older field infrastructure?

Yes, it is designed to be usable across classified areas built up over the basin's long production history.
