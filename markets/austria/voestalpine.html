The site · Linz and Donawitz

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is carried at voestalpine in Austria

An integrated works turns ore and coal into steel through coke ovens, blast furnaces, a converter steelmaking route, casting and rolling. Each stage produces or handles gas that burns. Coke-oven gas is rich in hydrogen, blast furnace gas and converter gas carry carbon monoxide, and the works collect, clean and distribute them through gas mains and holders to boilers, hot stoves and power stations. Published figures put five blast furnaces in Austria, three at Linz and two at Donawitz. The company's greentec steel programme adds an electric arc furnace at each site, with commissioning planned for 2027 at a stated investment of around 1.5 billion euros, followed by replacement of a further blast furnace at each location from 2030. For a handset that means plant which is both old and under construction.

Hydrogen-rich gas that needs an IIC device

Coke-oven gas carries a high proportion of hydrogen, which falls in gas group IIC. The EX-04Z1 marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, so the group fits; the plant's own area classification decides where it applies.

Carbon monoxide is a toxic hazard first

Blast furnace and converter gas are dangerous mostly through toxicity. A phone is not a CO detector, and personal gas monitors stay mandatory in the areas that need them.

Heat and scale near the furnace

Ambient temperature near tapholes and ladles is high, and site rules keep devices out of radiant heat zones. The ambient range on the certificate is published with the datasheet, and no figure is assumed here.

Very large sites on foot

Linz is a works of several square kilometres, and Donawitz is built along a valley. Four-constellation GNSS gives a position in the steel plant, and a battery sized for a shift is more useful than a charger point inside the zone.

Dust that accumulates quietly

Layers on structural steel and cable trays are the hazard. A handset that records a housekeeping inspection with a photograph and position helps the HSE team prove that routines are followed.

Use cases · voestalpine

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work at voestalpine.

Gas01

Gas main and holder inspection

Gas network walkers check valves, drains and condensate pots on mains that run through the works. A handset in the classified zone records which valve was checked and photographs corrosion or a leak, with a position instead of a verbal description.

Construction02

Greentec steel project work

Building new furnaces beside live gas mains requires hot work permits and strict area separation. A managed device lets contractors carry the permit, drawings and daily report in the classified area, locked to approved applications.

Outage03

Blast furnace and rolling mill outages

A planned outage brings hundreds of contractors into the works for a few weeks. Enrolled handsets carry the work order, photograph completed jobs and let the planner close tasks without waiting for the end of shift.

Questions · voestalpine

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for voestalpine's steelworks?

No. Certification is in progress for a Q4 2026 launch, and we have no relationship with voestalpine. Plant acceptance follows the works' own review.

Will the electric arc furnaces change the classification?

Not by themselves; electric furnaces are not a gas hazard in the way blast furnaces are. But the transition runs through the 2030s with blast furnaces and new plant side by side, so classification documents will change over time.

Is carbon monoxide covered by an Ex phone?

Ignition and toxicity are separate issues. The Ex marking addresses ignition. Toxic gas protection remains a matter for personal gas monitors and works rules.

Does approval at Linz carry to Donawitz?

Not automatically. The two works have separate classification documents and site rules, and a trial at one does not decide the other.
