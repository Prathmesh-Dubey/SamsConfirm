Why it fits petrochemicals

Built for refinery-integrated petrochemical complexes

Most major Indian petrochemical capacity sits alongside a refinery rather than as a standalone chemical plant, meaning classified-area equipment often needs to serve both refining and downstream petrochemical process units on the same site.

Zone 1 gas across integrated refining and petrochemical units

Cracking, aromatics and polymer production integrated with refining at sites like Jamnagar are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that combined operation.

PESO as the domestic regulatory authority

PESO governs hazardous-area equipment approval for Indian petrochemical sites domestically, alongside the ATEX and IECEx bases used for international reference.

A dense chemical belt concentrated in one state

The Gujarat chemical belt around Jamnagar, Dahej and Vadodara concentrates a large share of national petrochemical capacity into one geographic cluster, unlike a country where such production spreads more evenly across regions.

EPC and turnaround contractors moving between integrated sites

L&T, EIL and Tata Projects carry out construction and turnaround work across multiple integrated refining-petrochemical complexes, needing equipment approved for more than one site owner's rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for petrochemical use in India yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including PESO and ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does integration with a refinery change the classified-area rating for a petrochemical unit?

The underlying hydrocarbon gas groups are broadly consistent across integrated sites, so the same Ex ib IIC T4 rating typically applies to both the refining and petrochemical process areas.

Is PESO required in addition to ATEX for petrochemical sites?

PESO is the domestic authority for hazardous-area equipment in India; ATEX and IECEx serve as supporting international technical evidence rather than a substitute.

Does an EPC contractor's approval at one complex carry to another site?

No, each site owner typically runs its own separate procurement and site-access process even for the same contractor.
