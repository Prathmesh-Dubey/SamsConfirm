Explosion proof mobile phone for Petrochemical in Thailand

Thailand's petrochemical industry is concentrated on the Map Ta Phut estate in Rayong, with further plants in nearby estates, and an explosion proof mobile phone for Thailand's petrochemical plants has to cover gas, vapour and polymer dust. This sector page names the operators and the plant types; company pages carry the detail. The SAMS EX-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21; it is pre-launch and certification is in progress.

Petrochemical · the site

The Map Ta Phut estate and the plants around it

Map Ta Phut is described as Thailand's biggest industrial estate, around 180 kilometres east of Bangkok in Rayong. Operators include PTT Global Chemical (olefins, aromatics and derivatives), SCG Chemicals (the ROC and MOC crackers and polyolefin plants), IRPC, HMC Polymers and Star Petroleum Refining, with gas coming from the PTT separation plants nearby. The estate has a documented history that shapes how plants approach safety. In 2009 the courts suspended dozens of projects over health impact assessment, and in May 2012 an explosion at the Bangkok Synthetics butadiene plant killed 12 people, reported as occurring while toluene was being put into a tank. Plant teams there know what a vapour release can do.

Gas and dust in the same plant

The EX-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust, so one handset covers a route from reactor to bagging.

Group IIC across the estate

Hydrogen and ethylene mean IIB is not enough. The marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4.

Wet and salty

Rayong is humid all year, coastal, and takes the southwest monsoon from May. IP68 sealing and a rugged build are the design floor.

Thai and English crews

One Android 16 build carries both languages for a mixed Thai and expatriate workforce, so safety prompts and the app list read the same for everyone on a crew, from the operator to the visiting specialist.

Petrochemical · working and approvals

How work is organised across the estate, and what operators would ask for

Plants share the estate fence, and contractors move between them.

Overlapping shutdowns

Cracker and polymer stops bring contractor crews to several plants in the same months. A device enrolled to one operator should be re-enrolled, not reused blindly, for the next.

Two gates

IEAT sets the estate access and each operator sets its own. A handset has to satisfy both.

Vendor and equipment approval

We have not verified a published petrochemical sector approval scheme for portable devices, so we do not describe one. Ask each operator's procurement and HSE team for the steps and documents expected with an Ex certificate.

National file

TISI, the Department of Industrial Works and NBTC apply on top of the IECEx evidence. All are in progress.

Questions · Petrochemical

Frequently asked questions

Can petrochemical plants buy an explosion proof mobile phone from SAMS today?

No. The EX-04Z1 is pre-launch with a Q4 2026 launch, and IECEx, ATEX and NEC 500 certification are in progress. The datasheet and certification schedule are available now.

Why does this page mention the 2012 explosion?

It is a documented event on the estate and plant teams reference it. We cite it from published news reports only to explain why toluene and tank transfer are taken seriously.

Is one handset enough for gas and dust?

The design target is Zone 1 and Zone 21, but the area classification drawings for each plant decide which zone applies where. Contractors moving between plants should confirm the zone at each plant rather than assume the last one applies.

Does the camera matter in a petrochemical plant?

Yes, mostly as a controlled tool. Plants restrict photography near process units, so the sensible setup is a camera that the management console enables for inspection work and disables elsewhere, per site and per contractor group.
