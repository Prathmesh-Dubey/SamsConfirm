Built for Peruvian conditions

Peru runs classified-area operations across three very different environments — high-altitude desert mining, Amazon rainforest gas fields and coastal refining. These are the points a Peruvian evaluator presses on, and how the design answers them.

High-altitude mineral dust

Copper concentrator operations above 3,000 metres generate persistent fine dust in thin, dry air. The device is designed for Zone 21 as well as Zone 1, and IP68 sealing is what keeps it working through a full shift.

Amazon humidity at Camisea

Camisea’s gas fields sit in tropical rainforest, the opposite environment from the southern mining belt. MIL-STD-810H construction is specified for both extremes rather than one.

Altitude and UV exposure

Many mine sites sit well above 4,000 metres with intense UV and large temperature swings. A display built for outdoor use and rugged sealing are specified with that in mind.

A battery sized for remote shifts

Distances at an open-pit mine or a jungle gas plant are real, and there is no recharging point inside a classified area. The 4,000 mAh cell is specified for a full shift.

Spanish and English on one build

Peruvian operations run with local and international technical staff. Android 16 carries both interface languages on the same handset.

Regulatory route · Peru

What your HSE and procurement teams will ask for.

Conformity in Peru runs through the Instituto Nacional de Calidad (INACAL) and is overseen for the energy and mining sector by OSINERGMIN, the Organismo Supervisor de la Inversión en Energía y Minería. For hazardous-area equipment the technical evidence behind that conformity is an IECEx Certificate of Conformity, and because this product is a phone it also needs MTC (Ministerio de Transportes y Comunicaciones) type approval for the radio. None of the three is finished yet.

Getting past the mine or plant gate is a separate exercise. Southern Copper, Antamina and the other major operators each run their own vendor-registration and engineering-approval process for equipment entering a classified area, and supply is normally expected to run through a Peruvian-registered partner. A device can be fully INACAL-cleared and still be unlisted on an operator’s own equipment register, so plan for two timelines rather than one.

How to buy · Peru

How to evaluate and buy in Peru

The EX-04Z1 launches in Q4 2026. None of these steps needs a purchase order, and they are ordered so that your approvals and our certification run in parallel.

  1. 01

    Send us the area classification

    Give us the zone, gas or dust group and temperature class for the areas a device would enter, plus the operator whose approval you work under. That is enough for us to say plainly where certification stands against your requirement.

  2. 02

    Take the datasheet and the certificate schedule

    You receive the full specification and a written list of the certifications in progress with target dates. An HSE officer can start an equipment selection justification with it, and procurement can open a vendor file.

  3. 03

    Join the evaluation programme

    Evaluation units are going to Peruvian operators and contractors for handling and MDM trials in safe areas, moving to classified-area pilots once the certificate exists.

  4. 04

    Supply through a Peruvian partner

    Vendor-registration processes at the major mining and gas operators favour a locally registered supplier who can hold the INACAL documentation and carry spares. Evaluation quantities can be supplied directly while that channel is appointed.

Ask about Peru →
Can we buy an explosion proof mobile phone from SAMS in Peru today?

Not yet. The EX-04Z1 is pre-launch and every certification behind it, including IECEx, ATEX and the INACAL conformity route, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. What exists today is the datasheet, the certification schedule and a place in the evaluation programme.

Does it cover both mining dust and gas-field hazards?

Yes. The Zone 1 / Zone 21, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is designed to cover the combustible dust found in copper concentrator operations as well as the gas atmospheres at sites like Camisea.

What is the price of an explosion proof phone in Peru?

We do not publish one. Until certification completes there is no list price, and pre-launch pricing is available on request against a stated quantity and site.

Will it handle high-altitude mining conditions?

It is designed to, with MIL-STD-810H testing and IP68 sealing behind it. The certificate’s temperature class is the number to check against your specific site conditions.

Will Southern Copper or Antamina accept it on site?

That decision belongs to each operator and follows the certificates rather than preceding them. The IECEx certificate is the evidence their engineering review is built on.

Does it work in Spanish?

Yes. Android 16 runs the interface in Spanish or English on the same handset and the user manual will be issued in both.
