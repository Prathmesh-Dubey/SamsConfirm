Why it fits oil, gas and refining

Built for four large refiners with four different ownership models

Unlike markets dominated by one national oil company, South Korea's four major refiners each bring a distinct corporate ownership structure to their equipment decisions.

Zone 1 gas at world-scale refinery complexes

Crude distillation, catalytic units and tank farms at Korea's major refineries are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of refining service at this scale.

KOSHA certification alongside IECEx

Korea's Occupational Safety and Health Agency (KOSHA) administers domestic explosion-protection certification requirements, and Korean operators generally expect to see KOSHA recognition addressed directly rather than assuming an international certificate alone is sufficient.

Four distinct ownership structures in one sector

SK Energy sits within the SK Group chaebol, GS Caltex is a Chevron joint venture, S-Oil is majority Saudi Aramco-owned, and Hyundai Oilbank sits within the HD Hyundai group, meaning vendor documentation needs to be recognisable across genuinely different corporate engineering cultures.

Some of the largest single-site refineries in Asia

Korea's major refineries rank among the largest in daily throughput anywhere in the region, meaning turnaround periods bring very large temporary contractor populations through a single site.

Fleet management across large, high-throughput complexes

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a refiner managing devices across a large single-site complex or, for a group like HD Hyundai, across related refining and shipbuilding operations.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil and gas use in South Korea yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx and KOSHA recognition, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is IECEx certification sufficient for use in Korea?

IECEx is generally recognised as supporting technical evidence, but Korean operators typically also expect KOSHA-recognised domestic certification.

Do Korea's four refiners share a common vendor-approval process?

No, each is a separately owned and managed company with its own procurement process, even though all four operate at similarly large scale.

Does the device suit Korea's world-scale refinery turnarounds?

Yes, the EMM and MDM fleet management features are built to scale to the large contractor populations typical of major refinery turnarounds.
