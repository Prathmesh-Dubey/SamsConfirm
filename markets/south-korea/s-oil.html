Why it fits

Designed for a Saudi Aramco-affiliated Korean refiner

Aramco's majority ownership of S-Oil means the refiner's engineering expectations can draw on Aramco's own global downstream standards, not only Korean domestic practice.

Aramco's own engineering review as a global downstream investor

As Aramco's largest downstream investment outside Saudi Arabia, S-Oil's equipment decisions may be shaped by standards Aramco applies across its international downstream portfolio, distinct from how Aramco's fully domestic Saudi vendor-approval process works for wellhead and refinery equipment inside the Kingdom.

Zone 1 gas at a large integrated refining and petrochemical site

Onsan's crude distillation, catalytic units and associated petrochemical facilities are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that service.

A Korean operating company with Saudi ownership

S-Oil's day-to-day operations remain run as a Korean company subject to KOSHA and other domestic regulatory requirements, even as its ownership structure brings Aramco's global engineering culture into its investment oversight.

A shared industrial cluster near other Ulsan-area operators

Onsan sits close to SK Energy's Ulsan refinery and Hyundai Heavy Industries' shipyards, meaning contractors and inspectors may move between neighbouring operators' sites within the same region.

Fleet management for a large integrated Korean site

EMM and MDM enrolment supports S-Oil managing devices across its Onsan refining and petrochemical operations from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 approved for use at S-Oil yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and no approval exists ahead of the underlying IECEx and KOSHA-recognised certification being completed.

Does S-Oil follow the same vendor-approval process as Saudi Aramco's domestic operations?

Not directly. S-Oil operates as a Korean company under domestic regulatory requirements; Aramco's ownership informs investment-level standards rather than replacing Korea's own regulatory framework.

Is S-Oil the same as SK Energy or GS Caltex?

No, all three operate in the Ulsan area but are separately owned and managed companies with distinct procurement processes.

Does Aramco ownership speed up or complicate vendor evaluation?

It can mean documentation prepared to a standard Aramco recognises elsewhere is a useful reference point, though S-Oil's own Korean engineering team runs the actual site-level evaluation.
