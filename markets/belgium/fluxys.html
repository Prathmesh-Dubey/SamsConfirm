The site · Fluxys

What is classified at Zeebrugge and along the network

The Zeebrugge terminal sits on about 30 hectares and, according to public technical descriptions, has reception facilities, LNG storage tanks, vaporisation and send-out plant, and a second jetty built to receive carriers from small vessels of about 2,000 cubic metres up to large ones of 217,000. Public sources also describe a fifth, larger storage tank. LNG is stored at around minus 162 degrees Celsius, so the hazard picture is methane gas in air at the jetty, around vaporisers and boil-off compressors, with cold surfaces and vapour clouds as the working conditions. The existing page also mentions ship-to-ship transfer and small-scale bunkering, which add transfer hoses and moving vessels to the picture.

The transmission network is the other half. Fluxys Belgium operates the national grid, and its compressor and metering stations are Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas areas that are often unmanned, visited by a small crew. Gas is not a single gas group: methane is group IIA, but hydrogen blending trials and odorant or condensate areas could introduce others, which is why the design target remains group IIC.

What an LNG terminal and a gas grid do to a explosion proof mobile phone in Belgium

Cold and wet, not dusty

A jetty in the North Sea means driven rain, salt spray and cold mornings, while cryogenic transfer areas have cold surfaces. IP68 sealing and rugged construction are aimed at that duty. Exact operating temperature limits belong on the datasheet, which we will supply on request.

Remote stations and lone working

A metering station is a small plot visited by one crew, often out of coverage. GNSS gives a located record of the visit, and the camera records valve and instrument positions. Lone worker procedures on site will determine what monitoring is added.

Vessel schedules drive the day

Cargo operations run to arrival and departure times. A single handset for calls, photographs and checklists means that the jetty operator does not carry a radio, a camera and a clipboard.

One fleet, two kinds of site

The terminal crew and the pipeline crew use the same Zone 1 design. Fluxys would enrol both from one MDM console and set different application and camera policies for each.

What a gas infrastructure operator will ask for

We do not claim any relationship with Fluxys. Gas transmission operators are regulated and cautious about equipment on their sites, and Fluxys would set its own requirements. Generally, expect to be asked for the ATEX certificate, the CE marking, the declaration of conformity, the gas group and temperature class, and Dutch-language instructions. Contractors are commonly asked for SCC or VCA safety certification. Ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams for current requirements before any trial. Certification of the EX-04Z1 to ATEX 2014/34/EU is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Questions · Fluxys

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Fluxys sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and ATEX certification is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. No unit will be supplied for a classified area before the certificate covering it exists.

Is Zeebrugge mainly for Belgian gas supply?

No. It is a gas hub as well as an import point, and gas passes through to other markets. That affects how busy the terminal is, not how it is classified.

Does one device cover the terminal and the pipeline stations?

The design target is Zone 1 gas, group IIC, T4, which is meant to span both. Any cryogenic-adjacent duty must be confirmed against the certificate and the datasheet when issued, since this is not the same as being used on a cold surface.

How does this page relate to the port logistics page?

Fluxys is one operator in the port. Independent tank terminals at Antwerp and Zeebrugge are covered in the page on an explosion proof phone for bulk liquid storage and port logistics.
