Why it fits

Designed for a transit-and-trading hub, not a domestic-consumption terminal

Zeebrugge's role as a European gas trading gateway, rather than a terminal built primarily to feed one country's own demand, shapes both its classified-area equipment footprint and its transaction-driven operating pattern.

Zone 1 gas across LNG and pipeline transmission infrastructure

LNG unloading, storage and regasification at Zeebrugge, alongside compressor and metering stations across the national transmission network, are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups present across that chain.

Ship-to-ship transfer and small-scale bunkering beyond standard import

Zeebrugge's role includes LNG ship-to-ship transfer and small-scale bunkering operations for LNG-fuelled vessels, adding marine-transfer classified areas beyond a standard import-and-regasify terminal's operations.

A trading hub function drives variable, market-responsive throughput

Because much of Zeebrugge's activity responds to European gas trading and transit demand rather than fixed domestic consumption, operational tempo can shift with market conditions, putting a premium on communications equipment that supports flexible scheduling.

A distributed national transmission network beyond the terminal itself

Fluxys's remit extends beyond Zeebrugge to the national gas transmission grid, a distributed footprint of compressor and metering stations across Belgium.

Fleet management across a terminal and a national network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports Fluxys managing devices consistently across the Zeebrugge terminal and the broader national transmission network from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Fluxys sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and ATEX certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is Zeebrugge mainly for Belgian domestic gas supply?

No, it functions substantially as a European trading and transit hub, moving gas onward to other markets in addition to any domestic supply role.

Does LNG bunkering need different certification from standard LNG import handling?

Bunkering and ship-to-ship transfer both fall under the same general gas-hazard classification; the certificate will state its exact scope once issued, and site-specific procedures should be confirmed with Fluxys.

Does the device cover both the Zeebrugge terminal and the national pipeline network?

Yes, the Zone 1 specification is designed to cover both, so field crews working across the transmission network do not need separate devices from those working at the terminal.
