Why it fits

Built for a US multinational's global standard, applied in an ATEX jurisdiction

ExxonMobil's group-wide procurement practices, developed across its international operations, inform equipment evaluation in Antwerp even though the site itself operates within the EU's ATEX regulatory framework.

A global engineering standard, not a purely local one

ExxonMobil applies group-wide equipment standards across its international operations, and documentation built to a standard the parent company recognises elsewhere can carry particular weight with Antwerp's engineering team, alongside the site's ATEX regulatory compliance.

Zone 1 gas across refining, coking and lubricants production

Antwerp's refinery process units, coker and lubricants production lines are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that combined process range.

A coker adds a specific processing dimension

Coking units process the heaviest refinery residues into lighter products and petroleum coke, a specific unit operation with its own equipment and classified-area considerations beyond standard crude distillation.

Riverside location on the Scheldt

Antwerp's location on the Scheldt river adds marine-adjacent logistics for feedstock and product movement, alongside the site's core refining and petrochemical operations.

Fleet management for a large integrated site within a global network

EMM and MDM enrolment supports ExxonMobil managing devices consistently at Antwerp within its broader international fleet management approach.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at ExxonMobil Antwerp yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and certification, including ATEX and IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does ExxonMobil require different documentation from a European-owned Antwerp operator?

ExxonMobil's global engineering standard, informed by its US operations, sits alongside compliance with Belgium's ATEX framework, so documentation addressing both is being prepared.

What is a coker and does it need special equipment consideration?

A coker processes heavy refinery residues into lighter products; it is a specific unit operation within the broader Zone 1 refinery classification, and the certificate will state its exact scope once issued.

Would approval at ExxonMobil Antwerp carry to other ExxonMobil sites?

Not automatically, but documentation prepared to ExxonMobil's typical group standard is intended to be a useful starting point for other ExxonMobil-operated sites elsewhere.
