The site · ExxonMobil Antwerp

What is classified at the Antwerp refinery

A refinery is a set of pressure vessels, furnaces, columns, heat exchangers and pumps connected by pipework, and the classified area follows the hydrocarbon. At Antwerp ExxonMobil has added a delayed coker, started in 2018 at about 50,000 barrels a day to turn heavy, high-sulphur residue into marine gasoil and diesel; a diesel hydrotreater to raise low-sulphur diesel output; and a 130 megawatt cogeneration unit. Each of those brought new classified areas. Hydrotreating handles hydrogen at pressure, and the coker handles hot heavy oil and light gas from the drums.

We have kept to what ExxonMobil has announced publicly, which is the refinery, the coker, the hydrotreater and the cogeneration unit, and have not described other process units in detail. The refinery stands within the Antwerp cluster beside BASF and TotalEnergies, each with separate procurement.

What a refinery of this kind does to a explosion proof mobile phone in Belgium

Temperature class matters here

The coker and furnace areas run hot, and some streams have low auto-ignition temperatures. The marking target of the EX-04Z1 is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, and the temperature class on the certificate is the figure your instrumentation engineer compares with the area classification.

Hydrogen duty in the hydrotreater

Group IIC is the group that includes hydrogen. For hydrotreater and recycle gas areas a device that stops at IIB will be refused or replaced early, so IIC is the design target.

Gas detection and escape are part of the round

Operators carry personal gas monitors, and a handset must not add to the kit worn on the body. IP68 sealing is aimed at a device that is carried for twelve hours and put down on a rack or a rail in the rain.

A site within a global operator's standards

ExxonMobil operates under its own group engineering and procurement rules. We do not know the specific requirements and do not claim any approval; the practical step is to ask its site procurement and HSE teams what documentation they want to see for portable electrical equipment.

Managed like the rest of the estate

Android Enterprise enrolment lets the site manage units from its existing console, set camera policy by area and remove contractor units at the end of a job.

Approval and access

What the site will want to see

ExxonMobil Antwerp is inside the EU, so the product-side requirement is ATEX 2014/34/EU: a notified body certificate, the CE marking and the EU declaration of conformity, with instructions in Dutch for a Flemish site. IECEx documentation is being prepared alongside, because a global operator often works from IECEx evidence. We do not claim that either is accepted by ExxonMobil, and certification of the EX-04Z1 is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Contractors working on the site would normally be asked by its procurement and HSE teams for safety management credentials such as SCC or VCA, and for equipment records. Ask those teams for the current supplier and contractor requirements before a trial.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at ExxonMobil Antwerp yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and ATEX and IECEx certification are in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. Nothing will be supplied for a classified area before the certificate covering it exists.

What does a delayed coker do, and is it a harder place to work?

A delayed coker heats heavy residue and cracks it in coke drums into lighter liquids, gas and petroleum coke. The drums cycle between filling, steaming and cutting, so conditions change through the day. The classification is set by the site, and the certificate will state the exact scope.

Does ExxonMobil Antwerp need different documentation from a European operator?

Probably the same core documents, plus whatever its global standards add. We are preparing ATEX and IECEx documentation in English, Dutch and French and would ask the site team what else it wants.

Would approval here carry to other ExxonMobil sites?

No. Each site makes its own decision. Documents prepared to a group standard may be a useful starting point elsewhere, nothing more.
