Why it fits petrochemicals and refining

Built for Europe's densest petrochemical cluster

Antwerp's cluster concentrates an unusual number of major international chemical companies' flagship sites in one riverside industrial zone, each running its own operations but sharing infrastructure, contractors and, often, adjacent site boundaries.

Zone 1 gas across crackers, refineries and derivative plants

Ethane and naphtha crackers, refinery process units and downstream derivative production across the Antwerp cluster are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that range of processes.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, Belgium's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives, and the EX-04Z1's certification programme is built around that framework as the primary reference for Antwerp-area operators.

Multiple major international operators on adjacent sites

BASF, TotalEnergies, ExxonMobil, INEOS, Covestro, Borealis and Evonik all operate within the same general cluster, meaning contractors and inspectors move between neighbouring companies' sites regularly, favouring equipment recognised broadly across the cluster.

A mix of decades-old and newly built infrastructure

Alongside long-established refining and petrochemical assets, the cluster includes major new construction, such as INEOS's ethane cracker, meaning the area's classified-area landscape spans both mature and greenfield sites.

Fleet management across a dense multi-operator cluster

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator managing devices consistently across its own plants within the cluster, even as neighbouring companies run separate procurement processes.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for petrochemical and refining use in Belgium yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does approval at one Antwerp company's site carry to a neighbouring plant?

No, each company operating in the cluster runs its own separate procurement process, even where sites are adjacent and share some infrastructure.

Does the device suit both older refining assets and newer construction like INEOS's cracker?

Yes, the design intent is a broadly rated Zone 1 device usable across both mature and newly commissioned classified areas.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for Antwerp-area operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence, particularly for operators like ExxonMobil with a global engineering standard.
