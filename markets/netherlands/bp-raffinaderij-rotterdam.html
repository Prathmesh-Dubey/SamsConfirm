The site · Europoort

What is classified at the bp refinery in Europoort

The refinery sits at Europoort, at the seaward end of the Nieuwe Waterweg, which puts deep-water jetties, process units and tank farms inside one boundary. The classified area is not one zone but a mosaic: hydrocarbon vapour around pumps, flanges, sample points and tank roof seals; hydrogen where hydrotreating circuits recycle it; hydrogen sulphide where sour streams are handled; and sulphur or catalyst dust at the points where solids are moved. Which zone applies where is written in the site's own explosieveiligheidsdocument, and the operator's engineers own it. What follows is the general picture a supplier should understand before asking to be considered, not a substitute for that document.

Who is on the fence

Operations
Outside operators on rounds, panel operators in the control building, and shift supervisors who issue work permits.
Turnaround contractors
Mechanical, scaffolding and inspection crews, including Dutch maintenance firms such as Stork, arriving in large numbers for a stop.
Jetty and marine staff
Mooring crews, loading masters and ships’ officers during crude receipt and product loading.
Neighbouring terminals
Independent tank storage at Europoort, including Vopak, shares roads, pipe corridors and emergency planning with the refinery.
Regulators
Nederlandse Arbeidsinspectie, the regional environment service DCMR and the Rotterdam-Rijnmond safety region.

Classified units and areas

Crude jetties and loading arms
Open-air vapour zones that move with each ship; port facility security rules also apply.
Tank farms
Floating-roof tanks where vapour collects at roof seals and on the roof itself.
Distillation and conversion units
Light hydrocarbon vapour around pumps, heat exchangers and relief systems.
Hydrogen circuits
Hydrotreating make-up and recycle gas, where the gas group is IIC and the temperature class low.
Sulphur and sour water handling
Hydrogen sulphide exposure and, at solids handling points, combustible dust.
Control buildings and offices
Safe area; the handset is checked in and out of the classified boundary at the door.

Explosion proof mobile phone conditions at Europoort, in the Netherlands

A refinery on the open water at the mouth of the Maas is hard on equipment for reasons that have little to do with the zone marking. These are the four conditions that come up most when a site of this type reviews a handset.

Exposed to wind and salt at the water’s edge

Europoort has little shelter. Sea air, driven rain and wash-down are the normal state of a jetty head or tank roof, which is why IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H construction matter more here than any dust rating.

Hydrogen sets the gas group

Where hydrotreating circuits are present, IIC is the group an instrumentation engineer will look for. The EX-04Z1 marking target of II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 is aimed at that, with the certificate to confirm it in due course.

No charging inside the boundary

A round that covers units, tanks and a jetty means a long walk to the nearest safe-area socket. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified for a shift with the camera and a work-order application open.

One handset, several rules

The camera is a gate question at a refinery. Android Enterprise management lets the site’s own team set camera, location and application policy from its existing console, so the answer to the gate is demonstrable rather than promised.

How the work is organised, and what that asks of a handset

Turnaround01

A planned stop, with contractors by the hundred

A stop compresses months of work into a short window. Isolation photographs, job packs and sign-offs need to live in one system on one device that survives the shift, instead of a paper folder that leaves with the contractor.

Marine02

Crude receipt and product loading

A transfer runs to the ship’s schedule, not the shift pattern. The person at the loading arm needs a voice line to the control room, a camera for a manifold check and a position that GNSS can supply without leaving the vapour zone.

Operations03

Night rounds across a large site

Outside operators walk long routes alone at night. Positions from four-constellation GNSS, a data connection and a lone-worker check-in on the same handset replace the radio, the camera and the paper log.

Frequently asked questions

Will an ordinary phone ever be allowed at a refinery gate?

Many sites ban them in classified areas outright and restrict cameras in the rest. The operator’s own rules decide that, and an Ex handset with managed camera and location settings is the usual answer when staff do need a smartphone in the process area.

Which gas group does a refinery handset need?

It depends on the area classification, but hydrogen puts IIC on the table wherever it is handled. A target of IIC covers the lower groups as well, which is why the EX-04Z1 programme is aimed there.

Does contractor use differ from operator use?

Yes in practice. Contractors arrive for a stop with their own devices and rules, so a handset that the site can enrol, restrict and wipe at the end of the job is easier to admit than one it has to trust.
