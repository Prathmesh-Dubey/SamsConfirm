The sector · gas, hydrogen and CO2

Where gas, hydrogen and CO2 work is classified

Groningen production ceased in October 2023 and the field closed for good in 2024, so the hazard now sits in import, storage, hydrogen and CO2. Gasunie runs the transmission grid and is building the national hydrogen network, as described on our explosion proof phone for Gasunie page. The Maasvlakte holds LNG import, including the Gate terminal, and the site of Shell’s Holland Hydrogen 1 electrolyser project, and Eemshaven has an LNG terminal and power generation. Carbon capture projects such as Porthos will add compression and pipelines. Natural gas is group IIA, hydrogen is IIC, and CO2 is not flammable but is handled with flammable products.

How the work is organised on gas and hydrogen sites

Commissioning01

New hydrogen and CO2 plant

A commissioning team checks lines, valves and instruments in areas that are newly classified, often before the full operating routine exists. Loop checks, purge records and punch-list photographs all need a home, and a handset carrying drawings, photographs and a position record keeps them with the asset rather than in a contractor’s notebook.

Terminals02

LNG import

Terminal staff walk between ship connections, vaporisers and storage, often in cold, windy conditions at the waterside. The handset covers voice, camera and a lone-worker check-in on one device, so that a ship transfer can be reported from where it happens.

Network03

Pipeline and station inspection

Inspectors visit stations and valve sites on a schedule, many of them small and unmanned, recording results with photographs and positions. The record is only as good as the position it carries, so four-constellation GNSS is a working requirement, not an extra.

Frequently asked questions

Is CO2 a hazard for a phone?

CO2 is not flammable, so it does not create a classified area by itself. Carbon capture sites do handle flammable products nearby, such as fuel gas and process streams, and it is those that set the zones. Compression plant and pipelines for CO2 also add pressure and cold hazards that sit outside the Ex marking.

Why not equipment marked IIB?

Hydrogen is group IIC, with a very low ignition energy. Equipment marked only for IIB is not appropriate where hydrogen is present, and a site converting from natural gas to hydrogen may find that handsets bought for the old duty no longer fit the new area classification.

Does the handset work on new sites?

It runs Android 16 under Android Enterprise, so it can be enrolled wherever a console exists.
