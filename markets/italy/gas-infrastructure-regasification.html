How Italy’s gas system is laid out

Italy imports most of its gas, so the system is built around entry points and storage. Pipeline gas arrives at Mazara del Vallo from Algeria, at Gela from Libya, at Tarvisio and Passo Gries from the north and, since the Trans Adriatic Pipeline began flowing, at the terminal near Melendugno in Puglia. LNG arrives at the Panigaglia terminal near La Spezia, the Adriatic LNG terminal off Porto Levante, the FSRU off Livorno and the floating units at Piombino and off Ravenna. Storage is in depleted fields in the Po valley and Adriatic regions, which carry the winter swing. Snam is the largest operator, as covered on the page on explosion proof phone for Snam, but it is not the only one: other companies run storage, terminals and distribution, and Eni’s own upstream gas comes ashore around Ravenna. The hazard is mostly methane, which is gas group IIA, so the classification is less severe than at a refinery. The difficulty is the number of sites, their remoteness and the changing contractor mix.

Gas sites are quiet most of the time and then busy for a day. The handset has to work after months in a van or a locker.

Remote and unattended sites

A technician at a reduction station is often alone, so a position fix, a call and a record of the job all matter. Four-constellation GNSS on a Zone 1 handset covers that, subject to coverage.

Marine and exposed environments

FSRUs and the terminal at Porto Levante are in open water, so spray and salt are constant. IP68 sealing is aimed at that, and the handset is designed to take a drop onto steel deck.

Cold and cryogenic zones

LNG vaporiser areas have extreme cold at the pipework and warm ambient air around it. The datasheet will state the operating range; check it against the conditions where a person actually stands.

Methane, hydrogen and what comes next

Methane is gas group IIA and hydrogen blending is discussed in the sector. The EX-04Z1 is being certified to IIC, which includes hydrogen, so the same device is designed to stay suitable if gas mixes change.

Frequently asked questions

Which gas sites are on this page?

Named entry points, terminals and storage fields as examples, not a full list. The operator’s zone map decides classification for each site.

Do floating LNG units need different equipment?

They need equipment suited to marine areas, with sealing against spray and a certificate for the zone where it is used. The vessel’s classification society and the operator define what is acceptable aboard.

Who sets supplier requirements?

Each operator, through procurement and HSE. We have no relationship with any of them; ask for current registration and equipment-evidence requirements.

Is the handset available now?

No. It is pre-launch for Q4 2026, certification is in progress, and use in a classified area waits for the certificate.
