Why it fits power and desalination

Built for independent water and power plants, not just refineries

Bahrain's power and desalination fleet is a set of independent plants run by different operating companies rather than one integrated site, so vendor and site-access requirements vary plant to plant.

Zone 1 gas around fuel-gas trains

Gas-fired turbines at Al Dur, Al Ezzel and Hidd draw fuel gas through skids classified Zone 1. The Ex ib IIC T4 marking target covers that risk the same way it covers Sitra's refinery gas.

Continuous coastal exposure

All three sites sit directly on the coast for cooling-water and desalination intake, adding salt-air exposure on top of gas classification. MIL-STD-810H construction addresses that combined load.

Independent plants, independent vendor lists

Each IPP/IWPP is a separate legal entity with its own procurement process, so a device evaluated at Al Dur does not automatically carry over to Al Ezzel or Hidd without a separate vendor submission.

Long shifts with fixed control-room rounds

Plant operators walk fixed rounds between turbine hall, fuel-gas skid and control room across a full shift. IP68 sealing and full-shift battery life match that pattern.

Frequently asked questions

Does the same device work at Al Dur, Al Ezzel and Hidd?

The technical specification, Zone 1 Ex ib IIC T4, is the same across all three, but each plant is a separate operating company and runs its own vendor-approval process.

Is desalination a hazardous-area risk in the same way as gas turbines?

The desalination process itself is not typically classified, but it shares a site and often a fuel-gas supply with the co-located power plant, so the same Zone 1 areas around the turbine hall apply.

Who do we contact to get listed as an approved supplier?

SAMS is appointing a Bahraini partner who can carry the BSMD documentation and approach each IPP/IWPP's procurement team individually once certification completes.
