Why it fits aluminium and metals

Built for potroom and casthouse dust, not just refinery gas

Bahrain's aluminium sector is a dust hazard rather than a gas hazard, and it is easy to under-specify a device built primarily for oil and gas against it.

Zone 21 dust across six potlines

Alba's potlines generate fine carbon and alumina dust continuously. IP68 sealing is what a shift depends on after a dust event, and the equipment protection level target covers combustible dust as well as gas.

Casthouse heat radiates further than a control room

Molten metal handling in the casthouse runs high ambient temperatures well beyond general industrial ranges. The device's thermal design and IP68 sealing are specified against sustained radiant heat, not just ambient air temperature.

Rolling mill work adds a second dust source at Hidd

GARMCO's Hidd rolling mill handles aluminium fines during hot and cold rolling, extending the same Zone 21 requirement beyond Askar to a second site.

One spec for the whole downstream chain

Smelting, casting and rolling sit under a single dust classification, so the same handset issued at Askar also works at Hidd without a second procurement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dust-rated device different from a gas-rated one?

Yes. Zone 21 dust protection and Zone 1 gas protection are separate classifications with separate test regimes, though a single IP68-sealed device can be certified against both when the certificate covers it.

Does it suit both Alba's potrooms and GARMCO's rolling mill?

Yes, both generate aluminium-related dust under the same Zone 21 classification, so one specification covers smelting, casting and rolling.

Will Alba's engineering review accept it before certification completes?

No device enters a classified area without the underlying IECEx certificate. Evaluation units are available now for handling and enterprise-management trials in safe areas ahead of that.
