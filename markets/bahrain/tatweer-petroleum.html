Why it fits Tatweer Petroleum

Built for a brownfield EOR operation, not a greenfield plant

The Bahrain Field has produced since 1932, and Tatweer's EOR programme layers new steam and polymer-injection equipment onto that older wellhead footprint, which changes how equipment gets qualified for site use.

Mixed-vintage area classification

Older wellheads classified under legacy schemes sit next to newer EOR injection equipment classified under current IEC 60079 practice. Tatweer's engineering review checks a device against both eras of documentation on file, not just the newer one.

Steam and thermal EOR add heat load beyond ambient

Steam-flood EOR introduces localised thermal loads around injection wellheads beyond what a conventional oil field sees. IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H testing are specified against that combined heat and moisture exposure.

A dispersed wellfield changes patrol patterns

Unlike Sitra's concentrated refinery footprint, Bahrain Field wellheads are spread across a wide onshore area, so field technicians carry a device between widely separated sites for a full shift on a single charge.

Distinct from Bapco despite common ownership history

Tatweer operates under its own EOR concession agreement and runs a separate vendor-qualification process from Bapco's refinery-focused one, even though both trace back to the same national oil sector.

Questions · Tatweer Petroleum

Frequently asked questions

Is Tatweer Petroleum the same as Bapco Energies?

No, they are separate operating entities. Tatweer holds the enhanced oil recovery concession for the onshore Bahrain Field, distinct from Bapco's refinery and broader upstream operations.

Does the age of the Bahrain Field's equipment affect certification?

It affects Tatweer's own engineering review, which checks a new device's documentation against both older and newer area-classification records on file, though the IECEx certificate itself is unaffected by site vintage.

Does the device suit a spread-out wellfield rather than a compact plant?

Yes. Full-shift battery life and rugged sealing are specified for exactly that kind of dispersed, walk-between-sites field pattern.
