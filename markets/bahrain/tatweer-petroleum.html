The site · Tatweer Petroleum

Explosion proof mobile phone for the Bahrain Field

The Bahrain Field, also called the Awali Field after the district at its northern end, produced oil for decades before Tatweer took over operations in December 2009, according to trade press. That press has also reported well over a thousand new wells since then, a drilling programme into the deeper Khuff gas reservoir, and gas dehydration facilities. Bapco Energies lists Tatweer within its portfolio and has been reported as the owner; ask the operators for the current structure before assembling a vendor file.

A field this old mixes generations of equipment. Newer wellsites and gathering systems sit beside older installations whose area classification documents may have been revised several times, so a handset is checked against drawings of different ages.

How work runs · Tatweer

How work is organised across a wellfield

Dispersed sites, vehicle-based rounds

Wellsites are spread across the field, so rounds are driven. A technician cannot return to a safe-area charging point between wells, which makes full-shift battery a design requirement; the 4,000 mAh cell is specified for it.

A position for every defect

A leaking wellhead gland or a corroded flowline needs a coordinate more than a description. Four-constellation GNSS and the 50 MP camera together give that.

Mixed-vintage documentation

Older and newer installations carry classification documents of different dates. The engineering review will compare the certificate and its limits with whichever is current for the area.

Drilling campaigns move the hazard

A rig brings its classified area with it. Contractor crews on a campaign can be issued enrolled devices for its duration.

Language and photography policy

A field workforce mixes national and expatriate staff, so Arabic and English on one Android 16 build suits the shift. Photography at oil and gas sites runs on permit, and the camera policy can be pushed from the existing MDM console rather than left to individual users.

Sour gas awareness

Khuff gas and associated gas may carry hydrogen sulphide, which is a toxic hazard handled by personal and fixed gas detection, not by a phone.

What the operator will ask of a supplier

The EX-04Z1 is pre-launch (Q4 2026) and no certificate has been issued; IECEx, ATEX, NEC 500 and BSMD work is in progress. SAMS has no relationship with Tatweer.

We found no separate published Tatweer supplier-registration scheme. Bapco Energies, in whose portfolio it is listed, runs a Supplier Portal and requires adherence to a Supplier Code of Conduct, and Bapco Upstream publishes tenders on the Bahrain Tender Board. Ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams which applies to a handset, who reviews it, and how they treat older and newer area documentation. The national route (BSMD, GSO and TRA) is on the Bahrain country page.

Questions · Tatweer Petroleum

Frequently asked questions

Is Tatweer the same as Bapco Energies?

Trade press and the group's portfolio list Tatweer as a separate operating company within it. Check which entity is the buyer for the site you mean.

Does the age of the field affect certification?

No, the certificate does not depend on site age. It affects the operator's review, which compares the certificate with whatever classification drawings exist for each area.

Is the phone suitable for a spread-out wellfield?

It is designed for it: IP68 sealing, a 4,000 mAh battery for a shift and GNSS for positions. Evaluation in safe areas comes first.

Can it detect hydrogen sulphide?

No. Gas detection is a separate certified safety system. The handset must not be an ignition source and should carry the call when an alarm sounds.
