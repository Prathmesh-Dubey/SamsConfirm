Why it fits

Designed for a solar-evaporation potash process, not a conventional mine

APC's process runs through evaporation ponds and mechanical harvesting before the mineral even reaches a crushing or drying plant, a sequence with its own environmental demands ahead of the combustible-dust hazard downstream.

Zone 21/22 dust rating at the processing plant

Crushing, drying and compaction of harvested carnallite and potash generate combustible dust, and the EX-04Z1's Zone 21 / Zone 22 target addresses that stage of APC's operation.

Extreme heat and airborne salinity ahead of the dust hazard

The Dead Sea basin runs some of the highest sustained temperatures and humidity levels of any industrial site in the region, combined with fine airborne salt particulate from the evaporation ponds, a combination that stresses seals and connectors differently from a dry desert dust site.

A listed public company with export-driven scale

As a major global potash exporter and one of Jordan's largest private-sector employers, APC's operational scale means device standardisation decisions affect a large workforce spread across pond, harvesting and processing operations rather than one small unit.

Below-sea-level logistics

APC's Dead Sea operations sit roughly 400 metres below sea level, a lowest-point-on-Earth logistics reality that makes emergency response and equipment resupply slower than at a standard elevation industrial site.

Fleet management across pond, harvesting and plant operations

EMM and MDM enrolment supports APC managing a consistent device profile from the evaporation ponds through mechanical harvesting to the crushing and drying plant.

About the operator

Arab Potash Company (APC)

One of the world's largest potash producers, extracting and processing potash from Dead Sea brine.

Core process
Solar evaporation of Dead Sea brine, mechanical harvesting, then crushing, drying and compaction.
Location
Dead Sea basin, roughly 400 metres below sea level, the lowest point on Earth's land surface.
Primary hazard
Combustible potash and carnallite dust at the processing plant, alongside extreme heat and airborne salinity across the site.
Evaluation basis
Site engineering review, built on the IECEx Certificate of Conformity's dust-rating scope once issued.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at Arab Potash yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and the IECEx dust-rating scope is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Is this a gas-hazard or dust-hazard certification?

Dust. APC's process does not involve a significant flammable-gas hazard; combustible mineral dust in the crushing and drying plant is the relevant classification.

Does the extreme Dead Sea heat require special testing?

Environmental durability testing is specified with that heat and salinity exposure in mind, though material compatibility for the specific brine and dust conditions should be confirmed with APC's own engineering team.

Does APC share procurement with Jordan Phosphate Mines Company?

No. APC and JPMC are separate companies mining different minerals at different locations, each running its own procurement and engineering-approval process.
