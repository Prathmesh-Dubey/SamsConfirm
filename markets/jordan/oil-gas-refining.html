Jordan has no significant domestic crude production of its own, so its downstream sector centres on one inland refinery at Zarqa and the fuel storage and distribution terminals that move imported and refined product on to the rest of the country, including through Aqaba on the Red Sea coast.

Why it fits oil, gas and refining

Built for a single-refinery, import-dependent downstream sector

With one national refinery and fuel moving mostly by import, storage and pipeline, Jordan's downstream hazard profile centres on tank farms, loading terminals and one older inland processing site.

Zone 1 gas at the refinery and terminals

Crude and product processing units at Zarqa, along with tank farms and loading bays at Aqaba and other storage terminals, are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of refining and fuel storage.

An inland refinery, not a coastal one

Unlike most of the region's refineries, Zarqa sits well inland from the coast, meaning refined product moves onward by road, rail or pipeline to reach distribution points and the Aqaba export/import terminal, adding transfer and loading-bay classified areas along that route.

A long-operating, single national asset

As the country's sole refinery, Zarqa has been in continuous operation for decades, and equipment reaching site needs to work alongside a mixed-vintage classified-area layout typical of an older, expanded-in-place facility.

Aqaba's dual role as import and export gateway

Fuel storage terminals at Aqaba handle both imported product feeding the domestic market and, at times, product moving to export, adding marine-jetty and tank-farm loading exposure to the sector's classified-area footprint.

Centrally managed across refinery and storage sites

EMM and MDM enrolment supports an operator or distribution company managing devices consistently across the Zarqa refinery and Aqaba-area storage terminals.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use in Jordan's refining sector yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does the device suit an older, inland refinery like Zarqa?

It is designed to be usable across mixed-vintage classified areas typical of a long-operating, expanded-in-place refinery, though site-specific classification always takes precedence.

Does the device cover fuel storage terminals as well as the refinery?

Yes, the same Zone 1, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is intended to cover tank farms and loading bays at storage terminals in addition to refinery process units.

Is Jordan's downstream sector import-dependent?

Yes, with no significant domestic crude production, much of Jordan's fuel supply arrives as imported crude for refining or as imported refined product moving through terminals like Aqaba.
