The sector · Basel to the Valais

Explosion proof mobile phone use in Swiss pharmaceutical and fine chemical plants

Switzerland's pharmaceutical and fine chemical industry is not one site but a sequence of them. In the Basel region, the big groups combine research, development and production, and the Rhine corridor carries manufacturing and logistics up to Kaiseraugst. In the Valais, Visp is home to Lonza's largest site, and Siegfried and Bachem, whose main sites are at Zofingen and Bubendorf, also have Valais production. Monthey in the Chablais is a shared chemical platform. Schaffhausen is home to the Cilag pharmaceutical site, established there in 1936. These plants differ in what they make, but they share a hazard pattern. Reactions and purification use flammable solvents in charging, filtration, distillation and drying, which brings Zone 1 and Zone 2 questions, and the final steps of milling, blending and dispensing bring dust. Where both occur in one building, the classification can change within a few metres, and a single handset that covers gas and dust avoids the swap at the airlock. This sector page covers that common ground, and the company pages for Lonza, Novartis and Roche cover the specifics.

Questions · Pharma

Frequently asked questions

Which Swiss pharmaceutical operators does this page cover?

The sector in general. Lonza, Novartis and Roche have their own pages on this site, and Siegfried, Bachem and Cilag are named as examples of operators with solvent and powder handling. Naming a company does not imply any relationship.

Does hydrogen need a different handset?

Hydrogen is Group IIC, the most demanding gas group, and the marking target for the EX-04Z1 is IIC. Whether a particular room is classified for hydrogen is a site decision.

Does the handset replace the process control terminal?

No. It is for rounds, inspections and communication. Process control remains with the plant's own systems.

Can one certificate cover several sites?

The product certificate does, once it exists. Each site still decides where the device may be used, based on its own classification and approval rules.
