The sites · Basel and Stein

Where an explosion proof mobile phone fits at Novartis in Switzerland

Most of a pharmaceutical company's Swiss workforce does not work in an explosive atmosphere, and it is worth saying so plainly. The Novartis Campus in Basel is predominantly offices, laboratories and research space, and the classified areas are specific rooms rather than whole buildings: chemical development laboratories, solvent stores, pilot-scale equipment and the utilities that serve them. Stein, in the canton of Aargau, is the manufacturing site. It has historically carried solid oral dosage, injectable products and cell and gene therapy production, along with a grinding and mixing centre for chemical work, and Novartis has publicly announced changes to the manufacturing mix at Stein, so the current scope of each building should be taken from the company rather than from this page. The practical result is a small, specific population of people who really do need an Ex handset: laboratory technicians working with solvents, plant engineers, utilities and maintenance staff, and contractors on construction and refit projects. Where a dust or solvent area exists, such as powder handling or solvent-based coating, the classification sits in a site document and the handset is matched to it.

What a pharmaceutical technical site asks of a handset

A device that is carried by few people into a limited set of rooms has to earn its place. These are the practical questions a Novartis engineer would put to it.

Clearly separate from the ordinary phone

The handset needs to be unmistakable, so nobody carries a personal phone into a classified room by mistake. Managing the EX-04Z1 through the company's existing MDM console lets IT keep it enrolled and distinguishable from the standard fleet.

Cleaning regimes that wear a seal out

Pharmaceutical areas are cleaned often and with strong agents. The IP68 sealing and rugged build are aimed at a plant that is wiped down daily; the cleaning agents approved by the site should be checked against the manual.

Switzerland in three languages

Basel and Stein are German speaking, but a group workforce is mixed. The interface is available in German, French and Italian, and Swiss rules require product information in the official language of the market.

Connected to the existing systems

Android 16 with Android Enterprise supports the managed applications a maintenance team already uses, such as work-order and permit tools, without a separate Ex-only platform.

Questions · Novartis

Frequently asked questions

Is the whole Novartis Campus a hazardous area?

No. The campus is mostly offices and laboratories, and classification applies to specific rooms and plant. The EX-04Z1 is for the people who work in those rooms, not for the general workforce.

How would a pharmaceutical operator assess an Ex handset?

We do not know Novartis's internal process and have not been through it. Ask its EHS and procurement teams for the equipment approval requirements, the area classification documents and the data protection conditions that apply to a handset with a camera.

Who inspects explosion protection on a Swiss pharmaceutical site?

The employer is responsible under the Swiss accident prevention framework. SUVA, as accident insurer for most industrial employers, publishes the explosion protection guidance that safety officers use, and ESTI supervises electrical installations in zones.

Does the EX-04Z1 replace the radio network?

It is a smartphone, not a replacement for any site radio or alarm system. What it adds is data, camera and navigation. Any dependency on a site's own radio or paging system should be checked with its telecoms team.
