The site · SATORP

Explosion proof mobile phone for a full-conversion refinery at Jubail

SATORP is owned 62.5 per cent by Saudi Aramco and 37.5 per cent by TotalEnergies. It was designed for 400,000 barrels a day of heavy crude and is a full-conversion refinery, which means residue is upgraded rather than sold as fuel oil. The route there runs through a large delayed coker, hydrotreaters and hydrocracking, with sulphur recovery for the sour feed and integrated petrochemical units downstream. Hydrogen is present throughout the hydroprocessing units and falls in gas group IIC, hydrogen sulphide is present on the sour side, and petroleum coke and sulphur are handled as solids. The site lies within Jubail Industrial City, so most of the people moving through it are either shift operators or contractors on a planned job.

Hydrogen means the IIC question comes up

Hydroprocessing puts hydrogen in the picture, so the gas group on the drawing matters. The marking target for the EX-04Z1 is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, and the certificate will state the scope once it is issued.

A coker site is dusty as well as gassy

Handling coke and sulphur brings combustible solids into the plant alongside the gas. The device is designed for Zone 21 dust as well as Zone 1 gas, and IP68 sealing keeps the fine material out.

Two shareholders, two review habits

An Aramco-style vendor review and a European, ATEX-influenced engineering culture can look at the same file with different questions. The IECEx documentation is being built to be readable by either, with the ATEX marking target stated plainly.

Planned shutdowns drive the workload

Turnaround work at a refinery of this size puts a large contractor crew through a limited number of gates. Handsets enrolled in an existing console can be issued, configured and collected centrally.

Heat above ground level and wind-blown sand

Furnace areas, hot piping and open desert summers all raise the temperature around the handset. The temperature class on the certificate is what the instrumentation engineer checks against the area's maximum surface temperature.

Use cases · SATORP

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work at SATORP

Process01

Unit operator rounds

An operator walking the hydrotreater or the coker records readings, photographs a flange weeping and reports it to the control room without leaving the unit. One enrolled handset replaces the radio and camera that would otherwise be carried separately.

Shutdown02

Turnaround contractor crews

A contractor gang entering a coker or hydrocracker during a shutdown works to a permit issued that morning. The handset carries the permit application and site directory, and the operator's console controls what else is installed.

Inspection03

Tank farm and loading area checks

An inspector covering the tank farm needs a position and a photograph for each finding. Four-constellation GNSS and the 50 MP camera give a record that the maintenance planner can act on without a site visit.

Questions · SATORP

Frequently asked questions

Which shareholder's standards apply to equipment at SATORP?

That is for SATORP's own engineering and procurement teams to say. Ask them directly for the requirements that apply to a communications device, and what evidence they want with it.

Is the 62.5 and 37.5 per cent split relevant to us as a supplier?

Only indirectly. It tells you both companies have a stake in the engineering outcome, so expect questions from either side. It does not give any shareholder the right to approve a supplier on SATORP's behalf.

Does the EX-04Z1 cover hydrogen service?

It is being designed to Ex ib IIC, the gas group that includes hydrogen. The certificate will state its precise scope once issued, and an area drawing needs checking for temperature class as well as gas group.

Has SATORP approved the EX-04Z1?

No. It is pre-launch, certification is in progress ahead of Q4 2026, and no vendor approval can exist before the certificate does.
