Why it fits oil and gas

Built for the upstream-to-export chain

Oil and gas work in the Kingdom spans remote wellheads, sour gas plants and coastal export terminals — three different environments with one certification requirement.

Zone 1 gas as standard

Wellhead, gathering centre, gas plant and export terminal are all Zone 1 gas atmospheres. Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety covers the hydrogen-group service found in sour gas processing, not just the more common hydrocarbon gas groups.

A device that survives a remote posting

Shaybah and Khurais are a truck journey from the nearest stores, not a walk. IP68 sealing, MIL-STD-810H testing and a 4,000 mAh cell are specified for a site where a failed handset is not a same-day problem to fix.

One device for the whole value chain

A single Ex ib IIC T4 / Zone 1 specification covers the wellhead, the gas plant, the refinery and the export terminal, so operators do not need a different device for upstream versus downstream classified areas.

Built for turnaround and shutdown surges

Refinery and gas-plant turnarounds multiply the contractor population and the permit traffic with it. One handset carrying the permit app, the site directory and the camera cuts what every crew signs out.

Managed centrally across a large estate

EMM and MDM enrolment lets an oil and gas operator push the corporate profile and camera policy across hundreds of units spread over multiple sites from one console.

Questions · Oil & Gas

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for oil and gas use in Saudi Arabia yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and every certification, including IECEx, ATEX and the SASO conformity route, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. It will not be supplied into a classified area before the certificate covering it exists.

Does it cover sour gas service?

The Ex ib IIC T4 marking target is gas group IIC, the most demanding of the three IEC gas groups and the one that covers hydrogen and, by extension, the higher-risk end of sour gas service. The certificate will state the exact scope once issued.

Will Saudi Aramco accept it as an approved device?

That decision follows Aramco’s own vendor-approval and engineering-review process, which is built on the IECEx certificate once issued. A device being SASO/SABER-cleared for import does not by itself mean it is listed for use on an Aramco site.

Does it work for both upstream and downstream sites?

Yes — the same Zone 1 / Zone 21, Ex ib IIC T4 specification is designed to cover wellhead and gathering-centre work through to refinery and export-terminal operations, so an operator does not need separate devices for each stage.
