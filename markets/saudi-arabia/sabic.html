Why it fits

Designed for an affiliate structure, not a single vendor list

SABIC has grown through joint ventures and wholly owned affiliates — separate legal entities such as its olefins, polymer and fertiliser producers — several of which have their own procurement and engineering-approval history rather than a single group-wide device list.

Approval at the affiliate, not the group

A device accepted by one SABIC affiliate's engineering team does not automatically carry to another's, since procurement authority for site equipment has traditionally sat at the affiliate level. SAMS is scoping introductions per affiliate rather than assuming a single group-wide sign-off.

Now under an Aramco-majority ownership structure

Saudi Aramco holds a majority stake in SABIC, which is reshaping how some group-level standards and approved-vendor practices are aligned across the two organisations over time; the EX-04Z1's IECEx-based documentation is built to satisfy either review path.

Jubail and Yanbu as two distinct industrial-city environments

SABIC's manufacturing base spans both the Eastern Province's Jubail complex and the Red Sea coast at Yanbu, two sites with different climate exposure and different neighbouring-plant traffic patterns that affect how a device gets used day to day.

Fertiliser affiliates bring dust into scope

Where SABIC's fertiliser-producing affiliates run granulation and bagging, the Zone 21 dust target sits alongside the Zone 1 gas rating used across the olefins and polymer affiliates.

Fleet management built for a multi-entity group

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a corporate IT function issuing a consistent device profile across affiliates that remain operationally distinct.

Frequently asked questions

Is SABIC one procurement process or several?

Historically several — individual manufacturing affiliates have run their own vendor and engineering-approval processes, so introductions are being scoped affiliate by affiliate rather than assumed to be group-wide.

Does Aramco's ownership of SABIC change the approval route?

It is aligning some standards over time, but as of now the two organisations' vendor-approval practices are not identical, so documentation is being prepared to satisfy either review.

Is the EX-04Z1 approved for use on any SABIC site yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and no vendor approval exists at any SABIC affiliate before the underlying IECEx and SASO certification is complete.

Does the same device cover both olefins and fertiliser affiliates?

That is the design intent — a single Zone 1 / Zone 21 specification meant to serve gas-hazard process units and dust-handling fertiliser areas without requiring separate devices.
