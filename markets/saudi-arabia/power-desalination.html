Why it fits power & desalination

Built for classified pockets inside a mostly-unclassified plant

Unlike a refinery, a co-generation and desalination plant is mostly a safe-area industrial site with a small number of genuinely hazardous zones — fuel-gas metering, chlorine dosing and hypochlorite generation — which is exactly where a device gets carried without a second thought if it isn't obviously rated for it.

Fuel-gas skids as the core gas hazard

Gas turbine fuel-gas conditioning and metering skids are classified Zone 1 or Zone 2 depending on layout, and Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety is specified to cover that gas-handling equipment without over-specifying the rest of the plant.

Chlorine and hypochlorite dosing bring a distinct chemical hazard

Desalination trains use chlorine gas or on-site hypochlorite generation for biofouling control, areas that carry their own classification separate from the fuel-gas system and are easy to under-rate because they don't look like an oil and gas hazard.

Marine intake and outfall structures

Seawater intake and brine outfall works sit in a coastal, sometimes tidal environment, and IP68 sealing is specified with that exposure in mind rather than only a dry industrial setting.

Co-located but separately operated units

Power and desalination trains on the same site are sometimes operated by different entities or under different offtake agreements, so a device needs to be acceptable to more than one operating team's site rules.

Fleet management across a multi-site corridor

EMM and MDM enrolment supports a utility operator managing devices across several co-generation and desalination sites along the corridor from one console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use at power and desalination plants yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including IECEx and SASO, is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Do these sites need the same rating as a refinery?

The classified areas are smaller in extent but still require a genuine Zone 1 / Zone 2 rating for fuel-gas and chlorine-dosing areas; the rest of a co-generation and desalination site is typically unclassified.

Does it cover chlorine dosing as well as fuel gas?

The Ex ib IIC T4 target is a general Zone 1 gas-atmosphere rating rather than a chemical-specific certification; the certificate will state its exact scope once issued, and site HSE teams should confirm applicability to their specific dosing system.

Can one device be used across several sites on the same corridor?

That is the intent behind the MDM and EMM fleet management features, which are designed to let an operator manage a common device profile across multiple sites from one console.
