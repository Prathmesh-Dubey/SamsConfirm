The sites · BOTAŞ

Where an explosion proof mobile phone has to work at BOTAŞ

The Marmara Ereğlisi terminal on the Marmara shore has three storage tanks of 85,000 m³ each and can send out 37 million m³ of gas a day, taking roughly 65 LNG cargoes a year. A fourth tank of 160,000 m³ has been reported as under construction from July 2026, which puts a major civil and mechanical contractor workforce next to a live terminal for several years. Floating units add capacity on the İskenderun gulf and the Gulf of Saros, underground storage at Silivri and Tuz Gölü carries the winter peak, and the transmission network ties them together through compressor and metering stations spread across the country. Most of that is outdoors, spread over long distances, and a long way from a safe-area charging point.

What BOTAŞ work asks of a handset

The area classification decides where a device may go at all. After that, the shape of the network decides how it is used. These are the four conditions that come up when gas transmission work is described.

A network measured in hundreds of kilometres

A pressure reduction station or valve site is visited by a crew that has driven there, and the nearest colleague may be an hour away. Four-constellation GNSS puts a coordinate on the fault report, and the 4,000 mAh cell is meant to cover a full route without a charging stop inside a classified area.

Cargo transfers that do not wait for a shift change

LNG and crude cargoes arrive when the ship does. IP68 sealing copes with spray on a jetty, and the 50 MP camera records a manifold position or a seal number as evidence, so the handover note matches what was actually seen.

Construction beside live plant

The fourth tank at Marmara Ereğlisi means contractors working next to operating equipment. Android Enterprise enrolment lets BOTAŞ’s own console issue, restrict and reclaim handsets as crews rotate, instead of leaving a phone with a subcontractor.

A file that has to be quotable

A Turkish İSG specialist writes the equipment selection into the patlamadan korunma dokümanı. The marking string, gas group and temperature class need to be citable, and the Turkish interface and documentation set are meant to make that straightforward.

Questions · BOTAŞ

Frequently asked questions

What does BOTAŞ ask of a new equipment supplier?

BOTAŞ publishes tender notices and documents through its website and its procurement department, and its purchasing regulation provides for supplier qualification to be recorded electronically. Suppliers are usually asked to show manufacturer or authorised-dealer status. Site-level approval for hazardous-area equipment is a separate conversation with the procurement and HSE teams, and this page does not describe it.

Does every BOTAŞ site need a Zone 1 handset?

No. Zones come from the area classification drawing for each site, not from the operator’s name. Well-ventilated outdoor gas equipment is often Zone 2, with Zone 1 around specific releases. A Zone 1 device can also be used in Zone 2, which is why one handset can serve a route that crosses both.

How do floating terminals differ for equipment?

A floating unit is a ship, so the hazardous areas on board are defined under marine rules rather than only the terminal’s onshore drawings. Ask the vessel manager and the terminal HSE team whether a handset must be accepted by both before it goes aboard.
