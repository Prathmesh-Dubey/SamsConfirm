France · Chemicals and industrial gases

Explosion proof mobile phone for chemicals and industrial gases in France

An explosion proof mobile phone for France's chemical industry has to work on the plateformes chimiques, shared industrial zones where specialty producers like Arkema operate alongside industrial gas suppliers such as Air Liquide, whose hydrogen pipeline networks extend across several of these clusters. This page is for the preventionists, maintenance planners and telecoms teams who decide what a person may carry into a Zone 1 or Zone 21 area on those sites.

Why it fits chemicals and industrial gases

Built for shared chemical platforms with a growing hydrogen layer

A plateforme chimique brings several companies onto one site under shared infrastructure, and the addition of hydrogen pipeline networks on top of conventional chemical process areas adds a gas group consideration that pure specialty chemical production alone would not.

Zone 1/2 gas across chemical process units

Specialty chemical production at Arkema's Jarrie, Pierre-Bénite, Lacq and Carling sites carries Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas classifications, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon and process gas groups typical of that production.

ATEX as the governing EU framework

As an EU member state, France's hazardous-area equipment requirements are governed by the ATEX directives across the chemical sector.

Hydrogen pipeline networks layered onto existing chemical platforms

Air Liquide's hydrogen infrastructure in Normandy and at Fos runs through the same industrial zones as conventional chemical production, meaning equipment on these platforms increasingly needs to cover both gas scopes.

A shared-platform structure spanning multiple companies

Several producers typically share one plateforme chimique's utilities and safety infrastructure while running separate legal entities, so equipment approved for one company's process area does not automatically extend to its neighbour's.

The sites · France

Where the work happens on French chemical platforms

Chemical sites are classified by process, not by region, so two plants a few hundred metres apart can need different equipment. These are the places named most often when French chemical and gas teams talk about handheld devices.

Sites and what runs there

Jarrie, Isère
Arkema's largest hydrogen peroxide unit, with hydrogen produced on site as a feedstock.
Pierre-Bénite, Rhône
Fluorine chemistry on the Vallée de la Chimie, including fluorinated gases and PVDF polymer; a Seveso high-threshold site.
Lacq and Mourenx, Béarn
Thiochemicals on a platform built around the old Lacq gas field, also high-threshold Seveso.
Carling, Moselle
Acrylic monomers and superabsorbent production in the former coal basin; high-threshold Seveso.
Normandy and Fos
Air Liquide hydrogen and industrial gas networks serving neighbouring refiners and chemical producers.

Hazards a handset meets

Hydrogen
Group IIC, with a very wide flammable range and a very low ignition energy, at production, compression and pipeline interfaces.
Sulphur compounds
Flammable, toxic and strongly odorous; a leak is often smelt before it is detected.
Acrylate and solvent vapour
Flammable liquids and vapours around reactors, tank farms and drum filling; the group depends on the substance.
Process powders
Polymer and chemical powders create Zone 21 or 22 dust areas, sometimes next to a gas area in the same building.
Peroxide and corrosive areas
Corrosive and oxidising atmospheres where the housing and seals matter as much as the Ex marking.
How work is organised

Shutdowns, shared perimeters and contractor crews

Planned shutdowns with large contractor crews

A specialty plant stops a unit for maintenance on a published window, and entreprises extérieures arrive in numbers. Each crew works under a plan de prévention agreed with the host operator, so a handset that can be enrolled, restricted and collected at the end of the job suits that process.

Several employers inside one fence

On a plateforme the host operator, its neighbours and their contractors share roads, utilities and an emergency organisation. The same person may cross from one company's process area into another's within a shift, and each side will want to see the equipment documentation.

Rounds on foot between units

Operators walk long routes between reactors, tanks and loading points, with charging points in the control building rather than in the field. A 4,000 mAh battery is specified to cover a shift of calls, photographs and work-order entries.

Procurement stays with each company

Ask each operator's procurement and HSE teams what they require of an equipment supplier before a unit enters site. Expect requests for the certificate and marking string, the instructions in French, and a description of how the device is managed and recovered.

Questions · Chemicals and industrial gases

Explosion proof mobile phone questions for France

Is the EX-04Z1 certified for use on French chemical platforms yet?

No. The device is pre-launch and certification, including ATEX, is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does a hydrogen pipeline network need separate certification from conventional chemical production?

Hydrogen introduces a gas group consideration distinct from typical process chemicals, so equipment intended for both scopes needs a certification basis covering each.

Is ATEX the only certification needed for French chemical operators?

ATEX is the primary EU regulatory framework; IECEx serves as useful supporting international technical evidence.

Does approval with one company on a shared platform cover a neighbouring company?

No, each company on a plateforme chimique typically runs its own separate procurement process even where the site infrastructure is shared.

Which gas group matters most on a hydrogen-heavy site?

Hydrogen sits in group IIC, the most demanding of the three gas groups, so a handset used where hydrogen is present needs an Ex ib IIC marking. The EX-04Z1 target marking is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, which is why the IIC element is named on this page rather than left to the datasheet.

Can one handset cover both a solvent area and a powder area?

That is the purpose of Zone 1 and Zone 21 together. Gas and dust protection sit in the same certification programme, but the zoning for each room still comes from the operator's own explosion protection document, and the marking should be checked against it.

Who decides whether a contractor may bring a phone onto a platform?

The host operator, through its site rules and the plan de prévention for the job. The device being designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 does not replace that decision; it only gives the operator a certificate to review once one exists.
