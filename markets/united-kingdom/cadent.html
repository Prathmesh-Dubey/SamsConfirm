The network · Cadent

An explosion proof mobile phone for the gas network: what is classified

Cadent describes itself as carrying gas to around eleven million homes and businesses across north west England, the West Midlands, the East Midlands, the East of England and north London. Gas reaches its networks from the high-pressure National Transmission System and from a growing number of biomethane plants, and is then stepped down through pressure reduction stages to the mains that run under the street. Most of that is buried pipe and is not a classified area. The classified work is at the points where gas is vented, sampled, odorised, metered or released: above-ground installations, pressure reduction and governor kiosks, odorant handling, and the excavation around a damaged main during an emergency. Zone 2 is common around fixed equipment, and Zone 1 appears wherever gas can be expected in normal operation, such as near a vent or inside a confined compartment. Natural gas is a group IIA gas, so the IIC marking target adds margin rather than being a requirement of the fuel itself. The difficulty is not the gas group but the working pattern, because the person at the kiosk is often alone.

What a gas network does to a handset

Cadent’s engineers work outdoors in every season and are often a long way from a colleague. These are the points that matter for a device they would carry.

Lone working needs a call that always connects

A single engineer at a governor kiosk depends on the phone for their check-in. The EX-04Z1 carries 4G LTE and Wi-Fi, and an enrolled unit can run whichever lone-worker application the operator already uses, because it is a standard Android Enterprise device.

Weather on a roadside excavation

A leak repair happens in rain, mud and standing water. IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H testing are what keep the unit working at the edge of a trench, and the handset is designed to be wiped clean between jobs.

A shift with no safe place to charge

A day of call-outs means moving between sites, with the vehicle as the only charging point. The 4,000 mAh battery is sized for a full shift with GNSS and the camera running, so charging at depot handover is enough.

A position that is exact, not described

A gas escape is reported against a street, but a repair record needs an exact point. Four-constellation GNSS gives a coordinate in streets with poor sky view, and the 50 MP camera records the condition of a fitting before and after work.

Managed alongside the rest of the estate

Android 16 with Android Enterprise and MDM support lets Cadent’s IT team enrol, restrict and update these units next to its ordinary handsets, and lock the device to the job applications it has approved.

Questions · Cadent

Frequently asked questions

Do Cadent’s engineers need a Zone 1 phone for ordinary mains work?

Not for buried pipe in the street. The requirement comes from the area classification at the specific site or leak, which Cadent’s own safety procedures decide. The EX-04Z1 is designed for the sites and call-outs where a classified area is declared.

How would a handset fit with lone-worker monitoring?

The EX-04Z1 is an Android Enterprise device, so a monitoring application the operator already uses can run on it, subject to that application’s own approval. Ask Cadent’s operations and IT teams which service and which version they require.

Is Cadent the only gas distribution network in the UK?

No. Britain has eight regional networks, and Cadent runs four of them. SGN, which serves Scotland and southern England, is another large operator. The wider sector is covered on the explosion proof phone for UK utilities and waste page.

Has Cadent approved this handset?

No. The EX-04Z1 is pre-launch, certification is in progress, and SAMS has no relationship, pilot or supply agreement with Cadent. Vendor and equipment approval is for Cadent’s procurement and HSE teams to define.
