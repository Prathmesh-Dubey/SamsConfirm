UK utilities and waste sites put Zone 1 and Zone 2 work in places few people picture as hazardous: a gas governor on a verge, a sewage digester, a biogas upgrader, a waste bunker. An explosion proof mobile phone for the United Kingdom serves these sites because the people are often alone and the classification changes with the equipment. This page covers gas distribution, water and sludge treatment, anaerobic digestion and energy from waste. The sector · UK utilities and waste

Explosion proof mobile phone for UK utilities, biogas and waste sites

Gas distribution is the most visible part. Cadent runs four of the eight gas networks, and SGN serves Scotland, where it reaches about three-quarters of households, and southern England, where it supplies roughly four million customers, across about 74,000 km of pipe. Both take gas from the National Transmission System and, increasingly, from biomethane plants. Behind that, anaerobic digestion has grown to roughly 750 plants producing biogas, of which a share is upgraded and injected into the grid. Water companies run their own digesters: Thames Water’s Beckton works serves more than three million people and has a 6 MWe combined heat and power installation fuelled by biogas, and its Crossness and Beckton sites have advanced digestion plants. Energy from waste adds a different risk, because the combustible dust at lime and carbon handling points sits next to methane that collects in waste bunkers. Veolia and Viridor are among the large operators. The common thread is a classified area in a place that is otherwise a normal workplace.

What a utility or waste site does to a handset

These are working sites with weather, wet and dirt as well as gas and dust, and often only one or two staff on shift.

Lone working at a remote works

A rural digester or a kiosk on a verge usually has one person at a time. The EX-04Z1 carries 4G LTE and Wi-Fi and, as an Android Enterprise device, can run the lone-worker service a utility already uses.

Wet, dirty and washed down

Treatment works and waste halls are hosed down and get sludge, spray and grit on equipment. IP68 sealing and rugged build are what let a handset be rinsed under a tap at the end of a shift.

Biogas, not just natural gas

Biogas is mostly methane with carbon dioxide and traces of other compounds. The EX-04Z1 is designed to an II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 target, which covers the gas groups of both natural gas and biogas in Zone 1.

Dust and gas on one site

An energy-from-waste plant has lime and carbon dust in one area and methane in another. A device designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 together avoids a second handset for each hazard.

A single managed estate

Android 16 with Android Enterprise and MDM support lets a utility enrol, restrict and update these units next to its ordinary handsets, and lock the camera or applications where a site requires.

Questions · UK utilities and waste

Frequently asked questions

Is natural gas different from biogas for classification?

Both are flammable gas, and zoning is set by the site’s hazardous area assessment under the Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations. Biogas can also contain other compounds, so give the assessment to any supplier when you ask about a handset.

Do water companies and waste operators approve suppliers the same way?

Not necessarily, and we have not verified a standard programme across the sector. Ask each company’s procurement, engineering and HSE teams what a handheld in a classified area has to show.

Is the EX-04Z1 suitable for a bunker with methane?

A waste bunker’s classification depends on ventilation and the operator’s assessment, so the design target is an input to that assessment rather than a substitute for it. The handset is designed for Zone 1, and certification is in progress.

Is the EX-04Z1 available now?

No. It is pre-launch, with IECEx, ATEX and UKEX certification in progress ahead of Q4 2026, and SAMS has no pilot or supply agreement with any UK utility or waste operator.
