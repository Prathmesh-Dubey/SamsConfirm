Explosion proof mobile phone for Harbour Energy in the UK

Harbour Energy operates a spread of North Sea hubs, from the Central North Sea and the Greater Britannia area to the Southern North Sea, the East Irish Sea and Solan west of Shetland, and it reported an average UK production of 155 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day in 2025. This page is for Harbour’s HSE, operations and telecoms teams and for the contractors who work those installations, and it covers what an explosion proof mobile phone has to do on a UK offshore platform. The hubs · Harbour Energy

Explosion proof mobile phone for Harbour’s North Sea hubs: what is classified

Harbour’s own UK pages list the areas it operates: the J-Area (Judy, Jade, Jasmine, Joanne, with Talbot and Dunnottar), the Greater Britannia Area (Britannia, Brodgar and Callanish), the Armada area (with Drake, Fleming, Hawkins, Maria and Seymour), the Catcher area (Burgman, Varadero and Laverda), Johnston and Tolmount in the Southern North Sea, Calder, Dalton and Millom in the East Irish Sea, and Solan west of Shetland. Its regulatory filings group the operated assets into a Production Operations Centre and a Late Life Operations Centre, and the second of those takes in Armada, Everest, Lomond, Solan and the East Irish Sea. Export routes matter for the people who work there: Armada, Everest and Lomond send production through the Forties Pipeline System to the Kerse of Kinneil plant near Grangemouth, while J-Area gas goes by the Central Area Transmission System and its liquids to Teesside through Norpipe. A person on these platforms may therefore be working in a very different installation type, from an unmanned subsea tieback to a late-life platform, in the same trip.

What a mixed fleet of installations does to a handset

Harbour’s portfolio includes both mature platforms and newer subsea developments, so the same handset has to suit more than one kind of site.

Older platforms with limited coverage

Late-life platforms do not always have the network coverage of a newer facility. The EX-04Z1 carries 4G LTE and Wi-Fi, and four-constellation GNSS holds a position across a large deck, so it works with whatever network the installation offers.

Weather offshore and in the Irish Sea

From the central North Sea to Morecambe Bay the common conditions are salt, wind and wash-down. IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H testing are the specifications that tell a reviewer the unit survives a winter on deck.

A twelve-hour shift in the zone

There is nowhere to charge in the process area, so the 4,000 mAh battery is sized to carry a full shift with the camera and GNSS in use, with charging at handover in the accommodation block.

Late-life work needs better records

Mature installations generate a lot of inspection and integrity work, and a 50 MP camera with PDAF gives photographs of corrosion, supports and tags that an onshore engineer can use without asking for another visit.

One policy across assets and contractors

Android 16 with Android Enterprise and MDM support means Harbour’s IT team can enrol and restrict handsets by asset, and contractors on a campaign can be issued units with the same controls instead of an exception for each visit.

Use cases · Harbour Energy

Where the EX-04Z1 goes to work for Harbour.

Operations01

Production rounds on a Central North Sea hub

An operator on a Britannia or J-Area platform records readings, photographs a leaking flange and calls the control room, all inside the zone. One Zone 1 handset replaces the paper round sheet and the radio.

Late life02

Inspection and integrity campaigns

Armada, Everest and Lomond are mature, and inspection crews come offshore for a defined scope. An enrolled handset carries the inspection record, a camera and the permit, and is wiped on departure.

Logistics03

Unmanned and subsea tieback visits

A visit to an unmanned installation is short and expensive. The crew has one device that holds the drawings, the position and the photographs, so a missing detail does not mean a second trip.

Questions · Harbour Energy

Frequently asked questions

Which Harbour installations does this page cover?

It draws on the areas Harbour lists as operated in the UK, from the J-Area and Greater Britannia to Solan and the East Irish Sea. Zoning is set per installation in its safety case, so a handset is specified against the installation, not against the company.

Does Harbour operate Buzzard, Clair or Elgin?

Harbour lists non-operated stakes in Buzzard, Elgin/Franklin and Clair, so on those assets another company is the duty holder and sets the device rules. The Clair and Buzzard portfolio is covered on the explosion proof phone for Adura page.

What registration does Harbour ask of equipment suppliers?

We have not found a published supplier programme we could verify, so we do not name one. Ask Harbour’s procurement and HSE teams what they need for a handheld in a classified area, including certificates, a datasheet and any installation-specific approval.

Has Harbour chosen the EX-04Z1?

No. The EX-04Z1 is pre-launch, certification is in progress, and SAMS has no pilot, order or relationship with Harbour Energy. The page describes what a Harbour team would ask and what the device is designed for.
