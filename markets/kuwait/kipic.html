The site · Al-Zour

Al-Zour: a refinery, an LNG terminal and a petrochemical site on one coast

Kuwait Integrated Petroleum Industries Company was set up by Kuwait Petroleum Corporation in 2016 to build and run Al-Zour, about 90 kilometres south of Kuwait City. The refinery is rated at 615,000 barrels a day and was reported fully commissioned in early 2024. The LNG import terminal, built by a consortium led by Hyundai, took its first cargo in 2021 and now has eight 225,000 cubic metre tanks and two marine jetties. A petrochemical complex of gasoline, aromatics and olefins units is still planned, with trade press describing it as awaiting a final investment decision. KIPIC's merger into KNPC was reported complete on 1 July 2026. Because Al-Zour is new, its classification drawings and equipment records are more current than the old plants, and it is a site where contractor commissioning crews are still a familiar presence.

Methane and hydrogen on the same shift

An LNG round is mostly group IIA gas, while the refinery's hydroprocessing is group IIC. A design target of Zone 1, gas group IIC and temperature class T4 covers the stricter case, so a single marking string can be checked against both sets of drawings.

A very long site

Between the refinery, the tank farm and the jetties an operator may cover several kilometres on a round with no charging point inside a classified area. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified for a shift, and four-constellation GNSS gives a position that a site plan can be tied to.

Commissioning and construction crews

Where units are still being handed over or built, contractor crews rotate quickly. Managed enrolment lets the owner issue, restrict and recover devices for each contract instead of tracking loose handsets.

Salt air on the waterfront

The jetties and the sea-facing units sit in salt and humidity on top of Gulf heat. IP68 sealing and MIL-STD-810H construction are the figures a marine terminal's engineers will ask about, with certification still in progress.

Questions · KIPIC

Frequently asked questions

Is KIPIC still a separate company?

Press reports based on the Oil Minister's statement say the merger into KNPC was completed on 1 July 2026. We have not seen KIPIC's or KNPC's own filing, so ask the operator which entity name to use.

Is the EX-04Z1 approved for Al-Zour?

No. It is pre-launch, with IECEx, ATEX and NEC 500 certification in progress, and no certificate has been issued. Nothing enters a classified area before the certificate covering it exists.

Does an LNG terminal need group IIC equipment?

Methane is group IIA, so group IIC is not strictly needed there. The refinery's hydrogen units are why the EX-04Z1 is designed to IIC, and IIC equipment is suitable for IIA and IIB atmospheres.

Can we evaluate a unit before the certificate?

Yes, in a safe area. Evaluation units are for handling, battery and enrolment trials only.
